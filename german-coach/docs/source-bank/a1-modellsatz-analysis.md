## High-level exam description

The source states that Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 assumes very simple language knowledge and corresponds to A1 on the CEFR scale.

The learner should be able to:

- understand short everyday questions, instructions, messages, answering-machine announcements, public announcements, and short conversations
- extract relevant information from short written messages, public notices, and classified ads
- state and understand numbers, quantities, times, and prices
- complete forms with simple personal information
- write short personal messages
- introduce themselves and answer simple personal questions
- formulate and respond to common everyday requests

## Exam sections observed

The A1 model test includes:

```text
Hören
Lesen
Schreiben
Sprechen
```

The written individual exam includes Hören, Lesen, and Schreiben. The oral part is a group exam.

## Hören

Observed structure:

- approximately 20 minutes
- three parts
- short conversations and announcements
- tasks are answered on the answer sheet

Observed task families:

1. Multiple-choice listening from short texts
2. True/false listening for public announcements or short spoken information
3. Multiple-choice listening for short spoken details

Observed app implications:

- A1 listening engine should start with short, simple audio.
- It must include numbers, prices, time, rooms, routes, food, transport, and daily-life details.
- Some tasks should be heard twice; some once, depending on modeled task family.
- Transcript should be hidden until after the learner answers.

Original app task examples to generate:

- Listen to a short appointment message and select the correct time.
- Listen to a shop conversation and select the correct price.
- Listen to a station announcement and mark a statement true/false.
- Listen to a phone message and identify the meeting place.

## Lesen

Observed structure:

- Lesen and Schreiben together are shown as approximately 45 minutes in the candidate material.
- Lesen itself is described as approximately 25 minutes.
- The reading test has three parts.
- Learners read short letters, ads, and similar short texts.

Observed task families:

1. Short message / email true-false tasks
2. Information lookup from notices, ads, websites, or public information
3. Matching practical need to correct short text/source

Observed app implications:

- A1 reading should focus on short, realistic texts.
- Texts should include emails, invitations, signs, opening hours, ads, weather/travel/restaurant/hotel notices.
- Learner should learn keyword scanning, not full translation.

Original app task examples to generate:

- Read a short invitation and answer whether the party is inside or outside.
- Read two short notices and choose where to find specific information.
- Read an appointment message and select the correct statement.

## Schreiben

Observed structure:

- Writing is described with two parts.
- Learners complete a form and write a short text.
- Dictionaries are not allowed.

Observed task families:

1. Form filling with personal or event information
2. Short personal written message

Observed app implications:

- A1 writing engine should strongly train form fields and short messages.
- It should review task completion, simple word order, correct personal details, and basic sentence patterns.
- The app should force rewrite after correction.

Original app task examples to generate:

- Fill a registration form for a German course.
- Write a short message saying you are sick and cannot come.
- Write a short invitation reply.
- Write a short message asking for homework.

## Sprechen

Observed source content includes oral exam notes and speaking assessment sections.

Required app direction:

- A1 speaking should include self introduction.
- It should include simple personal questions.
- It should include everyday requests and responses.
- It should train spelling, numbers, address, and phone number.

Original app task examples to generate:

- Introduce yourself: name, country, city, language, profession.
- Spell your name.
- Give your phone number.
- Ask a partner for a phone number or address.
- Ask for water, help, repetition, or an appointment.

## Required grammar support for A1

The model test demand supports these grammar priorities:

- sein / haben
- present tense verbs
- verb position 2
- W-questions
- yes/no questions
- articles
- personal pronouns
- possessives
- nicht / kein
- accusative basics
- modal verbs
- time expressions
- numbers and dates
- noun capitalization
