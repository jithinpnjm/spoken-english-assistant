Lesson 16
Numbers Part 3: Above 100 — Zahlen Teil 3: Über 100
Knowing numbers above 100 is crucial for stating years, paying rent, and giving exact addresses. German strings numbers together into massive single words, which looks terrifying at first. However, the pattern is completely logical. Once you break it down into blocks, reading large numbers becomes a simple puzzle.
The Rules
To say numbers above 100, say the hundreds first, then add the tens and units exactly as you learned them (e.g., 121 = hundert einundzwanzig).
Numbers below one million are technically written as one single, long word (e.g., dreihundertfünfundvierzig for 345).
For years between 1100 and 1999, Germans say 'hundreds' instead of thousands. 1985 is 'neunzehnhundertfünfundachtzig' (19-hundred-85).
Years 2000 and above are spoken normally using 'tausend' (zweitausend, zweitausendfünf).
Formulas
Standard: [Hundreds] + hundert + [Tens/Units] → zwei + hundert + zehn = zweihundertzehn
Years (1100–1999): [First two digits] + hundert + [Last two digits] → neunzehn + hundert + neunzig = neunzehnhundertneunzig

Model Sentences
Das Auto kostet viertausendzweihundert Euro.
The car costs four thousand two hundred euros.
Das Auto = The car | kostet = costs | viertausend = four thousand | zweihundert = two hundred | Euro = euros → Just stack the blocks: thousands + hundreds + smaller number.
Ich bin im Jahr neunzehnhundertneunundneunzig geboren.
I was born in the year 1999.
Ich = I | bin = am | im Jahr = in the year | neunzehn = nineteen | hundert = hundred | neunundneunzig = ninety-nine | geboren = born → Years before 2000 use the 'hundreds' format.
Meine Miete ist siebenhundertfünfzig Euro.
My rent is 750 euros.
Meine Miete = My rent | ist = is | siebenhundert = seven hundred | fünfzig = fifty | Euro = euros → 750 is built as: seven hundred + fifty.
Zweitausendfünfzehn war ein gutes Jahr.
2015 was a good year.
Zweitausend = Two thousand | fünfzehn = fifteen | war = was | ein gutes Jahr = a good year → Years after 2000 use 'zweitausend' + the remaining number.

Common Mistakes
✗ eintausendneunhundertneunzig (for the year 1990)
✓ neunzehnhundertneunzig
While 'eintausendneunhundert...' is mathematically correct for the number 1,990, Germans almost never use it for historical dates before 2000. You must use the 'hundreds' format for years.
✗ zwei hundert und fünfzig
✓ zweihundertfünfzig
English speakers naturally add 'and' (und) between the hundreds and the tens. In German, there is no 'und' there. 'und' is ONLY used between single units and tens (fünf-und-fünfzig).
Cultural Note
When writing text messages or emails, Germans use digits for almost any number above 12 because writing out 'dreitausendvierhundertfünfundzwanzig' is visually exhausting. However, you must be able to read these numbers aloud accurately when dealing with bank clerks, real estate agents, or government offices.
Exam Relevance — Hören Teil 3: You will often hear radio announcements or news clips with large numbers, such as a traffic jam length (z.B. 15 Kilometer), a year, or a ticket price. Misinterpreting these numbers costs points.
Lesson Goal: Correctly pronounce years, prices, and large numbers up to 10,000, understanding the structural differences from English.
Lesson 17
Adjectives and Opposites — Adjektive und Gegenteile
Adjectives breathe life into your sentences, allowing you to describe feelings, sizes, and quality. Here is a massive relief for A1 learners: when an adjective comes AFTER the verb 'sein' (to be) to describe the subject, it takes NO complex endings. It stays exactly as it appears in the dictionary. Learning these adjectives in pairs of opposites is the fastest way to double your vocabulary.
The Rules
When an adjective comes after a form of 'sein' (is, am, are), it describes the subject and takes NO extra ending.
It does not matter if the noun is masculine, feminine, neuter, or plural — the adjective form stays the same (Das Auto ist klein. Die Autos sind klein).
Use 'sehr' (very) before the adjective to make it stronger in a positive or neutral way.
Use 'zu' (too) before the adjective to say that something is excessive or causes a problem.
Formulas
Subject + sein + Adjective → Der Kaffee ist heiß.
Subject + sein + (sehr / zu) + Adjective → Der Kaffee ist zu heiß.
Vocabulary
GermanEnglishExample
großbig / tallDas Haus ist groß. — The house is big.
kleinsmall / shortDas Kind ist klein. — The child is small.
heißhotDer Tee ist zu heiß. — The tea is too hot.
kaltcoldDas Wasser ist sehr kalt. — The water is very cold.
teuerexpensiveDas Auto ist teuer. — The car is expensive.
billigcheapDas Brot ist billig. — The bread is cheap.
neunewMein Handy ist neu. — My cellphone is new.
altoldDie Frau ist alt. — The woman is old.
sehrveryDas Essen ist sehr gut. — The food is very good.
zutoo (excessive)Die Wohnung ist zu klein. — The apartment is too small.
Model Sentences
Mein Haus ist neu, aber es ist zu klein.
My house is new, but it is too small.
Mein Haus = My house | ist = is | neu = new | aber = but | es = it | ist = is | zu = too | klein = small → No ending on the adjective — it follows 'ist'.
Der Kaffee ist sehr heiß.
The coffee is very hot.
Der Kaffee = The coffee | ist = is | sehr = very | heiß = hot → 'sehr' adds strength positively or neutrally.
Sind die Tomaten billig oder teuer?
Are the tomatoes cheap or expensive?
Sind = Are | die Tomaten = the tomatoes (plural) | billig = cheap | oder = or | teuer = expensive → Even with a plural noun, the adjectives 'billig' and 'teuer' take no extra ending.
Das Auto ist alt und sehr teuer.
The car is old and very expensive.
Das Auto = The car | ist = is | alt = old | und = and | sehr = very | teuer = expensive → Two adjectives after 'ist', both stay unchanged.

Common Mistakes
✗ Die Autos sind kleines.
✓ Die Autos sind klein.
Learners often try to make adjectives plural if the noun is plural. When the adjective comes after 'sein', it NEVER takes an ending. Only the verb (sind) changes for plurals.
✗ Der Kaffee ist zu gut.
✓ Der Kaffee ist sehr gut.
In English slang, 'too good' can mean 'amazing'. In German, 'zu' is strictly negative/excessive. 'Zu gut' implies it's suspiciously good or causing a problem. Use 'sehr' for positive enhancement.
Cultural Note
Germans are known for direct communication. If you ask a German friend if your new jacket looks good, and they don't like it, they might just say 'Nein, sie ist nicht schön' (No, it's not beautiful). While English speakers might cushion the blow, Germans view direct adjectives as honest rather than rude.
Exam Relevance — Schreiben Teil 1 & Sprechen Teil 2: Describing things simply is a core A1 task. You must be able to state whether your apartment is big, your food is hot, or a ticket is expensive without getting tangled in adjective endings.
Lesson Goal: Describe people, objects, and situations using basic adjectives and intensifiers without applying unnecessary grammatical endings.
Lesson 18
How to Introduce Someone Else — Jemanden vorstellen
Once you can introduce yourself, the next step is introducing colleagues, friends, or family to each other. This requires a mental shift from 'I' (ich) to 'he/she/they' (er/sie), ensuring you change the verb endings to match. Mastering this allows you to act as a bridge in social and professional settings.
The Rules
To point out or introduce someone, use the fixed phrase 'Das ist...' (This is...) for one person, or 'Das sind...' (These are...) for multiple people.
After the introduction, switch to the 3rd person pronouns 'er' (he) or 'sie' (she/they) to provide more details.
Remember to add a '-t' ending to the verb when talking about one person (er kommt, sie wohnt).
If you introduce two people, use 'sie' (they) and add '-en' to the verb (sie kommen).
Formulas
Singular Introduction: Das ist + [Name]. Er/Sie + verb(-t) + aus/in...
Plural Introduction: Das sind + [Name 1] und [Name 2]. Sie + verb(-en) + aus/in...
Vocabulary
GermanEnglishExample
vorstellento introduceIch möchte Tom vorstellen. — I would like to introduce Tom.
der Freundmale friend / boyfriendDas ist mein Freund. — This is my friend.
die Freundinfemale friend / girlfriendDas ist meine Freundin Anna. — This is my friend Anna.
der Kollegemale colleagueEr ist mein Kollege. — He is my colleague.
die Kolleginfemale colleagueSie ist eine Kollegin. — She is a colleague.
das istthis isDas ist Herr Weber. — This is Mr. Weber.
das sindthese areDas sind Maria und Tom. — These are Maria and Tom.
auchalso / tooEr kommt auch aus Berlin. — He also comes from Berlin.
zusammentogetherSie arbeiten zusammen. — They work together.
kennento know (a person)Kennst du Tom? — Do you know Tom?
Model Sentences
Das ist mein Kollege, Tom. Er arbeitet hier.
This is my colleague, Tom. He works here.
Das ist = This is | mein Kollege = my colleague | Tom = name | Er = He | arbeitet = works (verb ends in '-t' for 'er') | hier = here → When talking about one man, use 'er' + verb ending '-t'.
Das ist Frau Müller. Sie kommt aus Österreich.
This is Mrs. Müller. She comes from Austria.
Das ist = This is | Frau Müller = Mrs. Müller | Sie = She | kommt = comes (verb ends in '-t' for 'sie') | aus Österreich = from Austria → Same '-t' ending for 'sie' (she).
Das sind Laura und Paul. Sie wohnen in München.
These are Laura and Paul. They live in Munich.
Das sind = These are (plural) | Laura und Paul = two people | Sie = They | wohnen = live (verb ends in '-en' for 'they') | in München = in Munich → For two or more people, use 'sie' (they) + verb ending '-en'.
Kennst du Max? Er ist mein Freund.
Do you know Max? He is my friend.
Kennst = Know | du = you | Max = name | Er = He | ist = is | mein Freund = my friend → Simple introduction using 'er ist'.
Exercises

Answer: Das sind

Answer: Das ist

Conjugate the verb for the 3rd person.

Answer: kommt

Answer: arbeiten
Common Mistakes
✗ Das sind meine Kollegin.
✓ Das ist meine Kollegin.
Because 'Kollegin' (female colleague) ends in 'in', learners sometimes confuse it for a plural. It is singular, so it must be 'Das ist'.
✗ Das ist Herr Wagner. Sie kommt aus Berlin.
✓ Das ist Herr Wagner. Er kommt aus Berlin.
When stressed, learners mix up 'er' (he) and 'sie' (she). Always align the pronoun with the gender of the person you just introduced.
Cultural Note
In German, the words 'Freund' and 'Freundin' are notoriously ambiguous. Saying 'Er ist mein Freund' usually implies he is your romantic boyfriend. To introduce a platonic friend, Germans usually say 'Er ist ein Freund von mir' (He is a friend of mine) to avoid any awkward misunderstandings.
Exam Relevance — Sprechen Teil 1 & 2: While you primarily introduce yourself, the examiner might ask you questions about your partner, family members, or friends, forcing you to use 'er' or 'sie' and the correct 3rd person verb endings.
Lesson Goal: Introduce individuals and groups correctly using 'Das ist' / 'Das sind' and smoothly transition to 3rd person pronouns and verb endings to provide details.
Lesson 19
Definite Articles: der, die, das — Bestimmte Artikel
In English, there is only one word for 'the'. In German, every single noun is assigned a grammatical gender: masculine, feminine, or neuter. The article reveals this gender. Learning a German noun without its article is like learning only half the word, because every grammar rule you learn from this point forward will depend on whether a word is der, die, or das.
The Rules
Masculine nouns use the article 'der'. Feminine nouns use 'die'. Neuter nouns use 'das'.
Grammatical gender often ignores logic. For example, 'das Mädchen' (the girl) is neuter, and 'der Tisch' (the table) is masculine.
In the plural (more than one), the definite article is ALWAYS 'die', regardless of what gender the word had in the singular.
You must memorize the article at the exact same time you memorize the noun. Do not just learn 'Hund'. Learn 'der Hund'.
Formulas
Singular: der [Masculine], die [Feminine], das [Neuter]
Plural: die [All Genders]

Model Sentences
Der Tisch ist sehr groß und der Stuhl ist neu.
The table is very big and the chair is new.
Der Tisch = The table (masculine, use 'der') | ist = is | sehr groß = very big | und = and | der Stuhl = the chair (also masculine) | ist = is | neu = new → Both are masculine, so both use 'der'.
Die Frau kauft die Lampe.
The woman buys the lamp.
Die Frau = The woman (feminine, use 'die') | kauft = buys | die Lampe = the lamp (also feminine, use 'die') → Two feminine words, both use 'die'.
Das Buch ist gut. Die Bücher sind sehr gut.
The book is good. The books are very good.
Das Buch = The book (neuter, use 'das') | ist = is | gut = good | Die Bücher = The books (plural always uses 'die') | sind = are | sehr gut = very good → Once plural, always 'die'.
Das Mädchen heißt Anna.
The girl is named Anna.
Das Mädchen = The girl (neuter — words ending in '-chen' are always neuter) | heißt = is named | Anna = name → Even though a girl is female, the word itself is grammatically neuter.

Answer: die

Fill in the correct plural article.

Common Mistakes
✗ Die Mädchen spielt.
✓ Das Mädchen spielt.
Because a girl is female, learners assume the article is 'die'. However, words ending in '-chen' are always neuter in German. 'Mädchen' ends in '-chen', so it uses 'das'. Just memorize: das Mädchen.
✗ Der Bücher sind hier.
✓ Die Bücher sind hier.
'Buch' is 'das Buch'. But once it becomes plural (Bücher), you abandon the original article and MUST use the universal plural article 'die'.
Cultural Note
There is no fast trick to mastering articles. German toddlers learn them naturally through thousands of hours of listening. As an adult learner, you will make article mistakes for years. Don't let it stop you from speaking! Germans are used to foreigners mixing up 'der', 'die', and 'das', and they will still understand you perfectly.
Exam Relevance — Lesen, Schreiben, Hören, Sprechen: Articles are the bedrock of German grammar. If you don't know the article, you cannot form many sentence structures properly, which will cause massive point deductions in the Writing exam.
Lesson Goal: Understand the concept of grammatical gender and memorize the definite articles 'der, die, das' along with their base nouns.
Lesson 20
Indefinite Articles (ein, eine) — Unbestimmte Artikel
When you go to a bakery, you rarely ask for 'THE bread'; you usually ask for 'A bread'. For this, you need indefinite articles. Fortunately, if you have already memorized whether a word is 'der', 'die', or 'das', learning the indefinite articles is incredibly simple because masculine and neuter nouns share the exact same word.
The Rules
If the noun is masculine ('der') or neuter ('das'), the indefinite article is 'ein' (a/an).
If the noun is feminine ('die'), the indefinite article is 'eine' (a/an).
There is NO indefinite article in the plural! Just like in English, you cannot say 'a books'. You simply drop the article entirely.
This rule applies when the noun is the subject of the sentence, or when it comes after the verb 'sein' (to be).
Formulas
der (Masculine) → ein
das (Neuter) → ein
die (Feminine) → eine
Plural → [no article]

Model Sentences
Das ist ein Tisch und das ist eine Lampe.
That is a table and that is a lamp.
Das ist = That is | ein Tisch = a table (masculine → ein) | und = and | das ist = that is | eine Lampe = a lamp (feminine → eine) → Masculine uses 'ein', feminine uses 'eine'.
Ist das ein Auto? Nein, das ist ein Fahrrad.
Is that a car? No, that is a bicycle.
Ist das = Is that | ein Auto = a car (neuter → ein) | Nein = No | das ist = that is | ein Fahrrad = a bicycle (neuter → ein) → Both car and bicycle are neuter, both use 'ein'.
Das ist eine Banane und das sind Äpfel.
That is a banana and those are apples.
Das ist = That is | eine Banane = a banana (feminine → eine) | und = and | das sind = those are | Äpfel = apples (plural → no article) → Plural nouns drop the article entirely.
Hier ist ein Hund.
Here is a dog.
Hier = Here | ist = is | ein Hund = a dog (masculine → ein) → Masculine noun, so use 'ein'.

Common Mistakes
✗ Das sind eine Äpfel.
✓ Das sind Äpfel.
Learners often try to put 'eine' in front of plural nouns because 'eine' ends in 'e' like many plural words. Just like in English, you cannot say 'a apples'. Plurals take no indefinite article.
✗ Das ist eine Mädchen.
✓ Das ist ein Mädchen.
Even though a girl is biologically female, the word 'das Mädchen' is grammatically neuter because it ends in '-chen'. Therefore, it takes 'ein', not 'eine'. Just memorize: das Mädchen = ein Mädchen.
Cultural Note
When speaking quickly, Germans often swallow the end of the indefinite article. You might hear someone say 'Das is 'n Auto' instead of 'ein Auto', or 'Das is 'ne Lampe' instead of 'eine Lampe'. Do not write this in your exam, but expect to hear it on the street.
Exam Relevance — Sprechen Teil 2: Describing what you see on the prompt cards usually starts with identifying the object: 'Auf dem Bild sehe ich ein Auto' (On the picture I see a car). Misgendering objects here is a classic beginner mistake.
Lesson Goal: Correctly use 'ein' and 'eine' to identify singular masculine, feminine, and neuter objects, and correctly omit the article for plurals.
