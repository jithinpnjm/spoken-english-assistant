💡 "-schmerzen" = ache / pain — just add body part! Kopf + schmerzen = Kopfschmerzen (headache)
Bauch + schmerzen = Bauchschmerzen (stomach ache)
Zahn + schmerzen = Zahnschmerzen (toothache)
Ohr + schmerzen = Ohrschmerzen (earache)
All are plural in German — always "Schmerzen", not "Schmerz"!
💡 "weh tun" vs "Schmerzen haben" Both mean "to hurt" but used differently:
Mein Kopf tut weh. = My head hurts. (something hurts me)
Ich habe Kopfschmerzen. = I have a headache. (I experience pain)

📌 German Letter Punctuation Rule After the opening greeting, start the next line with a lowercase letter:
Sehr geehrter Herr Müller,
ich schreibe Ihnen wegen …
(The "ich" is lowercase even though it starts a new line — this is correct German!)
📌 Formal Letter Layout (Formeller Brief) Absender = sender (your name + address, top right)
Empfänger = recipient (name + address, left)

📌 The Anmeldung — Why it matters When you move to Germany, you must register your address at the local Einwohnermeldeamt (residents' registration office) within 2 weeks. This is called the Anmeldung. You'll need this for almost everything: bank account, tax number, renting an apartment.

💡 "Guten Rutsch!" — Why "good slide"? Rutsch literally = "slide." The expression comes from the Yiddish rosch (head/beginning). So "Guten Rutsch ins neue Jahr" originally meant "a good beginning of the new year." Today it just means "Happy New Year's Eve!"
→ Say it on 31 December (Silvester), not on 1 January.
