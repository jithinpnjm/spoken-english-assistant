Meetings vw02

Meetings are where careers are made or stalled. Knowing the vocabulary helps you contribute, lead, and follow up confidently.

Scheduling a Meeting

Scheduling Phrases
  • "I'd like to schedule a call to discuss the project timeline."
  • "Are you free on Thursday afternoon?"
  • "Would Tuesday at 2pm work for you?"
  • "I'll send a calendar invite now."
  • "Could we push the meeting back by 30 minutes?"
  • "I'm afraid I have a conflict at that time — could we reschedule?"
  • "Let me check my diary and get back to you."

Running a Meeting

PhrasePurpose
"Let's get started."opening the meeting
"Today's agenda covers three points."outlining the structure
"I'll hand over to [name] to present the figures."passing the floor to someone
"Can we come back to that point later?"parking an issue to stay on track
"Let's take a five-minute break."calling a break
"We're running over time — let's wrap up."closing when overrunning
"To summarise what we've agreed…"recapping decisions
"I'll send the minutes afterwards."promising written notes

Agreeing & Disagreeing Professionally

Agreeing
  • "That's a great point."
  • "I'm fully on board with that."
  • "That aligns with my thinking exactly."
  • "Absolutely — let's go with that approach."
  • "I think that's the right way to go."
Disagreeing Diplomatically
  • "I take your point, but I have some concerns."
  • "Could we explore an alternative approach?"
  • "I'm not entirely convinced — here's why."
  • "That might work, but what about…?"
  • "With respect, I see it slightly differently."

Action Items & Follow-Up

After the Meeting
  • action item — a task assigned to a specific person with a deadline
  • minutes — written record of what was discussed and decided
  • follow up — to check on progress after the meeting
  • circulate — to share a document with all participants
  • "I'll circulate the minutes by end of day."
  • "Your action item is to send the proposal by Friday."
  • "Can we schedule a follow-up call next week?"
Memory Trick Minutes in a meeting context has nothing to do with time — it refers to the written notes. The name comes from the Latin minuta scriptura meaning "small writing."

First Contact / Formal

  • "I was referred to you by [name]."
  • "I am reaching out to discuss a potential opportunity."
  • "I came across your profile on LinkedIn and…"

Subject Lines

Effective Subject Line Patterns
  • Action required: "Action Required: Please Review Contract by Thursday"
  • For information: "FYI: Updated Office Policy on Remote Working"
  • Follow-up: "Following Up: Design Proposal — [Your Name]"
  • Request: "Request for Budget Approval — Q3 Marketing Campaign"
  • Meeting: "Meeting Invitation: Quarterly Review — 14 March, 2pm"
  • Introduction: "Introduction: [Your Name], New Account Manager"
Common Email Mistakes to Avoid
  • Writing a blank subject line — always include one
  • "Dear Sir/Madam" when you know the name — use their name
  • "Yours faithfully" when you used the person's name — use "Yours sincerely"
  • Overusing "ASAP" — try "by end of day" or "by [specific date]" instead
  • Forgetting the attachment you mentioned in the email

Job Interviews vw05

Interviewers want to hear confident, structured, and honest answers. Learning these phrases helps you sound professional even when nervous.

Common Interview Questions & Strong Answers

QuestionStrong Answer Starter
"Tell me about yourself.""I have [X] years of experience in [field]. Most recently, I was [role] at [company], where I…"
"Why do you want this role?""I'm particularly drawn to this role because… and I believe my background in… makes me a strong fit."
"What are your strengths?""One of my key strengths is [X]. For example, in my previous role I…"
"What is your greatest weakness?""I used to struggle with [X], but I've actively worked on this by…"
"Give me an example of a challenge you overcame.""In my previous role, we faced [situation]. I [action I took], which resulted in [positive outcome]."
"Where do you see yourself in five years?""I'd like to grow into a more senior position where I can… and I see this company as the ideal place to develop those skills."
"Why did you leave your last job?""I'm looking for a new challenge / I wanted to develop my skills in [area] / The company was restructuring."
"Do you have any questions for us?""Yes — what does success look like in this role after six months?"

Talking About Experience

STAR Method — Answer Structure
  • S — Situation: "At my previous company, we had a problem with…"
  • T — Task: "My responsibility was to…"
  • A — Action: "I decided to… / I led a team to… / I introduced a new system…"
  • R — Result: "As a result, we reduced costs by 20% / the project was delivered on time / customer complaints fell by 30%."

Always try to quantify your results — numbers make your answers memorable and credible.

Strengths & Weaknesses Vocabulary

Positive Strengths to Mention
  • analytical thinking
  • attention to detail
  • strong communication skills
  • ability to work under pressure
  • team player / collaborative
  • proactive / self-motivated
  • adaptable to change
  • problem-solving ability
Weaknesses (with improvement)
  • "I used to take on too much at once — I now prioritise using a task management system."
  • "I was sometimes too detail-focused, slowing projects down — I now set time limits per task."
  • "Public speaking made me nervous — I joined a Toastmasters group to build confidence."
  • "I found it hard to delegate — I've been consciously trusting my team more."
Questions to Ask at the End
  • "What are the biggest challenges someone in this role typically faces?"
  • "How would you describe the team culture here?"
  • "What opportunities are there for professional development?"
  • "What does the onboarding process look like?"
  • "What are the next steps in your selection process?"

PhraseWhen to Use

  • "Make sure you save a copy before making any changes."
  • "Once you've done that, send it to me for review."
  • "If you run into any issues, just give me a shout."
  • "The key thing to remember is…"

  • "Can I check you're happy with what needs to be done?"

Giving & Receiving Feedback

Giving Positive FeedbackGiving Constructive Feedback
  • "This is really well done."
  • "You handled that brilliantly."
  • "Great work on the presentation."
  • "I really liked how you structured this."
  • "One thing to think about for next time…"
  • "It would be even stronger if you…"

The Feedback Sandwich A classic technique: say something positive → give your suggestion for improvement → end with something positive again. Example: "Really clear writing — I'd suggest adding a summary at the end — overall very professional work."

Transition Words in Reports

  • In addition to this, …
  • It should also be noted that …
  • A further consideration is …
Contrasting

  • Despite this, … / In spite of …
  • On the other hand, …
  • Whilst this is true, it should be noted…
Cause & Effect
  • As a result, … / Consequently, …
  • This led to … / This resulted in …
  • Due to … / Owing to …
  • Therefore, … / Thus, …
Concluding
  • In conclusion, … / To summarise, …
  • On balance, … / Overall, …
  • It is recommended that …
  • Based on the above findings, …

Hedging Language

Hedging softens claims — it shows you are being careful rather than overstating facts. This is highly valued in formal writing.

Direct (avoid in reports)Hedged (preferred)
"This will solve the problem.""This is likely to help address the issue."
"The product failed because of poor quality.""The decline may be partly attributable to quality concerns."
"Customers don't like the new design.""Customer feedback suggests some dissatisfaction with the new design."
"This proves that sales will increase.""The data indicates a potential improvement in sales."
"We must change this immediately.""It would be advisable to review this as a priority."
Report Structure Template
  1. Executive Summary — one paragraph: purpose, key findings, main recommendation
  2. Introduction — background and scope of the report
  3. Findings — what the research/data shows
  4. Analysis — interpretation of the findings
  5. Recommendations — what should happen next
  6. Conclusion — brief summary, re-state the main recommendation
