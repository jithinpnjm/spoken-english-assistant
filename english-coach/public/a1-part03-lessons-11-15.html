Lesson 11

Pronouns Overview — Pronomen

Personal pronouns replace nouns so you don't have to repeat them. German has nine personal pronouns across three persons and three numbers. Formal "Sie" is a critical distinction.

Grammar Rules
1. German has singular and plural forms for all three persons: ich/wir (1st), du/ihr (2nd informal), er–sie–es/sie (3rd).
2. Formal "Sie" (capitalised) uses the same verb form as 3rd person plural "sie". The only difference is the capital S and meaning.
3. "Sie" (she, 3rd person singular) vs "sie" (they, 3rd person plural) vs "Sie" (formal you) — all look similar but context and capitalisation distinguish them.
4. "Es" covers neuter nouns. In German, grammatical gender determines which pronoun to use, not natural gender: Das Mädchen → es (neuter, even though a girl).
Formula
Subject pronouns: ich / du / er / sie / es / wir / ihr / sie / Sie
Replacing a noun: Der Mann → er; Die Frau → sie; Das Kind → es; Die Kinder → sie

Lesson 12

Verb Conjugation: haben & sein — haben und sein

"haben" (to have) and "sein" (to be) are the two most important verbs in German. They are irregular and must be memorised. They also serve as auxiliary verbs for past tense.

Grammar Rules
1. "sein" (to be) is highly irregular: ich bin, du bist, er ist, wir sind, ihr seid, sie/Sie sind. There is no predictable pattern — memorise all forms.
2. "haben" (to have) is mostly regular but has irregularities in du and er/sie/es: ich habe, du hast, er hat — note the missing -b- in du hast/er hat.
3. "sein" is used for identity, origin, states, and professions: Ich bin Lehrer. / Er ist müde. / Wir sind aus Deutschland.
4. "haben" is used for possession: Ich habe ein Auto. It is also the auxiliary for most past tense (Perfekt) sentences: Ich habe gegessen.
Formula
sein: ich bin / du bist / er–sie–es ist / wir sind / ihr seid / sie–Sie sind
haben: ich habe / du hast / er–sie–es hat / wir haben / ihr habt / sie–Sie haben
Identity: Ich bin [profession/adjective/origin] → Ich bin Arzt. / Ich bin müde. / Ich bin aus Polen.
Possession: Ich habe [article + noun] → Ich habe eine Katze. / Er hat kein Auto.

Lesson 13

Regular vs Irregular Verbs — Regelmäßige vs unregelmäßige Verben

German verbs are either regular (predictable endings, no stem change) or irregular (stem changes in du/er/sie/es forms). Understanding this distinction will save you from many errors.

Grammar Rules
1. Regular (weak) verbs: the stem stays the same throughout; only the ending changes according to person/number. Example: lernen → lerne, lernst, lernt, lernen, lernt, lernen.
2. Irregular (strong) verbs: the stem vowel changes in the 2nd (du) and 3rd (er/sie/es) person singular. Example: lesen (e→ie) → ich lese, du liest, er liest. Example: sprechen (e→i) → du sprichst, er spricht.
3. Common stem changes: e→i (sprechen, helfen, essen), e→ie (lesen, sehen, stehlen), a→ä (fahren, schlafen, laufen).
4. The infinitive always ends in -(e)n. To find the stem, remove the -en: lernen → lern-, sprechen → sprech-.
Formula
Regular: [stem] + [ending] → lern + st = lernst (du form)
Irregular e→i: du [stem with i] + st → du sprichst, du isst, du hilfst
Irregular e→ie: du [stem with ie] + st → du liest, du siehst
Irregular a→ä: du [stem with ä] + st → du fährst, du schläfst

Lesson 14

Regular Verb Conjugation — Regelmäßige Verben konjugieren

Master the six personal endings for regular verbs and you can conjugate thousands of German verbs. The endings follow a consistent pattern every time.

Grammar Rules
1. To conjugate a regular verb: remove the -en infinitive ending to get the stem, then add the personal ending.
2. Standard endings: -e (ich), -st (du), -t (er/sie/es), -en (wir), -t (ihr), -en (sie/Sie).
3. Verbs whose stem ends in -t or -d add an extra -e- before -st and -t endings for pronunciation: arbeiten → du arbeitest, er arbeitet; zeichnen → du zeichnest, er zeichnet.
4. Verbs whose stem ends in -s, -ß, -z, -x: the du ending becomes just -t (the -s from -st merges): heißen → du heißt (not heißst).

Lesson 15

Irregular Verb Conjugation — Unregelmäßige Verben konjugieren

Irregular (strong) verbs change their stem vowel in the du and er/sie/es forms. There are three main patterns: e→i, e→ie, and a→ä. Learn the patterns and you can predict most changes.

Grammar Rules
1. Stem-changing verbs only change in 2nd person singular (du) and 3rd person singular (er/sie/es). All other forms use the infinitive stem unchanged.
2. Pattern e→i: sprechen, essen, helfen, geben, nehmen, treffen. The stem vowel e becomes i (or ie doesn't apply here — it shortens to i).
3. Pattern e→ie: lesen, sehen, stehlen. The stem vowel e becomes ie (longer sound). Note: lesen → du liest (the e in lese becomes ie in du/er).
4. Pattern a→ä: fahren, schlafen, laufen, tragen, waschen. The stem vowel a gets an umlaut in du and er/sie/es forms.
