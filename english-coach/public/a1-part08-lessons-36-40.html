Lesson 36

Personal Pronouns Dative — Personalpronomen im Dativ

Just as you learned that 'helfen' (to help) forces a noun into the indirect object form, it also forces personal pronouns to change. If you want to say 'Help me!' or 'I thank you', you cannot use 'ich' or 'mich'. You must use the special words 'mir' and 'dir'. Mastering these short words completely transforms how you interact with Germans.

Grammar Rules
When a pronoun is the indirect object (following verbs like helfen, danken, gehören) or a preposition like 'mit', it must take a special form.
'ich' becomes 'mir' (to me).
'du' becomes 'dir' (to you).
'er' (he) and 'es' (it) both become 'ihm' (to him / to it).
'sie' (she) becomes 'ihr' (to her).
Plurals: 'wir' → 'uns', 'ihr' → 'euch', 'sie/Sie' → 'ihnen/Ihnen'.
Formula
Subject + Verb + Indirect Object Pronoun
Example: Das Auto + gehört + mir.
Subject FormIndirect Object FormEnglish Meaning
ichmir(to) me
dudir(to) you (informal)
er / esihm(to) him / it
sieihr(to) her
wiruns(to) us
ihreuch(to) you all
sie / Sieihnen / Ihnen(to) them / you (formal)

ihmto him / itDas Buch gehört ihm. — The book belongs to him.

Model Sentences
Kannst du mir helfen?
Can you help me?
Kannst = Can | du = you (subject) | mir = to me (indirect object pronoun) | helfen = help (sent to the end) → 'ich' becomes 'mir' as the indirect object.
Das Essen ist sehr lecker. Es schmeckt mir gut.
The food is very delicious. It tastes good to me.
Das Essen = The food | ist sehr lecker = is very delicious | Es = It (subject) | schmeckt = tastes | mir = to me | gut = well → 'schmecken' always needs an indirect object pronoun.
Gehört der Stift dir oder gehört er ihm?
Does the pen belong to you or does it belong to him?
Gehört = Belongs | der Stift = the pen (subject) | dir = to you | oder = or | ihm = to him → 'gehören' always points to the owner.
Ich antworte der Chefin. Ich antworte ihr.
I answer the boss. I answer her.
Ich antworte = I answer | der Chefin = the boss (indirect object noun) | ihr = her (indirect object pronoun replacing 'die Chefin') → The pronoun replaces the noun but stays in the same indirect object form.
Practice Exercises
Der Kaffee ist für Tom. Er schmeckt ___ sehr gut.
Answer: ihm (to him)
Anna sucht ihr Buch. Das Buch gehört ___.
Answer: ihr (to her)
Herr Schmidt, kann ich ___ helfen?
Answer: Ihnen (to you formal)
Translate: I thank you (informal).
Answer: Ich danke dir.
Translate: The car belongs to me.
Answer: Das Auto gehört mir.
Common Mistakes
❌ Ich helfe dich.
✓ Ich helfe dir.
English speakers use the direct object for 'help'. In German, 'helfen' strictly requires the indirect object form — 'dich' MUST become 'dir'.
❌ Das T-Shirt gefällt mich.
✓ Das T-Shirt gefällt mir.
'gefallen' (to be pleasing to) requires the indirect object form. It pleases TO me → 'mir'.
Lesson 37

Separable Verbs — Trennbare Verben

German has a fantastic way of creating new verbs: it glues a little preposition (like 'an' or 'auf') to the front of a base verb. 'Kommen' means to come, but 'ankommen' means to arrive. However, in a normal sentence, these verbs break apart! The prefix detaches and flies all the way to the very end of the sentence, creating the famous German 'sentence bracket'.

Grammar Rules
A separable verb consists of a prefix (an-, auf-, ein-, mit-, etc.) and a base verb (fangen, stehen, kaufen).
In a present tense statement, the base verb takes Position 2 and is conjugated normally.
The prefix breaks off and goes to the VERY END of the clause.
If you use a Modal verb (like 'möchten' or 'können'), the separable verb does NOT split. The whole word goes to the end in its full form.
Formula
Standard: Subject (1) + Conjugated Base Verb (2) + Object/Time + Prefix (End).
Modal: Subject (1) + Modal Verb (2) + Object/Time + Full Separable Verb (End).
Vocabulary
GermanEnglishExample
aufstehento wake up / get upIch stehe um 7 Uhr auf. — I get up at 7 o'clock.
anfangento begin / startDer Kurs fängt jetzt an. — The course starts now.
einkaufento shop (groceries)Wir kaufen im Supermarkt ein. — We are shopping in the supermarket.
fernsehento watch TVEr sieht abends fern. — He watches TV in the evening.
anrufento call (phone)Rufst du mich heute an? — Are you calling me today?
mitkommento come alongKommst du mit? — Are you coming along?
zumachento closeIch mache die Tür zu. — I close the door.
aufmachento openMachst du das Fenster auf? — Are you opening the window?
abfahrento departDer Zug fährt um 8 Uhr ab. — The train departs at 8 o'clock.
ankommento arriveWann kommt der Bus an? — When does the bus arrive?
Model Sentences
Ich kaufe heute im Supermarkt ein.
I am shopping in the supermarket today.
Ich = I | kaufe = buy (base verb in position 2, without the 'ein' prefix) | heute = today | im Supermarkt = in the supermarket | ein = prefix sent to the very end → The prefix 'ein' breaks off from 'einkaufen'.
Der Film fängt um acht Uhr an.
The movie starts at eight o'clock.
Der Film = The movie (subject) | fängt = starts (base verb in position 2) | um acht Uhr = at eight o'clock | an = prefix sent to the end → 'anfangen' splits: base verb comes 2nd, prefix goes last.
Wann stehst du am Wochenende auf?
When do you get up on the weekend?
Wann = When (position 1) | stehst = stand (base verb, position 2) | du = you | am Wochenende = on the weekend | auf = prefix sent to the end → Question structure with a separable verb.
Ich möchte heute einkaufen.
I would like to shop today.
Ich = I | möchte = would like (modal verb, position 2) | heute = today | einkaufen = the full separable verb goes to the end without splitting → When a modal verb is used, the separable verb stays whole.
Practice Exercises
Reorder: stehe / um sechs Uhr / auf / Ich
Answer: Ich stehe um sechs Uhr auf.
Reorder: fern / wir / am Abend / sehen
Answer: Am Abend sehen wir fern.
Der Zug kommt um 15 Uhr ___. (ankommen)
Answer: an
Bitte mache das Fenster ___. (zumachen)
Answer: zu
Common Mistakes
❌ Ich aufstehe um 7 Uhr.
✓ Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.
Treating a separable verb like an English word. You MUST physically break the prefix off and move it to the end.
❌ Er seht fern.
✓ Er sieht fern.
Forgetting that the base verb might be irregular! 'sehen' changes its vowel from 'e' to 'ie' for 'er'. So 'fernsehen' becomes 'er sieht... fern'.
Lesson 38

Daily Routine — Tagesablauf

Talking about your daily routine is the ultimate test of everything you've learned so far. You will need to use time expressions, separable verbs (like waking up), and most importantly: Inversion. When you string actions together using 'first', 'then', and 'after that', those words take Position 1, which means the verb stays in Position 2, pushing the subject to Position 3.

Grammar Rules
To describe a sequence of events, use transition words: zuerst (first), dann (then), danach (after that), and später (later).
Because these transition words occupy Position 1, the verb MUST be in Position 2.
The subject ('ich') gets pushed to Position 3 (e.g., Dann trinke ich Kaffee).
If you use a separable verb (like aufstehen), the prefix still goes to the very end of the sentence.
Formula
Time/Sequence Word (1) + Verb (2) + Subject (3) + Rest + (Prefix).
Zuerst + stehe + ich + um 7 Uhr + auf.
Vocabulary
GermanEnglishExample
zuerstfirstZuerst dusche ich. — First I shower.
dannthenDann frühstücke ich. — Then I eat breakfast.
danachafter thatDanach gehe ich arbeiten. — After that I go to work.
späterlaterSpäter kaufe ich ein. — Later I go shopping.
duschento take a showerIch dusche morgens. — I shower in the morning.
frühstückento eat breakfastWann frühstückst du? — When do you eat breakfast?
arbeitento workIch arbeite von 9 bis 17 Uhr. — I work from 9 to 17.
Mittagspause machento take a lunch breakDann mache ich Mittagspause. — Then I take a lunch break.
Feierabend machento finish work for the dayUm 18 Uhr mache ich Feierabend. — At 18:00 I finish work.
ins Bett gehento go to bedIch gehe um 23 Uhr ins Bett. — I go to bed at 23:00.
Model Sentences
Zuerst stehe ich um sieben Uhr auf.
First I get up at seven o'clock.
Zuerst = First (position 1) | stehe = stand (base verb, position 2) | ich = I (pushed to position 3) | um sieben Uhr = at seven o'clock | auf = prefix sent to the end → Sequence word pushes 'ich' back.
Dann dusche ich und danach frühstücke ich.
Then I shower and after that I eat breakfast.
Dann = Then (position 1) | dusche = shower (position 2) | ich = I (position 3) | und = and | danach = after that (position 1 again) | frühstücke = eat breakfast (position 2) | ich = I (position 3) → The rule repeats for each new clause.
Um acht Uhr fahre ich zur Arbeit.
At eight o'clock I drive to work.
Um acht Uhr = At eight o'clock (position 1 — time block) | fahre = drive (position 2) | ich = I (position 3) | zur Arbeit = to work → A time block in position 1 works the same way.
Später sehe ich fern und gehe ins Bett.
Later I watch TV and go to bed.
Später = Later (position 1) | sehe = see (position 2) | ich = I (position 3) | fern = prefix at the end | und = and | gehe ins Bett = go to bed → Two actions linked with 'und'.
Practice Exercises
Reorder: Dann / ich / Kaffee / trinke
Answer: Dann trinke ich Kaffee.
Reorder: auf / Zuerst / ich / stehe
Answer: Zuerst stehe ich auf.
Reorder: gehe / ich / Danach / arbeiten
Answer: Danach gehe ich arbeiten.
Translate: Later I eat breakfast.
Answer: Später frühstücke ich.
Translate: Then I watch TV. (fernsehen)
Answer: Dann sehe ich fern.
Common Mistakes
❌ Dann ich frühstücke.
✓ Dann frühstücke ich.
The most persistent A1 mistake! When you start a sentence with 'Dann', 'Zuerst', or 'Danach', the verb MUST come immediately after it in Position 2.
❌ Zuerst ich stehe auf.
✓ Zuerst stehe ich auf.
A double mistake: the verb is pushed to the wrong position, and the separable prefix is forgotten. Correct order: 'Zuerst stehe ich auf'.
Lesson 39

Imperative Sentences — Imperativsätze

Sometimes you don't want to ask a question; you need to give a command, advice, or an instruction. 'Come here!', 'Read this!', 'Drink water!'. In German, this is called the Imperative. Depending on who you are talking to, the way you construct a command changes significantly.

Grammar Rules
For Formal 'Sie' (strangers/adults): Put the verb in Position 1, followed immediately by 'Sie'. (Kommen Sie!)
For Informal Plural 'ihr' (a group of friends): Just use the normal 'ihr' conjugation and drop the pronoun. (Kommt!)
For Informal Singular 'du' (one friend): Take the 'du' conjugation, and chop off both the '-st' ending and the word 'du'. (Kommst du → Komm!)
To make a command polite, simply add the word 'bitte' (please) anywhere after the verb.
Formula
Formal (Sie): Verb(en) + Sie + Rest! (Gehen Sie!)
Plural (ihr): Verb(t) + Rest! (Geht!)
Singular (du): Verb stem + Rest! (Geh!)
Base VerbFormal (Sie)Informal Plural (ihr)Informal Singular (du)
kommen (to come)Kommen Sie!Kommt!Komm!
machen (to do/make)Machen Sie!Macht!Mach!
trinken (to drink)Trinken Sie!Trinkt!Trink!
lesen (to read / e→ie)Lesen Sie!Lest!Lies! (keep vowel change, drop '-st')
Vocabulary
GermanEnglishExample
kommento comeKomm bitte her! — Please come here!
gehento goGehen Sie geradeaus. — Go straight ahead.
machento do / makeMach deine Hausaufgaben! — Do your homework!
sprechento speakSprechen Sie langsam, bitte. — Speak slowly, please.
lesento readLies den Text. — Read the text.
wartento waitWartet hier auf mich. — Wait here for me. (to a group)
helfento helpHilf mir! — Help me!

bittepleaseUnterschreiben Sie bitte hier. — Please sign here.
Model Sentences
Trinken Sie viel Wasser!
Drink lots of water! (Formal)
Trinken = Verb in position 1 | Sie = formal pronoun right after | viel Wasser = lots of water → Formal command: verb first, then 'Sie'.
Komm bitte heute Abend zu mir.
Please come to my place tonight. (Informal singular)
Komm = Base verb with '-st' removed (no 'du' needed) | bitte = please | heute Abend = tonight | zu mir = to my place → Informal singular command: just the bare stem.
Macht die Tür zu, Kinder!
Close the door, children! (Informal plural)
Macht = Normal '-t' ending for 'ihr' (no 'ihr' needed) | die Tür = the door | zu = prefix at end | Kinder = children → Informal plural command: normal '-t' form without the pronoun.
Sprich bitte etwas lauter.
Please speak a little louder. (Informal singular)
Sprich = Irregular vowel-changed stem (e → i), drop the '-st' | bitte = please | etwas lauter = a little louder → Irregular verb: keep the vowel change, drop the '-st'.
Practice Exercises
___ (kaufen) Milch! (du form)
Answer: Kauf (Drop the '-st')
___ (trinken) den Kaffee! (du form)
Answer: Trink (Drop the '-st')
___ (helfen) mir! (du form)
Answer: Hilf (Irregular vowel changes to 'i')
Translate (formal): Come here, please! (herkommen)
Answer: Kommen Sie bitte her!
Translate (formal): Wait here. (warten)
Answer: Warten Sie hier.
Common Mistakes
❌ Kommst du hier!
✓ Komm hier!
You must drop BOTH the '-st' and the word 'du' to form a command for a friend. Keeping them makes it a question ('Are you coming here?').
❌ Machen das bitte!
✓ Machen Sie das bitte!
For the formal command, you CANNOT drop the pronoun. The word 'Sie' is absolutely mandatory immediately after the verb.
Lesson 40

Giving Directions — Wegbeschreibung

Getting lost in a German city is inevitable, but asking for and understanding directions is a skill you can easily master. This lesson combines the Imperative (commands like 'go', 'turn') with specific directional vocabulary. Once you know your left from your right, you can navigate any street in Berlin or Munich.

Grammar Rules
Use the Imperative 'Gehen Sie...' (Go...) or 'Fahren Sie...' (Drive/Ride...) to give directions.
To say 'straight ahead', use 'geradeaus'.
To say 'turn right/left', use the separable verb 'abbiegen' → 'Biegen Sie rechts/links ab'.
Use 'bis zur' (until/up to) with feminine landmarks: 'bis zur Ampel' or 'bis zur Kreuzung'.
Formula
Gehen/Fahren Sie + [Direction: geradeaus / nach links / nach rechts].
Biegen Sie + [links/rechts] + ab.
Gehen Sie bis zur + [Landmark: Ampel / Kreuzung].

rechtsrightDie Bank ist auf der rechten Seite. — The bank is on the right side.
abbiegento turn (direction)Biegen Sie hier ab. — Turn here.
die StraßestreetGehen Sie diese Straße entlang. — Go along this street.

Model Sentences
Entschuldigung, wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?
Excuse me, how do I get to the train station?
Entschuldigung = Excuse me | wie = how | komme = come | ich = I | zum Bahnhof = to the train station ('zu' + 'dem' combined to 'zum') → Standard phrase to ask for directions.
Gehen Sie geradeaus bis zur Ampel.
Go straight ahead up to the traffic light.
Gehen Sie = Go (formal command) | geradeaus = straight ahead | bis zur = up to (preposition for feminine destination) | Ampel = traffic light → 'bis zur' means 'until you reach' a feminine landmark.
Biegen Sie an der Kreuzung rechts ab.
Turn right at the intersection.
Biegen Sie = Turn (formal command) | an der Kreuzung = at the intersection | rechts = right | ab = prefix at the end of the clause → 'abbiegen' is a separable verb — 'ab' goes to the end.
Der Supermarkt ist an der Ecke auf der linken Seite.
The supermarket is on the corner on the left side.
Der Supermarkt = The supermarket | ist = is | an der Ecke = on the corner | auf der linken Seite = on the left side → Describing the location of the destination.
Practice Exercises
Translate (formal): Go straight ahead.
Answer: Gehen Sie geradeaus.
Translate (formal): Turn left here. (abbiegen)
Answer: Biegen Sie hier links ab.
Gehen Sie bis zur ___ (traffic light).
Answer: Ampel
Die Apotheke ist an der ___ (corner).
Answer: Ecke
Common Mistakes
❌ Biegen Sie links.
✓ Biegen Sie links ab.
The verb is 'abbiegen', which is separable. If you just say 'biegen', it means 'bend'. You MUST put the 'ab' at the end of the sentence to mean 'turn'.
❌ Gehen rechts.
✓ Gehen Sie rechts. / Geh rechts.
Failing to use the proper command structure. You must either include 'Sie' for formal, or drop the '-en' entirely for informal ('Geh').
