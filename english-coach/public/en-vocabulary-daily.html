Food & Cooking vd02

Master food vocabulary to order confidently, follow recipes, and talk about meals in any social situation.

Meals of the Day

Meal Names

breakfast — first meal of the day
"I never skip breakfast on workdays."

brunch — late morning meal combining breakfast and lunch
"We went for brunch at eleven on Sunday."

lunch — midday meal
"She usually has a sandwich for lunch."

dinner / supper — main evening meal
"Dinner will be ready at seven."

snack — small amount of food eaten between meals
"He grabbed a snack before the meeting."

How Food Is Prepared

raw — not cooked
"I prefer raw carrots to cooked ones."

fresh — recently made or harvested
"This bread is fresh from the oven."

frozen — preserved at freezing temperature
"We only had frozen peas."

tinned / canned — preserved in a metal tin
"Open a tin of tomatoes for the sauce."

processed — industrially prepared, often with additives
"Try to eat less processed food."

Cooking Methods

MethodMeaningExample
boilcook in very hot water (100°C)Boil the pasta for ten minutes.
frycook in hot oil in a panFry the onions until golden.
grillcook under direct heat or on a grill rackGrill the chicken for 20 minutes, turning once.
roastcook meat or vegetables in a hot ovenRoast the potatoes at 200°C for 40 minutes.
bakecook bread, cakes or pastry in the ovenShe baked a lemon cake for the party.
steamcook using steam from boiling waterSteam the broccoli to keep the nutrients.
stir-frycook quickly in very hot oil while stirringStir-fry the vegetables for just three minutes.
simmercook gently in liquid just below boiling pointLet the soup simmer for half an hour.

Restaurant Phrases

Useful Restaurant Phrases
  • "A table for two, please." — when you arrive
  • "Could I see the menu?" — asking for the menu
  • "What do you recommend?" — asking the waiter for advice
  • "I'll have the chicken, please." — ordering your food
  • "Could I have it without onions?" — making a modification
  • "Is this dish suitable for vegetarians?" — dietary enquiry
  • "Could we have the bill / check, please?" — asking to pay
  • "Keep the change." — when tipping in cash
Memory Trick Bill (British) vs check (American) — both mean the paper showing what you owe. If in doubt, mime a writing action and say "whenever you're ready" — waiters understand everywhere!

Health & Body vd05

Whether visiting a GP, going to the pharmacy, or describing how you feel, accurate health vocabulary is essential.

Parts of the Body

Head & Torso
  • forehead — upper front of the head
  • temple — side of the forehead
  • jaw — lower part of the face
  • neck — connects head and shoulders
  • shoulder — top of the arm
  • chest — front of the upper body
  • back — rear of the torso
  • spine / backbone — central column of bones
  • waist — narrow middle part of the body
  • hip — side of the pelvis
Limbs & Extremities
  • upper arm / forearm — above/below the elbow
  • elbow — joint in the middle of the arm
  • wrist — joint between hand and forearm
  • palm — inside surface of the hand
  • thumb / index / middle / ring / little finger
  • thigh — upper leg
  • knee — joint in the middle of the leg
  • shin — front of the lower leg
  • ankle — joint above the foot
  • heel / sole / toe — parts of the foot

Weather & Seasons vd06

Weather is the most common small-talk topic in English — especially in the UK! Master this section and you'll never run out of conversation.

Weather Words

WordMeaningExample Sentence
sunnybright with sunshineIt's a beautiful sunny day — let's go to the park.

drizzlelight, fine rainIt's just a light drizzle — you won't need a coat.
showershort burst of rainThere'll be scattered showers in the afternoon.
downpourheavy rain falling suddenlyWe got caught in a downpour without umbrellas.

gale / gale-force windsvery strong windsGale-force winds caused delays at the airport.

Describing Climate

Temperature Words
  • boiling / scorching — extremely hot
  • warm — pleasantly high temperature
  • mild — moderate, not too cold or hot
  • cool / chilly — slightly cold
  • cold — low temperature
  • freezing / bitter — extremely cold
Seasons
  • spring — March–May (Northern Hemisphere)
  • summer — June–August
  • autumn / fall — September–November
  • winter — December–February

"My favourite season is autumn — I love the changing colours."

Forecasting Phrases

Weather Forecast Language
  • "Temperatures will reach a high of 28°C tomorrow."
  • "Rain is expected throughout the morning."
  • "There's a 70% chance of showers by mid-afternoon."
  • "Strong winds are likely along the coast."
  • "It's going to clear up later this evening."
  • "Visibility will be poor due to fog on high ground."
Memory Trick British people use the weather to break silence — "Terrible weather, isn't it?" is never rude; it's an invitation. Reply: "Awful, isn't it! At least it's supposed to improve by the weekend."

Expressing How You Feel

Useful Phrases
  • "I'm feeling a bit under the weather." — not very well
  • "I'm on top of the world!" — extremely happy
  • "I'm in two minds about it." — unsure, can't decide
  • "It gets on my nerves." — it irritates me
  • "I couldn't care less." — it doesn't matter to me at all
  • "I'm over the moon!" — thrilled (very British expression)
  • "It really got to me." — it affected me emotionally
  • "I'm a bit out of sorts today." — not feeling quite right

Asking Others How They Feel

Check-In Phrases
  • "Are you alright?" — British way of asking "are you okay?"
  • "How are you holding up?" — asked when someone is going through something difficult
  • "Is everything okay? You seem a bit quiet today."
  • "Do you want to talk about it?"
  • "That sounds really tough — I'm sorry you're going through that."

Memory Trick In English, Would you mind…? is answered confusingly: "No, not at all" means YES, I'll do it. If someone says "Do you mind if I sit here?" answer "No, go ahead" — not "Yes, please!" which sounds like you're objecting!
