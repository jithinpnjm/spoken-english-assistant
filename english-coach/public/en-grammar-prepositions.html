sp01 — Overview

Prepositions are small words that express relationships between other words — mainly relationships of time, place, movement, and reason. Despite being short, they are among the hardest parts of English for learners because:

  • The same preposition can mean different things in different contexts.
  • Prepositions often don't translate directly from other languages.
  • Many collocations (adjective + prep, verb + prep) are fixed idioms — there's no logical rule, only memory.
  • The boundaries between AT / ON / IN overlap and shift depending on whether you mean time, place, or something else.
AT

A specific point — in time or place. Think of a pin on a map or an exact clock time.

at 3 pm · at the door · at school

ON

A surface (place) or a day/date (time). Think of something resting on top.

on Monday · on the table · on the wall

IN

Inside an enclosed space (place) or a longer period (time). Think of something surrounded.

in January · in the box · in London

The AT / ON / IN ladder for time Go from most specific to most general: AT (exact time) → ON (day/date) → IN (month/year/season). "I'll see you at 3 pm on Monday in March."

sp02 — Time Prepositions

AT — specific / precise time

Use AT for…
  • Clock times: at 7 o'clock, at noon, at midnight, at 3:30 pm
  • Specific points in the day: at sunrise, at sunset, at dawn, at dusk
  • Mealtimes: at breakfast, at lunch, at dinner
  • Certain fixed expressions: at night, at the weekend (BrE), at Christmas, at Easter, at the moment, at the same time, at present, at that time
CategoryExample phrases
Clock timesat 8 am · at half past two · at a quarter to six
Mealtimesat breakfast · at lunch · at dinner · at teatime
Night (dark)at night (note: "in the morning/afternoon/evening")
Holidaysat Christmas · at Easter · at Diwali · at New Year
Fixed phrasesat the moment · at present · at first · at last · at once

ON — days and dates

Use ON for…
  • Days of the week: on Monday, on Friday, on weekdays
  • Specific dates: on 15 August, on 1st January
  • Named days: on Christmas Day, on my birthday, on New Year's Eve
  • Day + part of day: on Monday morning, on a sunny afternoon
CategoryExample phrases
Dayson Monday · on Tuesday evening · on weekends (AmE)
Dateson 3 March · on the 25th · on 1 April 2024
Named dayson Christmas Day · on my birthday · on our anniversary
Day + timeon Monday morning · on a cold winter's night

IN — months, years, seasons, periods

Use IN for…
  • Months: in January, in March, in December
  • Years: in 2024, in the 1990s, in the 21st century
  • Seasons: in spring, in summer, in autumn, in winter
  • Parts of the day: in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening
  • Periods of time (future): in a moment, in an hour, in two weeks
CategoryExample phrases
Monthsin January · in August · in mid-October
Years / decadesin 1999 · in the 80s · in the 20th century
Seasonsin spring · in summer · in the rainy season
Parts of dayin the morning · in the afternoon · in the evening
Durations (future)in five minutes · in a week · in three months' time

The "no preposition" rule Drop the preposition before last, next, this, every, yesterday, today, tomorrow: "I'll see you next Monday" (not on next Monday). "She called yesterday" (not on yesterday).

sp03 — Place Prepositions

AT
A point / location
  • at the bus stop
  • at the door
  • at the top of the page
  • at 10 Downing Street
  • at school / at work
  • at the airport
  • at the corner
  • at the end of the road
ON
A surface / line
  • on the table
  • on the wall
  • on the floor / ceiling
  • on the first floor
  • on Oxford Street
  • on the left / right
  • on the coast
  • on the border
IN
An enclosed space
  • in the box
  • in the room
  • in London / Paris
  • in England / France
  • in the sea / river
  • in the north
  • in bed (no article)
  • in the sky

AT vs IN for buildings and institutions

AT (function / activity)IN (physical location)
at school (studying)in the school building
at work (working)in the office
at the hospital (visiting / treated)in the hospital (admitted, staying)
at home (general)in the house (specific room/building)
at the cinema (watching a film)in the cinema (inside the building)
AT = function, IN = physical "She's at school" means she is studying there. "She's in the school" means she is physically inside the building (e.g. the principal saying this during a lockdown).

ON for streets and surfaces

Examples
  • I live on Baker Street. (not in Baker Street in British English)
  • The keys are on the table.
  • There's a spider on the ceiling.
  • We live on the third floor.
  • The village is on the coast.

IN for water bodies

Examples
  • The fish are swimming in the river.
  • She went for a swim in the sea.
  • There are sharks in the ocean.
  • He drowned in a lake. (not "on" a lake — that would mean on a boat)

Special place expressions to memorise

in the north/south/east/westin"Edinburgh is in the north of Scotland."
on the coast / on the borderon"The town is on the coast."
at the back / at the frontat"Meet me at the back of the theatre."
in the middlein"There's a fountain in the middle."
at the corner / on the cornerat/onBoth are acceptable.
in bed / in hospital (BrE)inNo article in British English.

sp04 — Movement Prepositions

Movement prepositions describe direction or path of motion. They often pair with specific motion verbs.

PrepositionMeaningExample
totowards a destination (arrival implied)She walked to the shop.
towardsin the direction of (arrival not implied)He moved towards the door slowly.
intomovement to insideShe walked into the room.
out ofmovement from inside to outsideHe ran out of the building.
ontomovement to a surfaceThe cat jumped onto the table.
offmovement away from a surfaceShe fell off the chair.
away frommovement increasing distanceHe drove away from the city.
throughmovement from one side to anotherWe walked through the forest.
acrossmovement from one side of a surface to the otherShe swam across the river.
alongmovement following a lineWe strolled along the beach.
pastmovement going beyond a pointThe bus went past my stop.
overmovement above and acrossThe plane flew over the mountains.
under / beneathmovement at a lower levelWe drove under the bridge.
aroundmovement in a circle or various directionsThey walked around the lake.
up / downmovement to a higher / lower levelShe climbed up the stairs.
to vs towards "I'm going to the library" — I will arrive there. "I'm walking towards the library" — I am moving in that direction but may not arrive (someone might stop me, or I might turn off).
into vs in to (two words) Into (movement): "She walked into the shop." Two separate words in to appear in phrases where "in" belongs to a phrasal verb: "He gave in to pressure" (gave in = surrendered).
Movement verbs + prepositions in context
  • The children ran across the playground.
  • She climbed over the fence.
  • He crawled through the tunnel.
  • They drove along the motorway.
  • The bird flew away from the cat.
  • We sailed past the lighthouse.
  • She dived into the pool.
  • He stepped out of the car.

sp05 — Tricky Preposition Pairs

Transport: IN vs ON vs BY

IN — enclosed, small vehicles
  • in a car
  • in a taxi / cab
  • in a van
  • in a helicopter
  • in a lift / elevator
ON — larger / public transport
  • on a bus / coach
  • on a train
  • on a plane / aircraft
  • on a ship / ferry
  • on a bike / motorbike / horse
Quick rule If you can stand up inside it (bus, train, plane, ship) → ON. If you sit enclosed and can't easily stand (car, taxi) → IN.
BY — mode of transport (no article)
  • I came by car.  /  She travels by train.
  • by bus · by plane · by bike · by foot (or on foot)
  • Note: "on foot" not "by foot" for walking.

IN vs AT for places

SituationATIN
General activityat work · at school · at university—
Physical inside—in the office · in the classroom · in the library
Eventsat a concert · at a party · at a meeting—
Cities / countries—in London · in Japan · in the UK
Addresses (point)at 5 Oak Lane · at this address—

BY vs WITH vs USING

WordUseExample
byagent in passive / method / meansThe book was written by Orwell. · I paid by card.
withinstrument / tool usedShe cut it with a knife. · He wrote with a pen.
usingmore explicit means (can replace "with")She solved it using a calculator.
BY or WITH? "He was hit by a ball" — the ball is the agent. "He was hit with a ball" — someone used the ball as an instrument. By = doer. With = tool.

FOR vs SINCE vs AGO

WordUseExample
forduration (length of time)I've lived here for three years.
sincestarting point (a moment in the past)I've lived here since 2021.
agotime before now (simple past only)She left two hours ago.
WRONG
I have been waiting since three hours.
CORRECT
I have been waiting for three hours.
WRONG
She left since two days.
CORRECT
She left two days ago.

sp06 — Phrasal Verbs with Common Prepositions

Phrasal verbs are verb + particle combinations where the meaning is often idiomatic. These are grouped by particle.

UP — often suggests completion, improvement, or increase
give up
stop doing sth / surrender: "Don't give up!"
pick up
collect / lift / learn informally: "Pick up the kids at 3."
set up
establish / arrange: "She set up a new business."
make up
invent / reconcile: "He made up an excuse."
turn up
arrive / increase volume: "She turned up late."
come up
arise / be mentioned: "An interesting point came up."
look up
search for information: "Look it up in the dictionary."
end up
finish in a state/place: "We ended up at the wrong station."
OUT — often suggests discovery, completion, or exhaustion
find out
discover: "I found out the truth."
run out of
exhaust supply: "We've run out of milk."
carry out
perform / execute: "They carried out the plan."
point out
indicate: "She pointed out my mistake."
work out
exercise / calculate / succeed: "It all worked out fine."
sort out
resolve / organise: "Let's sort out this problem."
figure out
understand: "I can't figure out how it works."
fall out
quarrel: "They fell out over money."
ON — often suggests continuation
carry on
continue: "Carry on working."
put on
wear / switch on: "Put on your coat."
go on
continue / happen: "What's going on?"
take on
accept responsibility: "She took on a new role."
move on
proceed / leave behind: "It's time to move on."
hold on
wait / grip: "Hold on a moment."
log on
access a computer system: "Log on with your password."
count on
rely on: "You can count on me."
OFF — often suggests stopping, leaving, or removal
turn off
switch off: "Turn off the lights."
call off
cancel: "They called off the meeting."
take off
remove clothing / (plane) depart: "Take off your shoes."
put off
postpone / discourage: "Stop putting it off."
go off
explode / ring / go bad: "The alarm went off."
lay off
dismiss workers: "They laid off 200 staff."
pay off
repay / prove successful: "Hard work pays off."
show off
display proudly (or boast): "Stop showing off."
Separable vs inseparable phrasal verbs Most transitive phrasal verbs are separable: you can put the object in the middle. "Turn it off." / "Turn off the light." But with pronouns, you MUST separate: "Turn it off" — never "Turn off it." Inseparable phrasal verbs (like "run into", "look after") cannot be split.

sp07 — Fixed Preposition Collocations

These combinations are memorised, not derived from rules. They are among the most common vocabulary gaps for advanced learners.

Adjective + Preposition

adjective + AT

good at — "She's good at maths."
bad at — "I'm terrible at singing."
brilliant at — "He's brilliant at chess."
surprised at/by — "She was surprised at the news."
shocked at/by — "We were shocked at the price."

adjective + OF

afraid of — "He's afraid of spiders."
proud of — "I'm proud of you."
ashamed of — "She was ashamed of her behaviour."
aware of — "Are you aware of the risks?"
capable of — "She's capable of more."

adjective + IN

interested in — "He's interested in art."
involved in — "She was involved in the project."
experienced in — "They're experienced in finance."
skilled in — "He's skilled in carpentry."
successful in — "She was successful in her appeal."

adjective + FOR / TO / WITH / ON

responsible for — "Who is responsible for this?"
married to — "She is married to John."
similar to — "This is similar to that one."
bored with — "I'm bored with this game."
keen on — "She's keen on hiking."
worried about — "I'm worried about you."

Verb + Preposition

look + preposition

look at — "Look at this photo."
look for — "I'm looking for my keys."
look after — "Can you look after the dog?"
look into — "Police are looking into it."
look forward to — "I look forward to meeting you."

depend / agree / consist

depend on — "It depends on the weather."
rely on — "You can rely on her."
agree with — "I agree with you."
disagree with — "She disagreed with the plan."
consist of — "The team consists of five people."

apply / apologise / believe

apply for — "She applied for the job."
apply to — "This rule applies to everyone."
apologise for — "I apologise for being late."
believe in — "She believes in hard work."
specialise in — "He specialises in tax law."

talk / ask / complain / care

talk to / with — "Talk to me about it."
talk about — "Let's talk about the plan."
ask for — "She asked for help."
complain about — "He complained about the noise."
care about — "She cares about others."
How to memorise collocations Don't learn the adjective/verb alone — always memorise the full chunk: "interested in", "responsible for", "married to". Record them in example sentences, not as isolated pairs.

sp08 — Common Mistakes

Fifteen preposition errors that appear constantly in learner English — with corrections and explanations.

#WrongCorrectRule
1 I arrived to London. I arrived in London. "Arrive" + in (city/country) or at (specific place). Never "arrive to".
2 She is married with him. She is married to him. Married to a person. "With" is wrong here.
3 We discussed about the problem. We discussed the problem. "Discuss" is transitive — no preposition needed.
4 She explained me the answer. She explained the answer to me. "Explain" uses "to" for the recipient — it's not ditransitive like "give".
5 I've been waiting since three hours. I've been waiting for three hours. "Since" + point in time. "For" + duration.
6 He is good in English. He is good at English. Adjectives of ability collocate with "at": good/bad/terrible/excellent at.
7 I am interested about cooking. I am interested in cooking. Fixed collocation: interested in.
8 She depends of her parents. She depends on her parents. Fixed collocation: depend on.
9 I went to home. I went home. "Home" as adverb needs no preposition: go home, come home, stay home.
10 She entered into the room. She entered the room. "Enter" is transitive — no preposition. "Into" is redundant here.
11 I listened the music. I listened to the music. Fixed: listen to (always). Compare: "hear" takes no preposition — "I heard the music."
12 We spoke about in French. We spoke in French. Language used: "in French/English/Spanish". No "about" unless you mean the topic.
13 He suffers of depression. He suffers from depression. Fixed: suffer from an illness.
14 I'm looking forward to see you. I'm looking forward to seeing you. "To" here is a preposition, not part of the infinitive → use gerund (-ing).
15 Despite of the rain, we went out. Despite the rain, we went out. "Despite" = preposition, needs no "of". Use "in spite of" if you want to include "of".
The "arrive" preposition in full
  • arrive in + city/country: "arrive in Paris"
  • arrive at + specific place: "arrive at the airport", "arrive at school"
  • arrive home — no preposition!
  • Never "arrive to"
Top 5 to drill first
  1. good at / interested in / responsible for / married to
  2. arrive in a city, arrive at a building
  3. listen to · depend on · suffer from
  4. "discuss" and "enter" → no preposition
  5. "looking forward to -ing" (to = preposition)
