B: "Gut, danke. Und Ihnen?" (Good, thanks. And you?)

💡 Job without article! In German, when stating your profession with sein (to be), you DON'T use an article:
✅ "Ich bin Arzt." (I am a doctor.)
❌ "Ich bin ein Arzt." — sounds odd in German!
Exception: when using arbeiten als → "Ich arbeite als ein Arzt." (fine either way)

🔊 The Special Characters Ä: like English "air" or "bear" — Ä = ähnlich → "AIR-nlich"
Ö: say "ee" then round your lips → Öl (oil) = "URL"
Ü: say "ee" with rounded lips → über = "OO-ber" with tight lips
ß: always sounds like a double "ss" → Straße = SHTRAH-sse
Tip: If you can't type ß, write "ss" instead — it's acceptable.
💡 Letters that sound different from English V sounds like English F: Vater = FAH-ter (not Vah-ter)
W sounds like English V: Wasser = VASS-er (not Wasser)
J sounds like English Y: ja = ya (not "jah")
Z sounds like "ts": Zeit = TSYTE (not zite)
S before vowel sounds like Z: sagen = ZAH-gen

💡 Pattern for 21–99 (ones before tens!) German says units BEFORE tens, connected by "und":
21 = einundzwanzig (one-and-twenty)
35 = fünfunddreißig (five-and-thirty)
67 = siebenundsechzig (seven-and-sixty)
Think like German clocks: "quarter to/past" reversed too!
💡 Irregular teens to memorise 16 = sechzehn (sechs drops the -s!)
17 = siebzehn (sieben drops the -en!)
30 = dreißig (not dreißzehn — it's a special form)
60 = sechzig (drops the -s again)
70 = siebzig (drops the -en)
🔊 Pronunciation Traps zwei = "TSVYE" (not "zwai")
sechs = "ZEKS" (not "sex" — the x sounds like ks)
sieben = "ZEE-ben" (soft b at end)
dreißig = "DRY-ssikh" (ß = ss, -ig = -ikh)

💡 sie vs Sie vs ihr sie (lowercase) = she OR they (context tells you which!)
Sie (CAPITAL) = formal "you" (for strangers, bosses, older people)
ihr = "you all" (talking to a group of friends) OR possessive "her"!
→ In German, "Sie" is always capitalised when meaning formal "you".
📌 When to use du vs Sie du = friends, family, children, young people, animals, God (in prayer)
Sie = strangers, older people, colleagues you don't know well, authority figures
Rule of thumb: If in doubt, use Sie. The other person can offer you the du form if they want to.

Tagesablauf — Daily Routine (sample paragraph)

🗣️ Sample Daily Routine Ich stehe um 6 Uhr auf. Dann jogge ich morgens. Danach dusche ich. Später gehe ich zum Goethe-Zentrum. Dann gehe ich ins Café. Danach gehe ich wieder zum Goethe-Zentrum. Um 12:30 Uhr gehe ich ins Hotel. Danach lese ich nachmittags. Später schwimme ich abends.

I get up at 6 o'clock. Then I jog in the morning. After that I shower. Later I go to the Goethe Centre. Then I go to the café. After that I go back to the Goethe Centre. At 12:30 I go to the hotel. After that I read in the afternoon. Later I swim in the evening.
