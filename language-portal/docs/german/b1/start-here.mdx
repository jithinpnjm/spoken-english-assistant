# B1: Start Here

:::info Status
B1 is in its first stage: the core grammar that most often decides B1 writing and speaking, the three writing tasks,
the three speaking tasks, and a repair bank. More chapters (passive, relative clauses, genitive, n-declension …)
will follow – see the [A2 & B1 Roadmap](../course-guide/a2-b1-roadmap.mdx).
:::

## What changes at B1
At A2 you can *explain*. At B1 you can **argue and organise**: give an opinion with reasons and examples, plan with
someone and negotiate, present a topic in a structure, and write personal and semi-formal emails and a short opinion
post. Explanations here are increasingly in German, with English for difficult grammar.

Konjunktiv II requests.

## Goethe-Zertifikat B1 (modular)
| Module | Time | Parts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lesen | 65 min | 5 parts: correspondence/blog (R/F), articles (MC), matching ads to situations, opinions (Ja/Nein), rules/instructions (MC) |
| Hören | 40 min | 4 parts: short announcements (twice), a talk (once), a conversation (once), a radio discussion (twice) |
| Schreiben | 60 min | personal email ≈80 words, opinion post ≈80 words, semi-formal email ≈40 words |
| Sprechen | 15 min per pair + 15 min preparation | plan something together, present a topic (5 slides), talk about both presentations |

Each module: max. 100 points, pass with **60**; modules can be taken separately. (Based on the official Goethe B1 Modellsatz.)

**Why B1 matters in Germany:** it is often the level asked for in work contexts and in residence or naturalisation
procedures. Requirements change and depend on your case – always check with the responsible office.
