# 5.4 Prices & Costs (Preise und Kosten)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can ask how much something costs in singular (*Wie viel kostet...?*) and plural (*Wie viel kosten...?*).
* I understand how Germans say prices out loud (*vier Euro fünfzig*).
* I know that currency words (*Euro, Cent*) do NOT take a plural "-s" after numbers.
* I understand the German decimal comma convention (4,99 € instead of $4.99).

---

## 1. Asking for Prices: The Two Golden Questions

When shopping in a market, store, or bakery, use these two simple formulas:

### Formula 1: "Wie viel kostet...?" (How much does... cost?)
* Singular: *„Wie viel **kostet** der Pullover?“* (How much does the sweater cost?)
* Singular: *„Wie viel **kostet** das Brot?“* (How much does the bread cost?)
* Plural: *„Wie viel **kosten** die Äpfel?“* (Notice: *kosten* adds an **-en** for plural items!).

### Formula 2: "Was kostet...?" (What does... cost?)
* *„Entschuldigung, was **kostet** ein Kilo Tomaten?“*
* *„Was **kostet** eine Fahrkarte nach Berlin?“*

---

## 2. Currency: Euro & Cent

The currency of Germany is **der Euro** (€), divided into 100 **Cent**:

### ⚠️ The No-Plural Rule for Currencies
In English, people often say "five euros". 
**In German, currency words after numbers stay in the singular:**
* ❌ *fünf Euros*
* ✅ *fünf **Euro***
* ❌ *fünfzig Cents*
* ✅ *fünfzig **Cent***

---

## 3. How Germans Say Prices Out Loud

In German, decimals are written with a **comma**, not a dot (*3,50 €*). 
When spoken, the word **Euro** is placed directly between the euro amount and the cents, and the word *Cent* is usually dropped:

```text
Written Price       Spoken in Everyday German          Literal Translation
    2,50 €      ➔   zwei Euro fünfzig                  "two Euro fifty"
    0,99 €      ➔   neunundneunzig Cent                "ninety-nine Cent"
   14,80 €      ➔   vierzehn Euro achtzig              "fourteen Euro eighty"
   25,00 €      ➔   fünfundzwanzig Euro                "twenty-five Euro"
    1,05 €      ➔   ein Euro fünf                      "one Euro five"
```

*Listening tip:* Because German numbers 21–99 are spoken backwards (Chapter 1.5), listen carefully to the ending of the cent amount (*achtundsiebzig* = 78).

---

## 4. Useful Shopping Phrases: Cheap vs. Expensive

* **günstig / preiswert** (affordable / good value)
  * *„Das ist sehr günstig!“* (That is very affordable!)
* **teuer** (expensive)
  * *„Das ist mir zu teuer.“* (That is too expensive for me.)
* **im Angebot** (on sale / special offer)
  * *„Die Erdbeeren sind heute im Angebot.“* (Strawberries are on sale today.)
* **zusammen** (altogether / total)
  * *„Das macht zusammen 18 Euro fünfzig.“* (That comes to 18.50 € altogether.)
