# 5.2 Food & Drinks (Essen und Trinken)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can name everyday food items with their articles and plurals (*Obst, Gemüse, Fleisch, Milchprodukte*).
* I can name the three main meals of the day (*Frühstück, Mittagessen, Abendbrot*).
* I understand the traditional German meal concept of **das Abendbrot**.
* I can specify my water preference in Germany: with gas (**mit Kohlensäure / Sprudel**) or still (**ohne Kohlensäure / stilles Wasser**).

---

## 1. The Three Meals of the Day (Die Mahlzeiten)

In Germany, daily meals follow this pattern:

| Meal | Noun & Gender | Everyday Verb | What do people eat? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Breakfast** | **das Frühstück** | *frühstücken* (to have breakfast) | Brötchen, Butter, Marmelade, Kaffee |
| **Lunch** | **das Mittagessen**| *zu Mittag essen* | Warme Mahlzeit (Fleisch, Pasta, Gemüse) |
| **Dinner** | **das Abendessen** / **das Abendbrot** | *zu Abend essen* | Brot, Käse, Wurst, Salat |

### 🍞 The Culture of "Abendbrot"
Notice that dinner is often called **das Abendbrot** (literally: "evening bread"). 
In traditional German households, lunch is the warm meal of the day. Dinner is often a simple cold meal consisting of slices of fresh bread (*Brot*), butter, cheese (*Käse*), cold cuts (*Wurst*), and pickles.

### 5. Saying what you (don't) eat
Important in canteens, restaurants and when you are invited:
* *„Ich bin Vegetarier / Vegetarierin.“* · *„Ich esse vegan.“* · *„Ich esse kein Schweinefleisch.“*
* *„Ich habe eine Allergie gegen Nüsse.“* · *„Ich vertrage keine Milch.“* (I can't tolerate milk.)
* *„Ist da Fleisch / Alkohol / Ei drin?“* (Is there … in it?) · *„Ist das scharf?“* (Is it spicy?)

## 3. Drinks & The Big German Water Question

When you order water in Germany, you will often be asked about bubbles:

```text
„Wasser mit oder ohne Gas?“
• mit Kohlensäure / mit Sprudel  ➔  Sparkling / fizzy water (Default in Germany!)
• ohne Kohlensäure / stilles Wasser  ➔  Still water (no bubbles)
```

⚠️ **Expat Warning:** If you simply ask for *"ein Wasser"*, you will often get **sparkling water** (*mit Kohlensäure*). If you want still bottled water, say:
> *„Ein stilles Wasser, bitte.“*

Tap water is *Leitungswasser*. Restaurants usually serve bottled water; you can ask *„Kann ich bitte ein Glas Leitungswasser haben?“* – some places serve it, some don't.

### Other Everyday Drinks:
* **der Kaffee** (die Kaffees) — *coffee*
* **der Tee** (die Tees) — *tea*
* **der Saft** (die Säfte) — *juice* (e.g. *der Orangensaft, der Apfelsaft*)
* **das Bier** (die Biere) — *beer*
* **der Wein** (die Weine) — *wine* (e.g. *der Rotwein, der Weißwein*)
