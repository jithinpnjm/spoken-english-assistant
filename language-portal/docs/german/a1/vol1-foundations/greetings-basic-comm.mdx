# 1.3 Greetings & Basic Communication (Begrüßung und Kommunikation)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can greet people appropriately depending on the time of day and the relationship.
* I can say goodbye in person and know the special phrase used on the telephone.
* I can use the multi-purpose word *bitte* correctly in its different situations.
* I can use essential "survival phrases" when a native speaker speaks too quickly.
* I can confidently conduct a simple greeting and payment interaction in a shop.

---

## 1. Greetings: Formal, Informal & Time of Day

In Germany, how you greet someone depends on **who you are talking to** and **what time of day it is**.

### General Everyday Greetings
* **Hallo!** — *Hello!* (Friendly, universal among friends, colleagues, and in casual shops).
* **Guten Morgen.** — *Good morning.* (Used until about 10:00 or 11:00 AM).
* **Guten Tag.** — *Good day / Hello.* (The standard formal daytime greeting from late morning until around 18:00).
* **Guten Abend.** — *Good evening.* (Used after about 18:00).

### Regional Greetings You Will Hear
German-speaking regions have distinct local greetings that you will immediately encounter:
* **Servus!** or **Grüß Gott!** — Common in Bavaria, southern Germany, and Austria.
* **Moin!** (or *Moin Moin!*) — Used in northern Germany at **any** time of day or night.

---

## 2. Saying Goodbye: In Person vs. On the Phone

German has a unique distinction between seeing someone and hearing someone when saying goodbye:

| Situation | Phrase | Literal Meaning | How it is used |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **In person (Formal)** | **Auf Wiedersehen.** | "Until we see each other again" | With strangers, officials, doctors, clients. |
| **In person (Casual)** | **Tschüss!** | "Bye!" | Universal with friends, colleagues, in shops. |
| **In person (Casual)** | **Bis bald!** | "See you soon!" | When you expect to see someone again soon. |
| **On the phone (Formal)** | **Auf Wiederhören.** | "Until we hear each other again" | **Only on the telephone!** (Do not say this in person). |

---

## 3. The Multi-Talented Word: "Bitte"

Beginners often wonder why Germans say *bitte* so often. The word *bitte* changes its English meaning depending entirely on where and how it is used:

1. **"Please"** (when asking for something):
   * *„Einen Kaffee, **bitte**.“* (A coffee, please.)
2. **"You're welcome"** (in response to *Danke*):
   * Colleague: *„Danke schön!“* ➔ You: *„**Bitte** schön!“* (or simply *„Bitte!“*)
3. **"Here you go"** (when handing an object or change to someone):
   * Cashier: *„Fünf Euro, **bitte**.“* (Hands you your change).
4. **"Pardon? / What did you say?"** (with rising question tone):
   * *„**Wie bitte?**“* (Excuse me? / Could you repeat that?)

## 4. The Essential "Survival Phrases"

When you live or travel in Germany, native speakers will naturally speak at normal, fast speed. **Never pretend you understood when you didn't.** Germans appreciate directness and are happy to slow down if you ask clearly:

* **Entschuldigung.** [Syllables: Ent-schul-di-gung] — *Excuse me / Sorry.*
* **Wie bitte?** — *Pardon? / What was that?* (Polite and natural).
* **Langsamer, bitte.** — *More slowly, please.*
* **Noch einmal, bitte.** — *Once again, please.*
* **Ich verstehe nicht.** — *I don't understand.*
* **Sprechen Sie Englisch?** — *Do you speak English?* (Formal).

## 5. Real-Life Germany: The Bakery & Checkout Ritual

In German bakeries (*Bäckerei*) and supermarkets, transactions follow a predictable, polite ritual:

1. **Greeting upon entering / reaching the counter:**
   * Customer: *„Guten Morgen!“*
   * Staff: *„Guten Morgen! Was darf es sein?“* (Good morning! What can I get for you?)
2. **Ordering:**
   * Customer: *„Ich möchte bitte zwei Brezeln.“* (I would like two pretzels, please.)
3. **Total & Payment:**
   * Staff: *„Das macht zwei Euro vierzig, bitte.“*
   * Customer: *„Hier, bitte.“* (Hands the money).
4. **The Departure Wish:**
   * Staff: *„Schönen Tag noch!“* (Have a nice day!)
   * Customer: *„Danke, gleichfalls! Tschüss!“* (*Gleichfalls* = likewise / you too!).
