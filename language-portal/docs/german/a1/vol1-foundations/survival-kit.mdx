# 1.0 Survival Kit: Your First 30 Sentences (Überlebensdeutsch)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can say 30 useful German sentences for my first weeks in Germany.
* I can keep a conversation going when I don't understand (*Wie bitte? Langsamer, bitte.*).
* I can handle the first seconds of a shop, an appointment, a doctor's reception, a bus stop and a Kita door.
* I can change one word in a sentence to make a new sentence (*Ich brauche **einen Termin** → Ich brauche **ein Ticket***).

---

## 1. Why start with whole sentences?

Grammar explains *why* German works. But in your first week you need to buy a ticket, not analyse one.
So this chapter treats each sentence as a **block** you learn by heart – like a key that opens a door.
Later chapters open the block and show you the grammar inside. You will recognise these sentences
again and again.

**How to learn a block:**
1. Read the German and the English.
2. Say it aloud three times (stress is on the **bold** syllable where shown).
3. Cover the German, look at the English, and say it from memory.
4. Change the underlined part to make a new sentence that is true for *you*.

## 2. The 30 sentences

### A. Being polite (every situation)
| # | German | English | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Guten Tag.** / **Hallo.** | Hello. | *Guten Tag* in offices and shops, *Hallo* with people you know or in casual places. |
| 2 | **Danke.** – **Bitte.** | Thank you. – You're welcome. | *Bitte* also means "please" and "here you are". |
| 3 | **Entschuldigung.** | Excuse me. / Sorry. | Use it to get attention *and* to apologise. |
| 4 | **Auf Wiedersehen.** / **Tschüss.** | Goodbye. / Bye. | On the phone: *Auf Wiederhören.* |

### B. When you don't understand (your most important sentences)
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 | **Wie bitte?** | Sorry? / Pardon? |
| 6 | **Ich verstehe das nicht.** | I don't understand that. |
| 7 | **Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?** | Can you repeat that, please? |
| 8 | **Langsamer, bitte.** | More slowly, please. |
| 9 | **Sprechen Sie Englisch?** | Do you speak English? |
| 10 | **Ich lerne noch Deutsch.** | I'm still learning German. |
| 11 | **Einen Moment, bitte.** | One moment, please. |
| 12 | **Wie sagt man … auf Deutsch?** | How do you say … in German? |

Sentence 10 is a small superpower: once people hear it, most will speak more slowly and use simpler words.

### C. About you
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 13 | **Ich heiße** <u>Jithin</u>. / **Mein Name ist** <u>Jithin Joseph</u>. | My name is … |
| 14 | **Ich komme aus** <u>Indien</u>. | I come from … |
| 15 | **Ich wohne in** <u>Frankfurt</u>. | I live in … |
| 16 | **Meine Telefonnummer ist** <u>0176 …</u> | My phone number is … (say the digits one by one) |

### D. Shops, bakery, café
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 17 | **Ich möchte** <u>zwei Brötchen</u>, **bitte.** | I'd like two bread rolls, please. |
| 18 | **Was kostet das?** | How much is that? |
| 19 | **Mit Karte, bitte.** / **Bar, bitte.** | By card, please. / Cash, please. |
| 20 | **Eine Tüte, bitte.** / **Keine Tüte, danke.** | A bag, please. / No bag, thanks. |

### E. Appointments and offices
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 21 | **Ich brauche einen Termin.** | I need an appointment. |
| 22 | **Ich habe um** <u>zehn Uhr</u> **einen Termin.** | I have an appointment at ten. |
| 23 | **Ich kann heute leider nicht kommen.** | Unfortunately I can't come today. |

### F. Doctor and pharmacy
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 24 | **Ich bin krank.** / **Mein Kind ist krank.** | I am ill. / My child is ill. |
| 25 | **Ich habe** <u>Fieber</u>. / **Ich habe Schmerzen.** | I have a fever. / I'm in pain. |
| 26 | **Haben Sie heute einen Termin frei?** | Do you have a free appointment today? |

### G. Getting around
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 27 | **Wo ist** <u>der Bahnhof</u>? | Where is the station? |
| 28 | **Fährt dieser Bus zum** <u>Hauptbahnhof</u>? | Does this bus go to the main station? |

### H. Kita and family
| # | German | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 29 | **Ich hole** <u>meine Tochter</u> **um** <u>15 Uhr</u> **ab.** | I'll pick up my daughter at 3 pm. |
| 30 | **Gibt es heute etwas Wichtiges?** | Is there anything important today? |

:::caution Emergency
Emergency (ambulance, fire): **112**. Police: **110**. Say: *„Ich brauche Hilfe. Die Adresse ist …“* (I need help.
The address is …). More in [9.14 Emergency Situations](../vol9-roleplay/emergency-situations.mdx).
:::

## 3. Three things to notice (just enough grammar)

1. **Sie** (capital S) is the polite "you" for strangers, offices, shops, doctors. *Können **Sie** …?* You will learn
   the informal *du* in [1.4](./personal-pronouns.mdx). Starting with *Sie* is the safe choice.
2. **The verb likes position 2.** *Ich **habe** um zehn Uhr einen Termin.* Even when the time comes first:
   *Um zehn Uhr **habe** ich einen Termin.* Full explanation in [3.7](../vol3-grammar/sentence-structure-v2.mdx).
3. **Second verb at the end.** *Ich **kann** heute leider nicht **kommen**.* *Können Sie das bitte **wiederholen**?*
   This "bracket" is explained in [3.6 Modal Verbs](../vol3-grammar/modal-verbs.mdx).

## 4. Common beginner mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Ich habe ein Termin.* | *Ich habe **einen** Termin.* | *der Termin* is masculine; after *haben* it becomes *einen*. Learn sentence 21 as a block. |
| *Ich komme nicht heute.* | *Ich kann heute **nicht** kommen.* | German says "I can not come today"; *nicht* goes before the second verb. |
| *Ich nicht verstehe.* | *Ich verstehe das **nicht**.* | The verb stays in position 2; *nicht* comes later. |
| *Können Sie wiederholen das?* | *Können Sie das **wiederholen**?* | The second verb goes to the end. |
| *Ich bin Fieber.* | *Ich **habe** Fieber.* | Symptoms use *haben* ("I have fever"). |

## 5. Guided practice

### Step 1: Which sentence do you need?
1. The receptionist speaks very fast.
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Langsamer, bitte.“* or *„Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?“* – and if needed *„Ich lerne noch Deutsch.“*
</details>

2. You want to know the price of a coffee.
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Was kostet das?“*
</details>

3. Your son has a fever and you call the doctor's practice.
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Guten Tag. Mein Kind ist krank. Es hat Fieber. Haben Sie heute einen Termin frei?“*
</details>

### Step 2: Change one piece (controlled production)
Replace the underlined part to make a new, correct sentence.

4. *Ich möchte <u>zwei Brötchen</u>, bitte.* → you want **a coffee** (*einen Kaffee*).
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Ich möchte einen Kaffee, bitte.“*
</details>

5. *Wo ist <u>der Bahnhof</u>?* → you are looking for **the pharmacy** (*die Apotheke*).
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Wo ist die Apotheke?“*
</details>

6. *Ich habe um <u>zehn Uhr</u> einen Termin.* → your appointment is at **half past two** (*halb drei*).
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Ich habe um halb drei einen Termin.“* (*halb drei* = 2:30 – halfway **to** three. See [1.5](./numbers-dates-time.mdx).)
</details>

### Step 3: Fix the sentence, then say the correct version aloud
7. *Ich habe ein Termin um neun Uhr.*
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Ich habe um neun Uhr **einen** Termin.“* – *einen* because *Termin* is masculine. (*Ich habe einen Termin um neun Uhr* is also fine – the important fix is *einen*.) Now say it twice.
</details>

8. *Ich komme nicht heute, ich bin krank.*
<details>
  <summary>Show answer</summary>

  *„Ich **kann** heute **nicht kommen**. Ich bin krank.“* Now say it twice.
</details>

### Step 4: Your own survival card (independent production)
Write five sentences that are true for you: name, country, city, phone number, and one sentence you will need
**this week** (a shop, an appointment, a Kita pick-up …). Read them aloud until you can say them without looking.

## 6. Real life: two mini-scenes

**At the doctor's reception**
> **Empfang:** Guten Morgen, was kann ich für Sie tun?  
> **Sie:** Guten Morgen. Ich bin krank. Haben Sie heute einen Termin frei?  
> **Empfang:** Um 11:30 Uhr. Ihre Versichertenkarte, bitte.  
> **Sie:** Wie bitte? Langsamer, bitte.  
> **Empfang:** Ihre Ver-si-cher-ten-kar-te, bitte. Ihre Karte von der Krankenkasse.  
> **Sie:** Ah, einen Moment, bitte … Hier, bitte.

**At the Kita door**
> **Erzieherin:** Hallo! Wer holt Maya heute ab?  
> **Sie:** Ich hole Maya um 15 Uhr ab.  
> **Sie:** Gibt es heute etwas Wichtiges?  
> **Erzieherin:** Morgen ist Ausflug. Maya braucht Gummistiefel.  
> **Sie:** Entschuldigung, ich verstehe das nicht. Was ist „Gummistiefel“?  
> **Erzieherin:** Rain boots.  
> **Sie:** Ah, danke!

Notice how the "not understanding" sentences keep both conversations going.
