# 1.4 Personal Pronouns & "Du" vs. "Sie" (Personalpronomen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can use all German personal pronouns (*ich, du, er, sie, es, wir, ihr, sie, Sie*).
* I know when to address someone with informal *du* and when to use formal *Sie*.
* I can address a group of friends with *ihr* and a group of strangers with *Sie*.
* I understand why German objects can be called *er* (he) or *sie* (she), not just *es* (it).

---

## 1. What are Personal Pronouns and Why Do We Need Them?

A **pronoun** is a small word that replaces a person's name or an object so you don't have to repeat the same noun over and over:
* Instead of: *"Thomas is here. Thomas is hungry. Thomas drinks coffee."*
* You say: *"Thomas is here. **He** is hungry. **He** drinks coffee."*

In German, personal pronouns do two critical jobs:
1. They tell you **who** is doing the action (*ich*, *du*, *wir*...).
2. They establish the **social relationship** (closeness vs. formal respect).

---

## 2. The Complete Pronoun Chart

German has 9 personal pronouns, divided into singular (one person) and plural (more than one):

| Pronoun | English Equivalent | Social Register | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | I | Neutral | *Ich bin Jithin.* (I am Jithin.) |
| **du** | you *(one person)* | **Informal** | *Wer bist du?* (Who are you?) |
| **er** | he | Neutral | *Das ist Lukas. Er kommt aus Berlin.* |
| **sie** | she | Neutral | *Das ist Maria. Sie lernt Deutsch.* |
| **es** | it | Neutral | *Das ist ein Buch. Es ist neu.* |
| **wir** | we | Neutral | *Wir wohnen in Deutschland.* |
| **ihr** | you all / y'all *(group)* | **Informal plural** | *Woher kommt ihr?* (Where are you guys from?) |
| **sie** | they | Neutral plural | *Das sind meine Freunde. Sie spielen Tennis.* |
| **Sie** | You *(one or many)* | **Formal** (Always capitalized!) | *Woher kommen Sie?* (Where do you come from?) |

---

## 3. The Golden Social Rule: "Duzen" vs. "Siezen"

English has only one word for "you". German has **three different words** for "you":
* **du** ➔ Talking to **one** friend, child, family member, or classmate.
* **ihr** ➔ Talking to **two or more** friends, children, or family members.
* **Sie** ➔ Talking to **one or more adults** you do not know well (colleagues, bosses, doctors, officials, shopkeepers). **It is ALWAYS capitalized.**

### Who Decides to Switch to "du"?
In German society, switching from formal *Sie* to informal *du* is a meaningful step called **das Du anbieten** (offering the "du"):
* By convention, **the person with higher authority or the older person** usually offers the *du* (many workplaces, especially start-ups, use *du* from day one):
  * Boss: *„Wir können gerne ‚du‘ sagen. Ich bin Thomas.“* (We can gladly say 'du'.)
* **The Golden Rule for Expats:** When in doubt, **always start with *Sie***. Starting with *Sie* is polite; starting with *du* with a stranger or official can sound disrespectful.

---

## 4. The "Er/Sie/Es" Trap with Objects

In English, people are *he* or *she*, and all objects are *it*:
* *"Where is the table? **It** is in the room."*

In German, **pronouns match the grammatical gender of the noun**, even for inanimate objects!
* **der Tisch** (the table, masculine) ➔ **er**
  * *„Wo ist der Tisch?“* — *„**Er** steht im Wohnzimmer.“* (Literally: "He stands in the living room.")
* **die Lampe** (the lamp, feminine) ➔ **sie**
  * *„Wo ist die Lampe?“* — *„**Sie** ist kaputt.“* (Literally: "She is broken.")
* **das Buch** (the book, neutral) ➔ **es**
  * *„Wo ist das Buch?“* — *„**Es** liegt hier.“* ("It lies here.")

Whenever you want to replace a noun with a pronoun, ask yourself: Was the noun *der* (➔ *er*), *die* (➔ *sie*), or *das* (➔ *es*)?

---

## 5. Pairing Pronouns with High-Frequency Verbs

At A1, two verbs appear constantly with these pronouns: **sein** (to be) and **heißen** (to be called):

| Pronoun | sein (to be) | heißen (to be called) | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | bin | heiße | *Ich bin Maria.* |
| **du** | bist | heißt | *Du bist mein Freund.* |
| **er / sie / es** | ist | heißt | *Er ist Arzt.* / *Sie heißt Anna.* |
| **wir** | sind | heißen | *Wir sind hier.* |
| **ihr** | seid | heißt | *Ihr seid spät!* |
| **sie / Sie** | sind | heißen | *Sie sind Herr Weber, richtig?* |
