# 1.5 Numbers, Dates & Time (Zahlen, Daten und Uhrzeit)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can count from 0 to 100 in German and understand prices and phone numbers.
* I can understand the official 24-hour clock (trains, appointments) and the casual 12-hour clock (*halb*, *Viertel*).
* I know the days of the week, months, and seasons.
* I can use the three golden time prepositions: **um** (time), **am** (days), and **im** (months/seasons).
* I can schedule, confirm, or reschedule an appointment in German.

---

## 1. Numbers (0 to 100) & The "Backwards" Rule

### Numbers 0 to 20
* **0–10:** null (0), eins (1), zwei (2), drei (3), vier (4), fünf (5), sechs (6), sieben (7), acht (8), neun (9), zehn (10)
* **11–12 (Irregular):** **elf** (11), **zwölf** (12)
* **13–19 (Number + zehn):** dreizehn (13), vierzehn (14), fünfzehn (15), **sechzehn** (16 — drops the 's'), **siebzehn** (17 — drops the 'en'), achtzehn (18), neunzehn (19)

### The Tens (20, 30, 40...)
* zwanzig (20), **dreißig** (30 — spelled with ß!), vierzig (40), fünfzig (50), sechzig (60), siebzig (70), achtzig (80), neunzig (90), **hundert** (100)

### The German "Backwards" Rule (21 to 99)
In English, you say the tens first, then the ones: *"twenty-one"*.
**German does the opposite: you say the ones first, then "und" (and), then the tens.**

* 21 = **ein**und**zwanzig** *(literally: "one-and-twenty")*
* 35 = **fünf**und**dreißig** *(literally: "five-and-thirty")*
* 64 = **vier**und**sechzig**
* 99 = **neun**und**neunzig**

*Listening tip for prices:* When a cashier says *„siebenundvierzig Euro“*, the **last** part (*vierzig*) tells you whether the price is in the 40s or the 70s – wait for it before you reach for your wallet!

### Phone numbers
Phone numbers are usually given digit by digit or in pairs: *0176 23 45 81* → *null-eins-sieben-sechs, dreiundzwanzig,
fünfundvierzig, einundachtzig*. On the phone many people say **zwo** instead of *zwei* so it isn't confused with *drei*.
If the pairs are too fast, ask: *„Bitte einzeln, Ziffer für Ziffer.“* (One at a time, digit by digit.)

## 2. Telling Time: Official (24h) vs. Informal (12h)

Germany uses two different systems for time. You need the official clock to read schedules and the informal clock to speak with friends.

### A. Official Time (The 24-Hour Clock)
Used for trains, flights, opening hours, business meetings, and doctor's appointments. 
**Pattern:** [Hour] + **Uhr** + [Minutes]
* 14:30 = *vierzehn Uhr dreißig*
* 08:15 = *acht Uhr fünfzehn*
* 19:45 = *neunzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig*

### B. Informal Spoken Time (The 12-Hour Clock)
Used in everyday conversations with friends and family. It relies on fractions of the hour:

* **Viertel nach** = *Quarter past*
  * 08:15 = **Viertel nach acht**
* **Viertel vor** = *Quarter to*
  * 08:45 = **Viertel vor neun**
* **Halb** = *Half to (30 minutes BEFORE the next hour)*
  * ⚠️ **The Big German Trap:** In English, "half three" often means 3:30. In German, **halb** always looks *forward* to the coming hour!
  * 02:30 = **halb drei** *(Halfway to three!)*
  * 07:30 = **halb acht** *(Halfway to eight!)*
* **nach / vor** with minutes: 08:10 = **zehn nach acht**, 08:50 = **zehn vor neun**
* Around *halb* many people count from the half hour: 08:25 = **fünf vor halb neun**, 08:35 = **fünf nach halb neun**
  (you only need to *understand* these at A1; *acht Uhr fünfundzwanzig* is always correct).

### Asking the time vs. asking "when?"
* *„Wie spät ist es?“* / *„Wie viel Uhr ist es?“* – *„Es ist halb drei.“* (What time is it now?)
* *„Wann beginnt der Kurs?“* – *„**Um** neun Uhr.“* (At what time does something happen?)
* *„Wie lange ist die Praxis offen?“* – *„**Von** acht **bis** zwölf Uhr.“* (from … to …)

⚠️ **die Uhr vs. die Stunde:** *Uhr* is the clock time (*um drei Uhr* = at three o'clock); *Stunde* is a duration
(*drei Stunden* = three hours). ❌ *Der Kurs dauert zwei Uhr.* → ✅ *Der Kurs dauert zwei Stunden.*

## 3. Days, Months & The Three Golden Prepositions

Don't guess which preposition to use with time. Memorize this 3-rule formula:

```text
UM  ➔  Specific clock times          (um 14:30 Uhr / um halb drei)
AM  ➔  Days, dates, parts of days    (am Montag / am Wochenende / am Morgen)
IM  ➔  Months and seasons             (im August / im Sommer / im Winter)
```

**One exception to remember:** *am Morgen, am Vormittag, am Mittag, am Nachmittag, am Abend* – but **in der Nacht** (at night).

### Days of the Week (Die Wochentage) — use "am"
* **Montag** (Monday), **Dienstag** (Tuesday), **Mittwoch** (Wednesday), **Donnerstag** (Thursday), **Freitag** (Friday), **Samstag** (Saturday), **Sonntag** (Sunday)
* *am Wochenende* = on the weekend

### Months (Die Monate) & Seasons (Die Jahreszeiten) — use "im"
* **Months:** Januar, Februar, März, April, Mai, Juni, Juli, August, September, Oktober, November, Dezember.
* **Seasons:** der Frühling (spring), der Sommer (summer), der Herbst (autumn), der Winter (winter).

All days, months and seasons are **masculine** (*der Montag, der Mai, der Sommer*) – that is why they share *am* (*an dem*)
and *im* (*in dem*).

### Time words you need with articles and plurals

| Word | Plural | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **die Uhr** | die Uhren | clock / o'clock | *Es ist drei Uhr.* |
| **die Stunde** | die Stunden | hour (duration) | *Ich arbeite acht Stunden.* |
| **die Minute** | die Minuten | minute | *Der Bus kommt in fünf Minuten.* |
| **der Tag** | die Tage | day | *Ich bin drei Tage in Berlin.* |
| **die Woche** | die Wochen | week | *Der Kurs dauert zehn Wochen.* |
| **der Monat** | die Monate | month | *Ich wohne seit zwei Monaten hier.* |
| **das Jahr** | die Jahre | year | *Ich bin 30 Jahre alt.* |
| **das Wochenende** | die Wochenenden | weekend | *Am Wochenende habe ich frei.* |
| **der Termin** | die Termine | appointment | *Ich habe am Montag einen Termin.* |
| **die Sprechzeiten** (pl.) | — | office hours (doctor, office) | *Die Sprechzeiten stehen an der Tür.* |
| **die Öffnungszeiten** (pl.) | — | opening hours (shops) | *Was sind die Öffnungszeiten?* |

*Examples in action:*
* *Ich habe **am** Freitag **um** 10 Uhr einen Termin.* (I have an appointment on Friday at 10 AM.)
* *Mein Geburtstag ist **im** Juli.* (My birthday is in July.)

## 4. Real-Life Germany: Dates & Doctor's Hours

### 1. How Germans Write Dates
In Germany, dates are written in the order **Day . Month . Year** (DD.MM.YYYY):
* *14.05.2025* = May 14th, 2025 (not October 5th!).
* When spoken with ordinal numbers: *„am vierzehnten Mai“* (on the 14th of May).

### 2. Reading Doctor's "Sprechzeiten" (Office Hours)
On the front door of medical practices, you will see schedules like:
> **Mo, Di, Do:** 08:00 – 12:00 Uhr  
> **Mi, Fr:** 08:00 – 11:30 Uhr  
> **Mo, Do:** 15:00 – 18:00 Uhr

Remember that 15:00 to 18:00 is afternoon office hours (3 PM to 6 PM)!
