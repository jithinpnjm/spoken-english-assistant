# 3.1 Nouns & Gender (Nomen und Artikel)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I understand that EVERY noun in German begins with a capital letter.
* I know the three grammatical genders: masculine (**der**), feminine (**die**), and neutral (**das**).
* I can use the indefinite articles **ein** (a/an for *der* and *das*) and **eine** (for *die*).
* I know that grammatical gender belongs to the *word*, not the physical object.
* I can use common word endings (like *-ung* or *-chen*) to guess the gender of unfamiliar words.

---

## 1. What is a Noun, and Why Does Every Noun Have a Gender?

A **noun** is a naming word for a person, place, object, or idea:
* *man, woman, table, water, house, city, time*

In English, the word "the" works for everything (*the man, the woman, the table*). 
**German divides all nouns into three grammatical categories ("teams")**:
* Masculine ➔ **der**
* Feminine ➔ **die**
* Neuter ➔ **das**

### The Mental Model: Gender Belongs to the Word, Not the Object
Beginners often assume a masculine noun must look "manly", or a feminine noun must be "girly". 
**In German, gender is grammatical, not biological.**
* A table (*der Tisch*) is grammatically masculine.
* A door (*die Tür*) is grammatically feminine.
* A window (*das Fenster*) is grammatically neutral.

Even biological people can have surprising grammatical genders:
* **das Mädchen** (the girl) is grammatically **neutral** because of its word ending (*-chen*), even though the person is biologically female!

*The Golden Rule for Beginners:* **Never learn a German noun by itself.** Never write down *Tisch = table*. Always write down the article together with the noun: **der Tisch**.

---

## 2. The Universal Rule: Capitalize Every Noun!

In English, you only capitalize proper names and the first word of a sentence.
**In German, EVERY SINGLE NOUN is capitalized, anywhere it appears in a sentence:**

* ❌ *ich habe ein buch und einen apfel.*
* ✅ *Ich habe ein **B**uch und einen **A**pfel.*

If you forget to capitalize a noun in a German writing test or official email, it is marked as a spelling mistake.

---

## 3. "The" (der, die, das) vs. "A / An" (ein, eine)

When you speak about a specific object, you use the **definite article** (*the*). When you speak about any general object, you use the **indefinite article** (*a / an*):

| Gender | Definite ("The") | Indefinite ("A / An") | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine (m)** | **der** | **ein** | *Das ist **der** Tisch. / Das ist **ein** Tisch.* |
| **Feminine (f)** | **die** | **eine** | *Das ist **die** Lampe. / Das ist **eine** Lampe.* |
| **Neutral (n)** | **das** | **ein** | *Das ist **das** Buch. / Das ist **ein** Buch.* |

*Notice the pattern:* Masculine (*der*) and Neutral (*das*) both share the exact same word: **ein**. Only feminine (*die*) adds the ending to become **eine**.

---

## 4. Cheat Codes: How to Spot Gender from Word Endings

While many nouns must simply be memorized with their article, certain word endings give away the gender with near-100% accuracy:

### Always DIE (Feminine):
* Words ending in **-ung:** *die Wohn**ung*** (apartment), *die Rechn**ung*** (bill/invoice), *die Zeit**ung*** (newspaper)
* Words ending in **-heit / -keit:** *die Krank**heit*** (illness), *die Möglich**keit*** (possibility)
* Words ending in **-schaft:** *die Freund**schaft*** (friendship), *die Nachbar**schaft*** (neighborhood)
* Words ending in **-tion:** *die Informa**tion***, *die Sta**tion***

### Always DAS (Neutral):
* Diminutive words ending in **-chen / -lein** (meaning "little"): *das Mäd**chen*** (girl), *das Bröt**chen*** (bread roll)
* Foreign words ending in **-ment:** *das Doku**ment***, *das Medika**ment*** (medicine)

### Usually DER (Masculine):
* Male professions and words ending in **-er:** *der Lehr**er*** (teacher), *der Comput**er***, *der Druck**er*** (printer)
* Days, months, and seasons: *der Montag, der Mai, der Sommer*

---

## 5. Real-Life Germany: Reading Dictionaries (m, f, n)

When you look up a German word on Leo.org, dict.cc, or in a print dictionary, the entries do not write *der, die, das*. Instead, they use single letters:
* **m** = *maskulin* ➔ **der**
* **f** = *feminin* ➔ **die**
* **n** = *neutral* ➔ **das**

*Example:* `Schlüssel, m` means the word is **der Schlüssel** (the key).
