# 3.11 Negation: "nicht" vs. "kein" (Die Verneinung)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I know a clear decision rule for when to use **kein** and when to use **nicht**.
* I can negate nouns using the correct form of *kein, keine, keinen*.
* I can negate verbs, adjectives, and entire sentences using *nicht*.
* I know where to place **nicht** in a German sentence.

---

## 1. The Big Beginner Dilemma: "nicht" or "kein"?

In English, you can say "not" or "no":
* *"I do not swim."*
* *"I have no time."*

In German, beginners frequently use the wrong word because they translate word-for-word. 
Here is a **simple mental model** that solves almost every A1 case:

```text
Are you negating a NOUN that would take "ein/eine" or has no article?
      YES  ➔  Use KEIN  (kein, keine, keinen)
      NO   ➔  Use NICHT (for verbs, adjectives, specific nouns, everything else!)
```

---

## 2. When to Use "kein" (Negating Nouns)

Think of **kein** as the opposite of **ein**. It is an article that attaches directly to a noun.
If you could put *ein* in the positive sentence, you MUST put *kein* in the negative sentence:

* Positive: *Ich habe **ein** Auto.* ➔ Negative: *Ich habe **kein** Auto.*
* Positive: *Das ist **eine** Lampe.* ➔ Negative: *Das ist **keine** Lampe.*
* Positive: *Er hat **einen** Bruder.* (Accusative) ➔ Negative: *Er hat **keinen** Bruder.*

### Nouns with NO Article in the Plural
Because *ein* does not exist in the plural, you also use **keine** to negate plural nouns:
* *Wir haben Kinder.* ➔ *Wir haben **keine** Kinder.*
* *Hier gibt es Bananen.* ➔ *Hier gibt es **keine** Bananen.*

### Uncountable Nouns (Time, Hunger, Money, Milk)
* *Ich habe Zeit.* ➔ *Ich habe **keine** Zeit.*
* *Ich habe Hunger.* ➔ *Ich habe **keinen** Hunger.*
* *Wir haben Geld.* ➔ *Wir haben **kein** Geld.*

---

## 3. When to Use "nicht" (Verbs, Adjectives & Everything Else)

Use **nicht** whenever you are NOT negating a general noun:

### A. Negating an Action / Verb:
* *Ich schwimme **nicht**.* (I do not swim.)
* *Er arbeitet heute **nicht**.* (He is not working today.)

### B. Negating an Adjective or Adverb:
* *Das Auto ist **nicht** teuer.* (The car is not expensive.)
* *Sie spricht **nicht** schnell.* (She does not speak fast.)

### C. Negating a SPECIFIC Noun (with *der/die/das* or *mein*):
If the noun has a definite article or a possessive word, you cannot use *kein*. You must use **nicht**:
* *Das ist **nicht der** Chef.* (That is not *the* boss — specific person!).
* *Das ist **nicht mein** Schlüssel.* (That is not *my* key!).

---

## 4. Where Does "nicht" Go in a Sentence?

Where you place *nicht* depends on what you want to negate:

1. **At the very end of a simple sentence** (negating the action):
   * *Ich komme heute **nicht**.*
   * *Er schläft **nicht**.*
2. **Directly before the adjective or adverb**:
   * *Das Wetter ist **nicht** schön.*
   * *Ich esse **nicht** gern Fleisch.*
3. **Directly before a prepositional phrase**:
   * *Ich wohne **nicht** in Berlin.* (I don't live in Berlin.)
   * *Der Zug fährt **nicht** nach München.*
4. **Before the second verb in a bracket** (modal/separable verbs):
   * *Ich kann heute **nicht** kommen.*
   * *Er steht heute **nicht** auf.*
