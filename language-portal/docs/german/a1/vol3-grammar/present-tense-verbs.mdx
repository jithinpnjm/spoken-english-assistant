# 3.4 Present Tense Verbs (Verben im Präsens)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can find the **stem** of a verb and add the right ending for *ich, du, er/sie/es, wir, ihr, sie/Sie*.
* I know the three small spelling rules (extra *-e-* after *d/t*; only *-t* for *du* after *s/ß/z*).
* I know the most common verbs that change their vowel for *du* and *er/sie/es* (*sprechen → spricht, fahren → fährt, nehmen → nimmt*).
* I can use the present tense for the near future with a time word (*Ich komme morgen.*).

To talk about what you do, what you are doing, or even what you *will* do, you need the present tense. German verbs change their endings depending on who is doing the action (I, you, he, we). English only does this once (*I work – he work**s***); German does it for every person, because the ending tells the listener **who** is acting.

---

## 1. At the Office: Talking about the Weekend

Imagine you are standing at the coffee machine on Monday morning. You and your colleague are talking about what you normally do on the weekend.

**Anna:** Was **machst** du am Wochenende, Max? *(What do you do on the weekend, Max?)*
**Max:** Ich **spiele** am Samstag Fußball. Und du? Was **machst** du? *(I play football on Saturday. And you? What do you do?)*
**Anna:** Ich **arbeite** meistens am Samstag. Aber am Sonntag **schlafe** ich lange. *(I mostly work on Saturday. But on Sunday I sleep late.)*
**Max:** **Fährst** du am Sonntag zu deinen Eltern? *(Are you driving to your parents on Sunday?)*
**Anna:** Nein, wir **bleiben** zu Hause. *(No, we are staying home.)*

Notice how the verbs change depending on who is speaking: *ich spiele*, but *wir bleiben*.

## 2. How German Verbs Work

Almost every German verb in the dictionary (the **Infinitive**) ends in **-en** or **-n** (e.g., *spielen, machen, kommen*).

To use the verb, you chop off the *-en*. What is left is called the **Stem**.
*   *spielen* (to play) -> Stem: **spiel-**
*   *machen* (to do/make) -> Stem: **mach-**

You then add a specific ending to the stem based on the pronoun. 
*(Memorize this pattern: **e, st, t, en, t, en**)*.

| Pronoun | Ending | Example: *spielen* (to play) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **-e** | ich spiel**e** |
| **du** | **-st** | du spiel**st** |
| **er/sie/es** | **-t** | er spiel**t** |
| **wir** | **-en** | wir spiel**en** |
| **ihr** | **-t** | ihr spiel**t** |
| **sie/Sie** | **-en** | sie spiel**en** |

**Memory hook (from class):** *"I requ**est** you to write it **ten ten** times."* The three underlined chunks
**est · ten · ten** split into the six endings in table order: **e|st** (ich, du) · **t|en** (er, wir) · **t|en**
(ihr, sie/Sie). And the sentence tells you how to practise: write each new verb out in full – ten times.

**Which form for a noun?** Every noun subject is "3rd person": one person or thing → the *er/sie/es* form
(*Mein Bruder spielt*), several → the *sie* form (*Meine Eltern spielen*). See [1.4](../vol1-foundations/personal-pronouns.mdx).

## 3. Three Small Spelling Rules

Language is never perfectly regular. These three rules cover almost every present-tense form you need at A1.

### Exception A: The "d/t" Rule (Adding an extra 'e')
If the verb stem ends in a **-d** or **-t** (like *arbeiten* -> *arbeit-*), it is physically hard to pronounce the normal *-st* or *-t* endings. 
So, German adds an extra **-e-** for *du, er/sie/es*, and *ihr*!
*   *arbeiten* (to work): du arbeit**e**st, er arbeit**e**t, ihr arbeit**e**t.
*   *finden* (to find): du find**e**st, er find**e**t.

The same extra *-e-* appears when the stem ends in a **consonant + m or n** – again because *-st/-t* would be
hard to say: *öffnen* → du öffn**e**st, er öffn**e**t · *regnen* → es regn**e**t · *rechnen* → er rechn**e**t.
It is **not** needed after a vowel + n or after *l/r* + n: *wohnen* → du wohnst, *lernen* → er lernt.

### Exception B: Stems ending in -s, -ß, -z (the *du* form only adds -t)
If the stem already ends in an *s*-sound, *du* just takes **-t** – otherwise you would get "-sst" or "-ßst":
*   *heißen*: du hei**ßt** (not ~~heißst~~) – *Wie heißt du?*
*   *tanzen*: du tan**zt**
*   *reisen*: du rei**st**

### Exception C: The Vowel Change (Strong Verbs)
Some very common verbs change the vowel inside their stem. **This only happens for "du" and "er/sie/es"!**

*   **e changes to i (or ie)**
    *   **sprechen** (to speak): ich spreche, du spr**i**chst, er spr**i**cht, wir sprechen.
    *   **essen** (to eat): ich esse, du **i**sst, er **i**sst, wir essen.
    *   **lesen** (to read): ich lese, du l**ie**st, er l**ie**st, wir lesen.
*   **a changes to ä**
    *   **fahren** (to drive): ich fahre, du f**ä**hrst, er f**ä**hrt, wir fahren.
    *   **schlafen** (to sleep): ich schlafe, du schl**ä**fst, er schl**ä**ft, wir schlafen.

**The A1 list to learn by heart** (the change happens only for *du* and *er/sie/es*):

| Infinitive | du | er/sie/es | Everyday sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| sprechen (speak) | sprichst | spricht | *Sprichst du Englisch?* |
| essen (eat) | isst | isst | *Er isst kein Fleisch.* |
| nehmen (take) | **nimmst** | **nimmt** | *Ich nehme den Bus, sie nimmt die Bahn.* |
| geben (give) | gibst | gibt | *Es gibt hier kein WLAN.* |
| helfen (help) | hilfst | hilft | *Hilfst du mir?* |
| sehen (see) | siehst | sieht | *Siehst du den Bus?* |
| lesen (read) | liest | liest | *Sie liest die Zeitung.* |
| treffen (meet) | triffst | trifft | *Er trifft seine Freunde.* |
| fahren (drive, go by vehicle) | fährst | fährt | *Fährt der Bus zum Bahnhof?* |
| schlafen (sleep) | schläfst | schläft | *Das Baby schläft.* |
| tragen (carry, wear) | trägst | trägt | *Sie trägt eine Brille.* |
| laufen (run, walk) | läufst | läuft | *Wie läuft's?* |
| waschen (wash) | wäschst | wäscht | *Er wäscht das Auto.* |

**Also irregular:** *wissen* (know a fact) – *ich weiß, du weißt, er weiß, wir wissen*. *Ich weiß es nicht.* = I don't know.

## 4. The Present Tense as the Future

In English, if you talk about tomorrow, you have to use the future tense: "I *will fly* to Berlin tomorrow." 
In German, you can simply use the present tense, as long as you include a "time word" (like tomorrow, next week, later).
*   *Ich **fliege** morgen nach Berlin.* (I am flying / will fly to Berlin tomorrow.)
*   *Wir **spielen** am Freitag Tennis.* (We will play tennis on Friday.)

Without a time word or clear context, the present tense is understood as **now / usually**: *Ich lese ein Buch.* =
I read / I am reading a book. Add the time word when you mean the future: *Ich lese **morgen** das Buch.*

### One German form, three English forms
German has no extra "am/is/are + -ing" and no "do/does" helper. One present-tense verb covers them all:

| English | German |
| :--- | :--- |
| I work. / I am working. / I will work (tomorrow). | *Ich arbeite (morgen).* |
| Do you work today? | *Arbeitest du heute?* |
| She doesn't work. | *Sie arbeitet nicht.* |

"am, is, are" are only translated when they are the **real** verb: *I am a student.* → *Ich **bin** Student.*

### *werden* – you will hear it too
*werden* means **to become**, and with an infinitive at the end it also makes the future. You don't need the future
form to speak at A1, but you will hear it (weather forecasts, announcements), and *werden* = "become" is everyday A1:

| ich | du | er/sie/es | wir | ihr | sie/Sie |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| werde | **wirst** | **wird** | werden | werdet | werden |

* *Mein Sohn will Arzt **werden**.* (…wants to become a doctor.) · *Es **wird** kalt.* (It's getting cold.)
* *Morgen **wird** es **regnen**.* (It will rain tomorrow.) = *Morgen regnet es.*

⚠️ **False friend:** German *will* comes from *wollen* and means **want**: *Er **will** kommen* = he **wants** to come.
English "he will come" is *Er kommt (morgen)* or *Er **wird** kommen*.

## 5. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Er sprecht Deutsch.* | *Er **spricht** Deutsch.* | *sprechen* changes e → i for *du* and *er*. |
| *Du heißst Anna.* | *Du **heißt** Anna.* | Stem ends in *ß* → *du* only adds *-t*. |
| *Er arbeitt.* | *Er **arbeitet**.* | Stem ends in *t* → extra *-e-*. |
| *Ich nehmst den Bus.* | *Ich **nehme** den Bus.* | The vowel change never affects *ich*. |
| *Wir fährt nach Berlin.* | *Wir **fahren** nach Berlin.* | Plural forms keep the normal vowel and *-en*. |
| *Ich bin arbeite.* | *Ich **arbeite**.* | German has no "I am working" form – one verb is enough. |
| *Du öffnst die Tür.* | *Du **öffnest** die Tür.* | Consonant + *n* stem → extra *-e-*. |
| *Ich will morgen kommen.* (meaning "I will come") | *Ich **komme** morgen.* | *will* = want. Future = present + time word. |

<details>

  **Sprichst** — *Sprechen* is a strong verb. The stem vowel *e* changes to *i* for *du*.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>4. fahren (sie - she):</strong> "Sie ________ mit dem Auto zur Arbeit."</summary>

  **fährt** — *Fahren* is a strong verb. The stem vowel *a* changes to *ä* for *er/sie/es*.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>5. heißen (du):</strong> "Wie ________ du?"</summary>

  **heißt** — the stem ends in *ß*, so *du* only adds *-t*.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>6. nehmen (er):</strong> "Mein Mann ________ jeden Tag den Bus."</summary>

  **nimmt** — *nehmen* changes to *nimm-* for *du* and *er/sie/es*.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>7. öffnen (die Apotheke):</strong> "Die Apotheke ________ um 8 Uhr."</summary>

  **öffnet** — stem *öffn-* ends in consonant + *n*, so an extra *-e-* makes it sayable. And *die Apotheke* is one thing → *er/sie/es* form.
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>8. Translate:</strong> "My parents are visiting us tomorrow."</summary>

  ***Meine Eltern besuchen uns morgen.*** — no "are + -ing" in German; *meine Eltern* = *sie* (they) → *-en*; *morgen* makes it future.
</details>
