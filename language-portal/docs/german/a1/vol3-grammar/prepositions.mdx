# 3.13 Basic Prepositions: Time, Place & Movement (Präpositionen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I understand that German prepositions dictate the **case** of the noun that follows.
* I know the high-frequency **Dative prepositions** (*mit, zu, bei, nach, aus*).
* I know the high-frequency **Accusative prepositions** (*für, ohne, um*).
* I know common contractions like **im**, **ins**, **am**, **zum**, and **zur**.
* I can describe where I am (*im Büro*) and where I am going (*ins Büro*).

---

## 1. What is a Preposition, and Why Does it Matter in German?

A **preposition** is a small positioning word that connects nouns to show time, location, or direction:
* *in, at, to, with, for, without, from*

In English, the words that follow a preposition don't change: *"with the man, for the man"*.
**In German, every preposition demands a specific case (Accusative or Dative).** 
When a German hears a preposition like **mit**, their brain automatically expects a Dative article (*dem / der*) right after it!

---

## 2. Fixed Dative Prepositions (Always Dative!)

Whenever you see these prepositions, the noun following them is **ALWAYS in the Dative case**:

| Preposition | Meaning | Everyday Example | Contraction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **mit** | with / by vehicle | *Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus / **mit der** Bahn.* | — |
| **zu** | to (places, people) | *Ich gehe **zu dem** Arzt. / Ich gehe **zu der** Bank.* | zu + dem = **zum** / zu + der = **zur** |
| **bei** | at / with (someone's place) | *Er wohnt **bei seinen** Eltern. / Ich bin **beim** Arzt.*| bei + dem = **beim** |
| **nach** | to (cities/countries) / after | *Ich fahre **nach** Berlin. / **Nach der** Arbeit gehe ich nach Hause.* | — |
| **aus** | from / out of | *Ich komme **aus** Indien / **aus der** Schweiz.* | — |
| **von** | from (person/point) / of | *Das Geschenk ist **von meinem** Bruder.* | von + dem = **vom** |

---

## 3. Fixed Accusative Prepositions (Always Accusative!)

Whenever you see these prepositions, the noun following them is **ALWAYS in the Accusative case** (masculine changes to *-en*):

| Preposition | Meaning | Everyday Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **für** | for | *Das Geschenk ist **für meinen** Vater (der Vater).* |
| **ohne** | without | *Kaffee **ohne** Zucker, bitte.* |
| **um** | at (time) / around | *Wir treffen uns **um** 15 Uhr.* |
| **durch** | through | *Wir fahren **durch den** Tunnel.* |

---

## 4. Location vs. Movement: "Wo?" vs. "Wohin?"

Three very common prepositions change their case depending on whether you are **already there** or **moving there**:
* **in** (in / into)
* **an** (at / to the edge)
* **auf** (on / onto)

```text
Static Location (Where are you? ➔ Wo?)
Ask: WO?  ➔  Use DATIVE (im / am / auf dem)
• Ich bin IM Büro. (in + dem = im)
• Ich stehe AM Bahnhof. (an + dem = am)
• Das Buch liegt AUF DEM Tisch.

Movement / Destination (Where are you going? ➔ Wohin?)
Ask: WOHIN?  ➔  Use ACCUSATIVE (ins / an den / auf den)
• Ich gehe INS Büro. (in + das = ins)
• Ich fahre AN DEN Bahnhof.
• Ich lege das Buch AUF DEN Tisch.
```

---

## 5. Summary of Everyday Contractions

Germans love to merge prepositions with articles. You will see these everywhere:
* in + dem = **im** (*im Restaurant*)
* in + das = **ins** (*ins Kino*)
* an + dem = **am** (*am Montag, am Bahnhof*)
* an + das = **ans** (*ans Meer*)
* zu + dem = **zum** (*zum Arzt, zum Supermarkt*)
* zu + der = **zur** (*zur Post, zur Arbeit*)
* bei + dem = **beim** (*beim Bäcker*)
* von + dem = **vom** (*vom Bahnhof*)
