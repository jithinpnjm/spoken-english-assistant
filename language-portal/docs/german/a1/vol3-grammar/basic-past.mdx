# 3.16 Talking About the Past: "war" & "hatte"

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can talk about the past using **war** (was/were) and **hatte** (had).
* I understand why Germans prefer *war* and *hatte* over complex past-tense forms in conversation.
* I know how to conjugate *war* and *hatte* for all personal pronouns.
* I can use essential past-time markers (*gestern, letzte Woche, früher*).

---

## 1. What You Need for the Past at A1 Level

In German, there are two past tenses:
1. The spoken conversational past (**Perfekt** — *Ich habe gelernt*), which you will master in A2.
2. The written narrative past (**Präteritum**).

At A1, **you do not need to memorize the past tense of hundreds of verbs.** 
However, every single German speaker in daily life uses the simple past of two core verbs:
* **sein** (to be) ➔ **war** (was / were)
* **haben** (to have) ➔ **hatte** (had)

Instead of saying the clunky phrase *„Ich bin krank gewesen“*, Germans naturally say:
> *„Ich **war** krank.“* (I was sick.)

Instead of saying *„Ich habe keine Zeit gehabt“*, Germans naturally say:
> *„Ich **hatte** keine Zeit.“* (I had no time.)

---

## 2. Conjugating "war" (Past of sein)

Notice the familiar rule: **`ich` and `er/sie/es` have NO endings and are completely identical!**

| Pronoun | war (was / were) | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **war** | *Ich **war** gestern zu Hause.* (I was home yesterday.) |
| **du** | **warst** | *Wo **warst** du am Wochenende?* (Where were you on the weekend?) |
| **er / sie / es** | **war** | *Das Wetter **war** herrlich.* (The weather was wonderful.) |
| **wir** | **waren** | *Wir **waren** im Urlaub.* (We were on vacation.) |
| **ihr** | **wart** | *Wo **wart** ihr gestern?* (Where were you guys yesterday?) |
| **sie / Sie** | **waren** | *Herr Weber, **waren** Sie krank?* (Were you sick, Mr. Weber?) |

---

## 3. Conjugating "hatte" (Past of haben)

Again, **`ich` and `er/sie/es` are completely identical**:

| Pronoun | hatte (had) | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **hatte** | *Ich **hatte** gestern keine Zeit.* (I had no time yesterday.) |
| **du** | **hattest** | ***Hattest** du am Sonntag frei?* (Did you have Sunday off?) |
| **er / sie / es** | **hatte** | *Er **hatte** hohes Fieber.* (He had a high fever.) |
| **wir** | **hatten** | *Wir **hatten** viel Spaß.* (We had a lot of fun.) |
| **ihr** | **hattet** | ***Hattet** ihr ein schönes Wochenende?* (Did you guys have a nice weekend?) |
| **sie / Sie** | **hatten** | *Hatten Sie gestern viel Arbeit?* (Did you have a lot of work yesterday?) |

---

## 4. Past Time Markers: When Did it Happen?

To place your sentence in the past, pair *war* and *hatte* with these common time words:

* **gestern** = *yesterday* (*Gestern war ich im Büro.*)
* **vorgestern** = *the day before yesterday*
* **letzte Woche** = *last week* (*Letzte Woche hatte ich Urlaub.*)
* **letzten Monat / letztes Jahr** = *last month / last year*
* **am Wochenende** = *on the weekend* (*Am Wochenende waren wir in Köln.*)
* **früher** = *in the past / earlier in life* (*Früher hatte ich kein Auto.*)
