# 3.10 The Dative Case: The Receiver (Der Dativ)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I understand what the **Dative Case** is and why German uses it for the receiver of an action (*to whom*).
* I know the article changes in the Dative: **dem** (m/n), **der** (f), and **den** (pl).
* I can use essential Dative verbs like **helfen** (to help) and **gefallen** (to like/please).
* I can answer the daily question *„Wie geht es Ihnen / dir?“* using **mir** (*Mir geht es gut*).
* I know the most common prepositions that always take the Dative (*mit, nach, zu, bei*).

---

## 1. What is the Dative Case?

Think of a sentence with three parts:
> *"I give the colleague a book."*

Let's break down who is doing what:
1. **Who is doing the giving?** ➔ *I* (The Subject = **Nominative**)
2. **What is being given?** ➔ *a book* (The Direct Object = **Accusative**)
3. **To whom is it being given?** ➔ *to the colleague* (The Receiver = **Dative**)

In German:
> *„Ich (Nom) gebe **dem Kollegen** (Dat) ein Buch (Akk).“*

The **Dative Case (Dativ)** answers the question: **Wem?** (To whom? For whom?). It is the case of the **receiver** or **beneficiary** of an action.

---

## 2. How Articles Change in the Dative

In the Dative case, all articles shift. Notice that masculine (*der*) and neutral (*das*) share the exact same ending (**-m**):

| Gender | Nominative | Dative (definite) | Dative (*ein / kein / mein*) | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine (der)** | der | ➔ **dem** | **einem / keinem / meinem** | *Ich helfe **dem** Mann.* |
| **Neutral (das)** | das | ➔ **dem** | **einem / keinem / meinem** | *Ich helfe **dem** Kind.* |
| **Feminine (die)** | die | ➔ **der** | **einer / keiner / meiner** | *Ich helfe **der** Frau.* |
| **Plural (die)** | die | ➔ **den** + noun **-n** | (no *ein*) **keinen / meinen** + noun **-n** | *Ich helfe **den** Kinder**n**.* |

### The plural "-n" rule
In the dative plural, the **noun itself** also gets an **-n** – unless it already ends in *-n* or in *-s*:
*die Kinder → mit den Kinder**n***, *die Freunde → mit meinen Freunde**n***, *die Tage → seit zwei Tage**n***,
but *die Frauen → mit den Frauen* (already -n) and *die Autos → mit den Autos* (-s).

### A few masculine nouns also add -(e)n
Some masculine nouns for people add **-(e)n** in every case except the nominative: *der Kollege → dem Kolleg**en***,
*der Herr → Herrn Weber* (*Ich rufe Herrn Weber an. Ich helfe Herrn Weber.*), *der Student → dem Student**en***.
At A1 just learn these few as you meet them.

### ⚠️ The Big Beginner Trap: "der" for Feminine!
In the Nominative, *der* means masculine (*der Mann*). 
**In the Dative, *der* is FEMININE!**
* *die Mutter* ➔ *Ich helfe **der** Mutter.* (I help the mother).
Do not panic when you see *der* in front of a woman; it simply means the sentence is in the Dative case!

## 3. The Most Common Everyday Dative Verbs

In German, certain verbs do not take an Accusative direct object. They require the person to be in the **Dative Case**:

### 1. helfen (to help — *Irregular: du hilfst, er hilft*)
* *„Können Sie **mir** helfen?“* (Can you help me?)
* *„Ich helfe **dem Kollegen**.“* (I am helping the colleague.)

### 2. danken (to thank)
* *„Ich danke **Ihnen**.“* (I thank you — formal).
* *„Ich danke **dir**.“* (I thank you — informal).

### 3. gefallen (to please / to like the look of something)
Besides *Ich mag die Wohnung*, German very often says *"The apartment pleases me"* – especially for things you see or experience:
* *„Die Wohnung gefällt **mir** sehr gut.“* (I like the apartment a lot.)
* *„Gefällt **dir** die Musik?“* (Do you like the music?)

### 4. schmecken (to taste good to someone)
* *„Die Suppe schmeckt **mir** gut.“* (The soup tastes good to me.)
* *„Schmeckt es **euch**?“* (Does it taste good to you guys?)

### More everyday dative verbs – learn them as a group
English doesn't mark this, so these verbs cause most dative mistakes. The person is always dative:

| Verb | Example | Typical mistake |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **gehören** (belong to) | *Wem gehört die Tasche? – Sie gehört **mir**.* | ❌ *gehört mich* |
| **passen** (suit / fit) | *Passt **Ihnen** Dienstag?* · *Die Hose passt **mir** nicht.* | ❌ *passt Sie* |
| **antworten** (answer someone) | *Bitte antworten Sie **mir** bis Freitag.* | ❌ *antworten mich* |
| **gratulieren** (congratulate) | *Ich gratuliere **dir** zum Geburtstag!* | ❌ *gratuliere dich* |
| **zuhören** (listen to) | *Hör **mir** bitte zu!* | ❌ *hör mich zu* |
| **fehlen** (be missing / be wrong) | *Was fehlt **Ihnen**?* (at the doctor's) | ❌ *Was fehlt Sie* |
| **wehtun** (hurt) | *Der Rücken tut **mir** weh.* | ❌ *tut mich weh* |

### Asking "to whom?": *Wem?*
The question words change with the case, just like the articles:
* **Wer** hilft? (subject, nominative) – **Wen** siehst du? (object, accusative) – **Wem** hilfst du? (dative)
* *„**Wem** gehört das Handy?“* – *„Meiner Kollegin.“*

### Word order tip: short pronouns come early
A dative pronoun usually comes **before** a full noun subject: *Gefällt **dir** der Film?* (more natural than
*Gefällt der Film dir?*) · *Hat **Ihnen** das Essen geschmeckt?*

### Verbs with two objects: person = dative, thing = accusative
"Giving" verbs – you give, bring, show, send, explain, buy or write **something** (accusative) **to someone**
(dative). Memory hook (from class): **P-D, T-A** = **P**erson **D**ative, **T**hing **A**ccusative.

| Verb | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **geben** | *Ich gebe **dem Kollegen** (D) **den Schlüssel** (A).* |
| **bringen** | *Ich bringe **meiner Mutter** **einen Stuhl**.* |
| **zeigen** | *Ich zeige **dir** **den Bahnhof**.* |
| **schicken** | *Er schickt **seiner Schwester** **ein Paket**.* |
| **schenken** | *Wir schenken **dem Kind** **ein Buch**.* |
| **erklären** | *Die Lehrerin erklärt **den Studenten** **die Regel**.* |
| **kaufen** | *Sie kauft **dem Kind** **ein Eis**.* (buys the child an ice cream) |

Word order when **both are nouns**: usually person (dative) first, then the thing (accusative) – as in the table.
When the thing is a **pronoun**, it comes first: *Ich gebe **ihn** dem Kollegen.* (the key → *ihn*) ·
*Ich gebe **ihn** dir.* At A1, being understood matters more than perfect order here.

## 4. Personal Pronouns in the Dative: "Wie geht's?"

You have already used Dative pronouns since your very first German lesson without knowing it!
When someone asks:
> *„Wie geht es **dir**?“* (How is it going *to you*? — informal)  
> *„Wie geht es **Ihnen**?“* (How is it going *to you*? — formal)

You answer:
> *„**Mir** geht es gut, danke!“* (It goes well *to me*!)

### Core Dative Pronoun Chart

| Person | Nominative (Subject) | Dative (To whom) | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I / me | **ich** | **mir** | *Das Buch gehört **mir**.* (The book belongs to me.) |
| you / you | **du** | **dir** | *Wie geht es **dir**?* |
| he / him | **er** | **ihm** | *Ich helfe **ihm**.* (I help him.) |
| she / her | **sie** | **ihr** | *Ich gebe **ihr** den Schlüssel.* (I give her the key.) |
| it / it | **es** | **ihm** | *Das Kind ist müde. Ich helfe **ihm**.* |
| we / us | **wir** | **uns** | *Der Chef dankt **uns**.* (The boss thanks us.) |
| you all / you | **ihr** | **euch** | *Passt es **euch** morgen?* (Does tomorrow suit you guys?) |
| they / them | **sie** | **ihnen** | *Meine Eltern kommen. Ich helfe **ihnen**.* |
| You / You *(formal)* | **Sie** | **Ihnen** | *Wie kann ich **Ihnen** helfen?* |

⚠️ Two traps: **ihr** is both "you all" (nominative) and "to her" (dative) – context tells you which. And *ihnen*
(to them) vs. *Ihnen* (to you, formal) differ only in the capital letter.

### "How is your brother?" – *es geht* + dative for anyone
The same pattern works for every person. The person is dative; *es* stays the subject:
* *Wie geht es **deinem Bruder**?* – *Es geht **ihm** gut.* / ***Ihm** geht es gut.*
* *Wie geht es **Ihrer Mutter**?* – *Danke, es geht **ihr** besser.*
* *Geht es **deinen Eltern** gut?* – *Ja, es geht **ihnen** gut.*

⚠️ ❌ *Ich bin gut.* for "I'm fine" – German hears "I am good (at something) / a good person". Say ***Mir geht es
gut.*** or just *Gut, danke.*

### Things are *er, sie, es* – in every case
Pronouns follow the **gender of the noun**, not "person or thing". A pen (*der Kuli*) is *er / ihn / ihm*,
exactly like a man:

| Noun | Subject (Nom.) | Object (Akk.) | Dative | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **der** Kuli / Mann | er | **ihn** | ihm | *Der Kuli ist neu. **Er** ist blau. Ich kaufe **ihn**.* |
| **das** Buch / Kind | es | es | ihm | *Das Buch ist gut. Ich lese **es**.* |
| **die** Tasche / Frau | sie | sie | **ihr** | *Die Tasche ist teuer. Anna will **sie** kaufen.* |
| **die** (plural) | sie | sie | **ihnen** | *Die Schuhe sind bequem. Ich nehme **sie**.* |

❌ *Der Kuli ist blau. Es ist neu. Er kauft es.* → ✅ *… **Er** ist neu. Er kauft **ihn**.* (English "it" ≠ German *es*.)

### Decoding *sie / Sie* and *ihr / Ihr*
*sie/Sie* can mean she, her, they, them, it (for a *die*-noun) or formal you. *ihr/Ihr* can mean you-all, (to) her,
her, their or formal your. Four clues sort them out:

1. **Capital letter** in the middle of a sentence → formal: *Ich frage **Sie**.* (you) · *Ist das **Ihr** Haus?* (your)
2. **Verb ending** → *sie **wohnt*** = she · *sie **wohnen*** = they (or formal *Sie wohnen*).
3. **Subject or object?** When another word is already the subject, *sie* is the object: *Er fragt **sie**.* (her/them)
4. **Noun after it?** *ihr* **+ noun** = her/their/your (*ihr Bruder*); *ihr* **alone** = you-all (*Wo wohnt ihr?*)
   or "to her" after a dative verb (*Er hilft **ihr**.*).

If all four clues still leave two options (*Sie wohnen in Berlin* = they / you), the **context** decides.

## 5. Dative Prepositions You Use Every Day

Whenever you use these prepositions, the noun that follows is **ALWAYS in the Dative case**:

* **mit** (with / by vehicle): *Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus / **mit der** Bahn.*
* **zu** (to): *Ich gehe **zum** Arzt (*zu + dem = zum*) / **zur** Apotheke (*zu + der = zur*).*
* **nach** (after / to cities): *Nach **der** Arbeit gehe ich nach Hause.*
* **bei** (at / with): *Ich wohne **bei meinen** Eltern.*
* **seit** (since / for): *Ich wohne **seit einem** Jahr hier.* – with the present tense! See [3.13](./prepositions.mdx).
* **aus, von** (from): *Ich komme **aus der** Türkei.* · *Das ist ein Brief **vom** Vermieter.*

### Common mistakes
| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Kannst du mich helfen?* | *Kannst du **mir** helfen?* | *helfen* + dative. |
| *Das gefällt mich.* | *Das gefällt **mir**.* | *gefallen* + dative. |
| *Ich helfe die Frau.* | *Ich helfe **der** Frau.* | Feminine dative → *der*. |
| *mit den Freunde* | *mit den Freunde**n*** | Dative plural adds *-n*. |
| *Wie geht es Sie?* | *Wie geht es **Ihnen**?* | Dative of *Sie* is *Ihnen*. |
| *Ich bin gut.* (meaning "I'm fine") | *Mir geht es gut.* | Well-being → *es geht* + dative. |
| *Ich gebe den Kollegen das Buch.* (one colleague) | *Ich gebe **dem** Kollegen das Buch.* | Person who receives → dative *dem*. |
