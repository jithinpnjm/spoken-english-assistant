# 3.14 Conjunctions: Linking Sentences (Satzverbindungen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can connect two simple sentences using **und** (and), **oder** (or), **aber** (but), and **denn** (because).
* I understand the **"Position 0" rule**: these conjunctions DO NOT change the word order!
* I can give reasons using **denn** without needing complex grammar.
* I can write smooth, connected emails and messages instead of short, robotic sentences.

---

## 1. Why Do We Need Conjunctions?

When you first start learning German, your speech can sound like a robot:
* *"I live in Frankfurt. I work at a bank. I am learning German."*

To sound natural, you need connector words called **Conjunctions (Konjunktionen)**:
* *„Ich wohne in Frankfurt **und** ich arbeite bei einer Bank.“*
* *„Ich lerne Deutsch, **denn** ich wohne in Deutschland.“*

---

## 2. The Golden Rule: Position 0 (Zero Position)

In German, some connectors change the word order, pushing the verb to the very end (like *weil* at A2). 
However, the four most important A1 connectors **do not count as a position at all**. They occupy **Position 0**:

```text
[Sentence 1: Main Clause]  ,  [Pos 0: Conjunction]   [Pos 1: Subject]   [Pos 2: Verb]   [Rest]
Ich kann heute nicht kommen ,         denn                 ich               bin         krank.
Ich spiele gern Tennis      ,         aber                 ich              habe       keine Zeit.
```

Because the conjunction sits in "Position 0", **the word order of the second sentence stays 100% normal**:
* Subject is in Position 1.
* The conjugated verb is in **Position 2**!

---

## 3. The 4 Core A1 Connectors

### 1. und (and)
Adds more information:
* *Ich heiße Maria **und** ich komme aus Spanien.* (I am called Maria and I come from Spain.)
* *Er trinkt Kaffee **und** er liest die Zeitung.*

### 2. aber (but)
Shows a contrast or limitation:
* *Ich möchte kommen, **aber** ich muss arbeiten.* (I would like to come, but I have to work.)
* *Die Wohnung ist schön, **aber** sie ist teuer.* (The flat is nice, but it is expensive.)

### 3. oder (or)
Presents a choice or alternative:
* *Trinkst du Kaffee **oder** möchtest du Tee?* (Are you drinking coffee or would you like tea?)
* *Fahren wir mit dem Bus **oder** gehen wir zu Fuß?*

### 4. denn (because / for)
Gives a reason. **This is your secret weapon for A1 speaking exams!**
* *Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich bin krank.* (I am staying home because I am sick.)
* *Ich lerne Deutsch, **denn** ich arbeite in Deutschland.*

⚠️ **Why *denn* is easier than *weil*:** 
In German, *weil* (because) forces the verb to the absolute end (*weil ich krank bin*). But **denn** keeps the verb in normal Position 2 (*denn ich bin krank*)!
