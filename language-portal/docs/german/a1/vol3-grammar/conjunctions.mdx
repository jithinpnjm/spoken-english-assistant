# 3.14 Conjunctions: Linking Sentences (Satzverbindungen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can connect two simple sentences using **und** (and), **oder** (or), **aber** (but), and **denn** (because).
* I understand the **"Position 0" rule**: these conjunctions DO NOT change the word order!
* I can give reasons using **denn** without needing complex grammar – and I recognise *weil* (verb at the end).
* I can correct information with **sondern** (*nicht in Köln, sondern in Bonn*) instead of *aber*.
* I can write smooth, connected emails and messages instead of short, robotic sentences.

---

## 1. Why Do We Need Conjunctions?

When you first start learning German, your speech can sound like a robot:
* *"I live in Frankfurt. I work at a bank. I am learning German."*

To sound natural, you need connector words called **Conjunctions (Konjunktionen)**:
* *„Ich wohne in Frankfurt **und** ich arbeite bei einer Bank.“*
* *„Ich lerne Deutsch, **denn** ich wohne in Deutschland.“*

---

## 2. The Golden Rule: Position 0 (Zero Position)

In German, some connectors change the word order, pushing the verb to the very end (like *weil* at A2). 
However, the four most important A1 connectors **do not count as a position at all**. They occupy **Position 0**:

```text
[Sentence 1: Main Clause]  ,  [Pos 0: Conjunction]   [Pos 1: Subject]   [Pos 2: Verb]   [Rest]
Ich kann heute nicht kommen ,         denn                 ich               bin         krank.
Ich spiele gern Tennis      ,         aber                 ich              habe       keine Zeit.
```

Because the conjunction sits in "Position 0", **the word order of the second sentence stays 100% normal**:
* Subject is in Position 1.
* The conjugated verb is in **Position 2**!

---

## 3. The Core A1 Connectors

### 1. und (and)
Adds more information:
* *Ich heiße Maria **und** ich komme aus Spanien.* (I am called Maria and I come from Spain.)
* *Er trinkt Kaffee **und** er liest die Zeitung.*

### 2. aber (but)
Shows a contrast or limitation:
* *Ich möchte kommen, **aber** ich muss arbeiten.* (I would like to come, but I have to work.)
* *Die Wohnung ist schön, **aber** sie ist teuer.* (The flat is nice, but it is expensive.)

### 3. oder (or)
Presents a choice or alternative:
* *Trinkst du Kaffee **oder** möchtest du Tee?* (Are you drinking coffee or would you like tea?)
* *Fahren wir mit dem Bus **oder** gehen wir zu Fuß?*

### 4. denn (because / for)
Gives a reason. **This is your secret weapon for A1 speaking exams!**
* *Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich bin krank.* (I am staying home because I am sick.)
* *Ich lerne Deutsch, **denn** ich arbeite in Deutschland.*

### ⚠️ *denn* vs. *weil* – same meaning, different verb position
Both mean "because". You will **hear** *weil* all the time, so you need to recognise it – but at A1 you can
produce every reason with *denn*.

```text
                  0        1       2 (VERB)   …
Ich bleibe zu Hause,  denn   ich     bin        krank.       ← verb in position 2 (normal order)
Ich bleibe zu Hause,  weil   ich     krank      bin.         ← verb at the very END (A2 topic)
```

* *denn* = a Position-0 connector: the sentence after it is a completely normal sentence.
* *weil* starts a subordinate clause: the conjugated verb goes to the end. You learn to build these at A2 –
  for now, if you say *weil*, you must put the verb last; if that feels hard, use *denn*.
  A separable verb joins up again at the end: *…, weil ich mein Zimmer **aufräume**.*
* One more difference: **a *denn* sentence cannot stand alone.** *Warum kommst du nicht? – ~~Denn ich bin krank.~~*
  In a short spoken answer people say *Weil ich krank bin.* – or at A1 simply *Ich bin krank.*

⚠️ *denn* has a second job: in questions it's a softening word (*Wie heißt du **denn**?* = "So, what's your name?").
That *denn* is not a conjunction and doesn't mean "because".

### 5. sondern (but rather / instead) – the correction word
Use **sondern** only when the first part says **nicht** or **kein** and the second part gives the **correct**
information instead. English uses "but" for both – German doesn't:

| Situation | Connector | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Contrast: both things are true | **aber** | *Die Wohnung ist klein, **aber** sie ist billig.* (small AND cheap) |
| Correction: the first thing is false | **sondern** | *Die Wohnung ist **nicht** klein, **sondern** groß.* (not small – big!) |

More everyday examples:
* *Ich komme **nicht** aus Österreich, **sondern** aus der Schweiz.*
* *Wir treffen uns **nicht** am Montag, **sondern** am Dienstag.*
* *Ich trinke **keinen** Kaffee, **sondern** Tee.*

Test: *Does the second part **replace** the first?* → **sondern**. *Does it add a contrast or a problem?* → **aber**.
(*Ich trinke keinen Kaffee, **aber** ich trinke gern Tee.* is also possible – here you add information instead of correcting.)
Like *und, aber, oder, denn*, **sondern** is in Position 0.

**Memory hook (from class): O-S-U-D-A** = **o**der, **s**ondern, **u**nd, **d**enn, **a**ber – the five
Position-0 connectors. If a connector is in OSUDA, the next clause keeps normal word order. Any *other* connector
you meet (*weil, dass, wenn* …) changes the word order – treat those as "listen and recognise" for now.

---

## 4. The Same Sentence – Different Starting Points

After a Position-0 connector you can still start the second clause with something other than the subject –
then the normal V2 inversion applies:

```text
 0       1           2 (VERB)   3
aber     morgen      habe       ich   Zeit.
denn     heute       muss       ich   arbeiten.
```
*Ich kann heute nicht kommen, **aber morgen habe ich** Zeit.* – *aber* doesn't count; *morgen* is position 1;
the verb *habe* is position 2.

---

## 5. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *…, denn ich krank bin.* | *…, denn ich **bin** krank.* | After *denn* the verb stays in position 2. |
| *…, aber morgen ich habe Zeit.* | *…, aber morgen **habe ich** Zeit.* | *aber* is position 0; *morgen* is position 1 → verb next. |
| *Ich wohne nicht in Köln, aber in Bonn.* | *…, **sondern** in Bonn.* | Correction after *nicht* → *sondern*. |
| *Ich bin nicht müde, sondern ich habe Hunger.* (meaning both) | *Ich bin nicht müde, **aber** ich habe Hunger.* | Nothing is being corrected – it's extra information → *aber*. |
| *Und ich komme aus Indien und ich wohne in Köln und ich …* | Two or three clauses, then a new sentence. | Connectors help – but not every sentence needs one. |
