# 3.15 The Imperative: Instructions & Requests (Der Imperativ)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can give instructions and polite requests using the **formal Sie** form (*Kommen Sie bitte!*).
* I can give commands to a friend (**du**) or a group of friends (**ihr**).
* I know how to soften commands with **bitte** (please) and **mal** so they sound natural and polite.
* I can understand doctor's instructions, traffic directions, and recipe steps.

---

## 1. What is the Imperative?

When you give an instruction, command, or request, you are using the **Imperative (Der Imperativ)**:
* *"Please sit down."*
* *"Sign here, please."*
* *"Turn right at the corner."*

In German, commands have **no subject pronoun** (except for the formal *Sie*). You only use the verb, and the verb always jumps to **Position 1**.

---

## 2. The 3 Forms of the Imperative

German commands change depending on who you are talking to:

### 1. Formal Command (with "Sie" — The Easiest!)
To make a formal request to an adult, colleague, or official:
**Formula:** [Infinitive Verb] + **Sie** + **bitte**!
* *Kommen Sie bitte!* (Please come!)
* *Nehmen Sie bitte Platz.* (Please take a seat.)
* *Unterschreiben Sie hier, bitte.* (Please sign here.)
* *Sein (irregular):* ***Seien** Sie bitte leise!* (Please be quiet!)

### 2. Group of Friends (with "ihr")
To tell a group of friends, children, or family members to do something:
**Formula:** Use the normal *ihr* verb form, but **drop the word "ihr"**!
* Normal: *Ihr kommt.* ➔ Command: ***Kommt!*** (Come!)
* Normal: *Ihr wartet.* ➔ Command: ***Wartet!*** (Wait!)
* Normal: *Ihr esst.* ➔ Command: ***Esst!*** (Eat!)

### 3. One Friend or Child (with "du")
To tell one friend or child to do something:
**Formula:** Take the *du* form of the verb, but **drop the "du" AND drop the "-st"**!
* Normal: *du kommst* ➔ Command: ***Komm!***
* Normal: *du machst* ➔ Command: ***Mach!*** (*Mach das Fenster auf!*)
* Normal: *du liest* ➔ Command: ***Lies!*** / *du nimmst* ➔ ***Nimm!*** / *du isst* ➔ ***Iss!*** (the **e ➔ i/ie** change stays).
* *If the stem ends in -d or -t:* add an -e for pronunciation: *du wartest* ➔ ***Warte!***

⚠️ **The one exception to the formula: a ➔ ä verbs lose the umlaut.** The *du* form has an umlaut
(*du fährst, du schläfst, du läufst*), but the command does **not**:
* *du fährst* ➔ ***Fahr** langsam!* (never ~~*Fähr!*~~)
* *du schläfst* ➔ ***Schlaf** gut!*
* *du läufst* ➔ ***Lauf!***

Why? The imperative is built from the verb's basic stem. Only the **e ➔ i/ie** change survives into the command – the
**a ➔ ä** change doesn't. You will often hear an extra *-e* in careful speech (*Mache! Komme!*); both are correct, the
short form is more common.

### The irregular one: *sein*
| to | Command | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| du | **Sei!** | *Sei bitte leise, das Baby schläft.* |
| ihr | **Seid!** | *Seid bitte pünktlich!* |
| Sie | **Seien Sie!** | *Seien Sie bitte vorsichtig.* |

### Separable verbs in the imperative
The prefix still goes to the **end**: *Ruf mich an!* · *Steigen Sie bitte hier aus.* · *Macht die Tür zu!*

### Quick overview
| Infinitive | du | ihr | Sie |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| kommen | Komm! | Kommt! | Kommen Sie! |
| warten | Warte! | Wartet! | Warten Sie! |
| nehmen | **Nimm!** | Nehmt! | Nehmen Sie! |
| fahren | **Fahr!** (no ä) | Fahrt! | Fahren Sie! |
| anrufen | Ruf … an! | Ruft … an! | Rufen Sie … an! |
| sein | **Sei!** | **Seid!** | **Seien Sie!** |

## 3. How to Sound Polite (Don't Be Too Direct!)

A bare imperative (*„Komm!“*, *„Unterschreiben Sie!“*) can sound harsh. To sound natural and polite, people usually add softening words:

1. **bitte** (please):
   * *„Kommen Sie **bitte** herein.“* (Please come in.)
2. **mal** (just / for a second):
   * *„Schau **mal**!“* (Just look! / Look here!)
   * *„Warte **mal** kurz.“* (Wait a second.)

## 4. Real-Life Germany: Where You Hear the Imperative

### 1. At the Doctor's Clinic (Die Praxis)
* *„**Öffnen** Sie bitte den Mund.“* (Please open your mouth.)
* *„**Nehmen** Sie die Tablette vor dem Essen.“* (Take the pill before meals.)

### 2. Asking for Directions (Wegbeschreibung)
* *„**Gehen** Sie geradeaus und **biegen** Sie rechts **ab**.“* (Go straight and turn right.)

### 3. At the Bürgeramt / Bank
* *„**Geben** Sie mir bitte Ihren Ausweis.“* (Please give me your ID.)
* *„**Warten** Sie bitte im Warteraum.“* (Please wait in the waiting room.)

### Step 3: Common mistakes – correct the sentence
| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Kommst du!* (as a command) | *Komm!* | Drop *du* and *-st*. With *du* in position 2 it's a question. |
| *Fähr nicht so schnell!* | *Fahr nicht so schnell!* | a ➔ ä verbs lose the umlaut in the command. |
| *Nehm das!* | *Nimm das!* | e ➔ i verbs keep the change. |
| *Bitte Sie warten.* | *Warten Sie bitte.* | Verb first, then *Sie*, then *bitte*. |
| *Anrufen Sie mich!* | *Rufen Sie mich an!* | The separable prefix goes to the end. |
| *Sind Sie leise!* | *Seien Sie bitte leise!* | *sein* has its own command forms. |
