# 3.8 Questions: W-Questions, Yes/No & "Doch" (Fragesätze)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can ask open questions using question words (**W-Fragen**).
* I can ask closed questions starting with the verb (**Ja/Nein-Fragen**).
* I know that in Yes/No questions, the verb moves to **Position 1**.
* I can use the special German word **doch** to answer "yes" to a negative question.
* I can conduct an interview or ask for assistance politely in German.

---

## 1. The Two Types of Questions

In every language, you ask questions in two fundamentally different ways:

1. **Open Questions (W-Fragen):** You want specific information (Who, What, Where, When).
   * *„**Wo** wohnst du?“* ➔ *„In Frankfurt.“*
2. **Closed Questions (Ja/Nein-Fragen):** You want a simple confirmation (Yes or No).
   * *„**Wohnst** du in Frankfurt?“* ➔ *„Ja.“* or *„Nein.“*

In German, these two types of questions have **two completely different word orders**.

---

## 2. Type 1: W-Questions (Verb in Position 2)

When your question begins with a question word (*Wer, Was, Wo, Wann, Warum, Wie*), the rule from Chapter 3.7 applies: **The conjugated verb sits in Position 2.**

```text
[Position 1: W-Word]   [Position 2: Conjugated Verb]   [Position 3: Subject]   [Rest of sentence]
         Wo                        wohnst                       du?
        Wann                       beginnt                      der Kurs?
        Was                        kaufen                       wir                     heute?
```

### The 8 Core German Question Words:
* **Wer?** = Who? *(Wer ist das?)*
* **Was?** = What? *(Was machst du?)*
* **Wo?** = Where? *(Wo ist der Bahnhof?)*
* **Woher?** = Where from? *(Woher kommen Sie?)*
* **Wohin?** = Where to? *(Wohin fährst du?)*
* **Wann?** = When? *(Wann haben Sie Zeit?)*
* **Warum?** = Why? *(Warum lernst du Deutsch?)*
* **Wie?** = How / What? *(Wie heißt du? / Wie viel kostet das?)*

Useful extras: **Wie viel?** (how much – *Wie viel kostet das?*), **Wie viele?** (how many – *Wie viele Kinder haben Sie?*),
**Wie lange?** (how long), **Wie oft?** (how often), **Welcher/Welche/Welches?** (which – see [3.21](./welch-and-dies.mdx)),
**Wen?** (whom? — see [3.9 Accusative](./accusative.mdx)). Learn *Wem?* only inside the useful phrase *Wem geht es gut?* if you meet it; it is not a question pattern you need to build in this lesson.

### ⚠️ English "where" = three German words
The question word already tells you how to answer:

| Question | Meaning | Typical answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wo** wohnst du? | where (location, no movement) | **in** Köln · **im** Büro · **bei** meinen Eltern · **zu** Hause |
| **Wohin** fährst du? | where to (direction) | **nach** Köln · **ins** Büro · **zum** Arzt · **nach** Hause |
| **Woher** kommst du? | where from (origin) | **aus** Indien · **aus der** Schweiz · **vom** Arzt |

❌ *Wo gehst du?* → ✅ *Wohin gehst du?* (you are moving). In speech you'll also hear it split: *Wo gehst du **hin**?* /
*Wo kommst du **her**?*

## 3. Type 2: Yes/No Questions (Verb in Position 1!)

In English, to ask a Yes/No question, you usually need a helper verb like *do* or *are*:
* *"**Do** you speak German?"*
* *"**Is** the doctor here?"*

In German, **you never add an extra word like "do".** 
Instead, **the main verb simply jumps to Position 1:**

```text
[Position 1: Conjugated Verb]   [Position 2: Subject]   [Rest of question]
           Sprichst                      du                  Deutsch?
             Ist                         der Arzt            hier?
           Haben                         Sie                 einen Termin?
           Trinkst                       du                  gern Kaffee?
```

*The Mental Model:* To turn any normal statement into a Yes/No question, simply swap the first two words!
* Statement: *Du **lernst** Deutsch.* (Position 1: Du, Position 2: lernst).
* Question: ***Lernst** du Deutsch?* (Position 1: Lernst, Position 2: du).

---

## 4. The Magic German Word: "Doch!"

In English, answering a negative question is awkward:
* *"Aren't you coming to the party?"* ➔ If you answer "Yes", do you mean "Yes, I am not coming" or "Yes, I am coming"?

German solved this problem with one brilliant word: **doch**.

```text
Question is POSITIVE:
„Kommst du zur Party?“  ➔  Ja, ich komme.  (Yes, I am)
                         ➔  Nein, ich komme nicht.  (No, I am not)

Question is NEGATIVE (contains "nicht" or "kein"):
„Kommst du NICHT zur Party?“  ➔  Nein, ich komme nicht.  (Agreeing with negative: No, I am not)
                               ➔  DOCH! Ich komme!  (Contradicting the negative: Oh yes, I AM coming!)
```

### More Everyday "Doch" Examples:
* *„Hast du kein Geld dabei?“* (Don't you have money with you?)
  * ➔ *„**Doch!** Hier sind 20 Euro.“* (Yes, I DO have money!)
* *„Sprichst du kein Deutsch?“* (Don't you speak German?)
  * ➔ *„**Doch!** Ein bisschen.“* (On the contrary, yes I do!)

### Common mistakes
| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Wo du wohnst?* | *Wo **wohnst du**?* | W-question: verb in position 2. |
| *Du sprichst Deutsch?* (in writing) | ***Sprichst du** Deutsch?* | Yes/no question: verb first. (Rising intonation alone is casual speech only.) |
| *Machst du sprechen Deutsch?* | *Sprichst du Deutsch?* | German has no "do"-helper. |
| *Wo gehst du?* | ***Wohin** gehst du?* | Movement → *wohin*. |
| *Hast du keine Zeit? – Ja, ich habe Zeit.* | *– **Doch**, ich habe Zeit.* | Positive answer to a negative question. |
