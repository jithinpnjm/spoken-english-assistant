# 3.12 Separable Verbs (Trennbare Verben)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I understand what a separable verb is and how adding a prefix changes a verb's meaning.
* I can identify the most common separable prefixes (*an-, auf-, ab-, ein-, aus-, mit-, zu-*).
* I know how to split the verb in normal sentences (prefix to the very end).
* I know that separable verbs **stay glued together** when used with a modal verb (*muss aufstehen*).

---

## 1. What is a Separable Verb?

In English, we have phrasal verbs where a small preposition follows a verb:
* *stand ➔ stand **up***
* *turn ➔ turn **off***
* *pick ➔ pick **up***

In German, these prepositions are glued directly onto the front of the verb as a **prefix**:
* *stehen* (to stand) + **auf** = **aufstehen** (to get up)
* *machen* (to do) + **zu** = **zumachen** (to close / shut)
* *holen* (to fetch) + **ab** = **abholen** (to pick someone up)

### The Splitting Rule:
When you use a separable verb in a normal German main sentence:
1. The **base verb** conjugates to match the person and sits in **Position 2**.
2. The **prefix** detaches and flies to the **absolute end of the sentence**.

```text
[Position 1: Subject]   [Position 2: Base Verb]   [Middle Information]   [End of sentence: Prefix]
         Ich                     stehe                um 07:00 Uhr                   auf.
         Er                      ruft                 seinen Arzt                    an.
         Wir                     holen                dich am Bahnhof                ab.
```

Here are the essential separable verbs you will hear and use daily in Germany:

| Prefix | Verb | Meaning | Everyday Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **an-** | **anrufen** | to call on phone | *Ich **rufe** dich morgen **an**.* |
| **an-** | **anfangen** | to begin / start | *Der Kurs **fängt** um 18 Uhr **an**.* |
| **an-** | **ankommen** | to arrive | *Der Zug **kommt** um 15:30 Uhr **an**.* |
| **ab-** | **abholen** | to pick up | *Ich **hole** dich am Flughafen **ab**.* |
| **ab-** | **abfahren** | to depart (train/bus) | *Der Bus **fährt** gleich **ab**.* |
| **auf-** | **aufstehen** | to get up (out of bed) | *Wann **stehst** du morgens **auf**?* |
| **auf-** | **aufmachen** | to open (doors/windows)| *Bitte **machen** Sie das Fenster **auf**.* |
| **zu-** | **zumachen** | to close / shut | *Er **macht** die Tür **zu**.* |
| **ein-** | **einkaufen** | to shop for groceries | *Wir **kaufen** am Samstag **ein**.* |
| **ein-** | **einladen** | to invite | *Ich **lade** dich zum Geburtstag **ein**.* |
| **mit-** | **mitbringen** | to bring along | *Ich **bringe** einen Kuchen **mit**.* |
| **mit-** | **mitkommen** | to come along / join | ***Kommst** du heute **mit**?* |

| **auf-** | **aufräumen** | to tidy up | *Am Samstag **räumen** wir die Wohnung **auf**.* |

## 3. The Big Trap: What Happens with Modal Verbs?

In Chapter 3.6, you learned that when you use a modal verb (*können, müssen, wollen*), the second verb goes to the end of the sentence in its basic dictionary form (the infinitive).

**When you combine a modal verb with a separable verb, the verb DOES NOT SPLIT!** It stays glued together at the end of the sentence:

* Normal sentence (splits): *Ich **stehe** um 6 Uhr **auf**.*
* With modal verb (glued!): *Ich muss um 6 Uhr **aufstehen**.*
* Normal sentence (splits): *Er **ruft** den Arzt **an**.*
* With modal verb (glued!): *Er möchte den Arzt **anrufen**.*

*The Mental Model:* The modal verb in Position 2 is the boss. Because the modal verb does the conjugating, the separable verb stays whole in its dictionary form at the end.

## 4. Real-Life Germany: Public Transport Announcements

On German trains and platforms, separable verbs are everywhere in announcements:
* *„Der ICE 574 nach Hamburg **fährt** jetzt **ab**.“* (abfahren = to depart)
* *„Der Zug aus München **kommt** auf Gleis 4 **an**.“* (ankommen = to arrive)
* *„Nächster Halt: Hauptbahnhof. Bitte **steigen** Sie in Fahrtrichtung rechts **aus**.“* (aussteigen = to exit/disembark)

### Common mistakes
| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Ich aufstehe um 7 Uhr.* | *Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf**.* | Prefix goes to the end. |
| *Ich rufe an meine Mutter.* | *Ich **rufe** meine Mutter **an**.* | The prefix goes to the very end, after the object. |
| *Ich kann dich um 8 Uhr holen ab.* | *Ich kann dich um 8 Uhr **abholen**.* | With a modal verb, the verb stays together at the end. |
| *Wann fährt ab der Zug?* | *Wann **fährt** der Zug **ab**?* | Also in questions: prefix at the end. |
| *Ich besuche meine Oma be.* | *Ich **besuche** meine Oma.* | *be-* is not separable. |
