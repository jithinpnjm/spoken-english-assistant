# 4.6 City & Public Places (Stadt und Orte)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can name the key public buildings and services in a German town or city.
* I can ask where a facility is located (*Wo ist hier eine Apotheke?*).
* I know how to use **es gibt** (there is / there are) with the **Accusative case**.
* I understand the difference between a bank (*die Bank*) and a public bench (*die Bank*).

---

## 1. Key Public Buildings & Services in Germany

Learn every place **with its article** – you need it for *Wo ist der/die/das …?*, for *es gibt einen/eine/ein …* and for
*zum/zur …* (section 4).

## 2. Asking Where Something Is

When you arrive in a new German city or neighborhood, you will constantly need to ask passersby for locations:

### Formula 1: "Wo ist...?" (Where is the specific place?)
* *„Entschuldigung, wo ist **der** Bahnhof?“* (Where is the central station?)
* *„Wo ist **das** Rathaus?“* (Where is the town hall?)

### Formula 2: "Gibt es hier...?" (Is there a... around here?)
When you don't know if one exists nearby:
* *„Entschuldigung, gibt es hier in der Nähe **eine Apotheke**?“* (Is there a pharmacy nearby?)
* *„Gibt es hier **einen Geldautomaten**?“* (Is there an ATM here?)

---

## 3. The Grammar Rule: "Es gibt" + Accusative

In German, **es gibt** means both *"there is"* and *"there are"*. 
No matter whether you are talking about one item or twenty items, the phrase **es gibt** NEVER changes!

**The rule:** The noun that follows *es gibt* is **in the Accusative Case**:
* Masculine (*der Bahnhof*): *Hier gibt es **einen** Bahnhof.* (Adds *-en*!)
* Feminine (*die Bank*): *Hier gibt es **eine** Bank.* (Unchanged).
* Neutral (*das Museum*): *Hier gibt es **ein** Museum.* (Unchanged).
* Plural (*viele Parks*): *Hier gibt es **viele** Parks.* (Unchanged).

**The negative:** *Hier gibt es **keinen** Supermarkt.* · *Es gibt **keine** Apotheke in der Nähe.* · *Es gibt hier **kein** Kino.*

---

## 3b. Going there: *zum, zur, ins, in die*
When you say where you are **going**, the place changes its little word. At A1, learn these as chunks:

| Where to? | Chunk | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| der-places | **zum** (zu + dem) | *zum Bahnhof, zum Supermarkt, zum Arzt, zum Bäcker* |
| die-places | **zur** (zu + der) | *zur Post, zur Bank, zur Apotheke, zur Arbeit* |
| buildings you go **into** | **ins** (in + das) | *ins Kino, ins Café, ins Museum, ins Krankenhaus* |
| the town centre | **in die Stadt** | *Ich fahre in die Stadt.* |

And where you **are** (location): *am Bahnhof, im Supermarkt, in der Apotheke, bei der Post / beim Arzt*.
The grammar behind this is in [3.13 Prepositions](../vol3-grammar/prepositions.mdx); directions are in [4.7](./directions.mdx).

---

## 4. ⚠️ The Wordplay Trap: "Die Bank" vs. "Die Bank"

The word **die Bank** has two completely different meanings in German:
1. **die Bank** (Plural: *die Banken*) = **The financial institution** (*Deutsche Bank, Sparkasse*).
   * *Ich gehe zur Bank und hole Geld.* (I go to the bank to get money).
2. **die Bank** (Plural: *die Bänke*) = **A park bench to sit on**!
   * *Er sitzt im Park auf einer Bank.* (He sits on a bench in the park).
