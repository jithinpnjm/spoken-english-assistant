# 4.2 Furniture & Household Objects (Möbel und Haushalt)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can name the most common furniture items in each room with their articles.
* I can name essential kitchen and bathroom appliances (*Kühlschrank, Herd, Waschmaschine*).
* I know the difference between something standing upright (**stehen**) and lying flat (**liegen**).
* I can describe where a piece of furniture is in a room using *im* and *auf dem*.

---

## 1. Living Room & Bedroom Furniture (Wohn- und Schlafzimmer)

Notice how knowing your base words from Chapter 1.2 helps with compound words: words ending in **-schrank** are always masculine (**der**), and words ending in **-tisch** are always masculine (**der**)!

### The Living Room (Das Wohnzimmer)
* **das Sofa / die Couch** (die Sofas) — *sofa / couch*
* **der Sessel** (die Sessel) — *armchair*
* **der Fernseher** (die Fernseher) — *television / TV*
* **der Couchtisch** (die Couchtische) — *coffee table*
* **das Regal** (die Regale) — *bookshelf / shelving unit*
* **der Teppich** (die Teppiche) — *carpet / rug*
* **die Lampe** (die Lampen) — *lamp*

### The Bedroom (Das Schlafzimmer)
* **das Bett** (die Betten) — *bed*
* **der Kleiderschrank** (die Kleiderschränke) — *wardrobe / clothes closet*
* **das Kopfkissen** (die Kopfkissen) — *pillow*
* **die Bettdecke** (die Bettdecken) — *blanket / duvet*
* **der Nachttisch** (die Nachttische) — *bedside table*

## 3. Position Verbs: "stehen" vs. "liegen"

When describing where furniture or objects are, Germans rarely just say "is". They describe the physical posture:

* **stehen** = To stand upright (chairs, tables, wardrobes, bottles, refrigerators).
  * *Der Kühlschrank **steht** in der Küche.* (The fridge stands in the kitchen.)
  * *Der Tisch **steht** am Fenster.* (The table stands by the window.)
* **liegen** = To lie flat (books, carpets, blankets, keys).
  * *Der Teppich **liegt** auf dem Boden.* (The carpet lies on the floor.)
  * *Das Buch **liegt** auf dem Tisch.* (The book lies on the table.)
* **hängen** = To hang (pictures, lamps, mirrors).
  * *Die Lampe **hängt** über dem Tisch.* (The lamp hangs over the table.)

## 4. Real-Life Germany: The Unfurnished Kitchen (Die Einbauküche)

One of the biggest shocks for newcomers moving to Germany is the **EBK (Einbauküche)**:
* In many rental listings in Germany, an apartment is rented completely empty without a kitchen! That means there is no sink, no stove, and no cupboards.
* When looking at apartment ads, always look for the magical three letters: **mit EBK** (with fitted kitchen). 
* If it says **ohne EBK**, you must buy and install the kitchen cabinets and appliances yourself or purchase them from the previous tenant (*der Nachmieter*).
