## Masterclass 1: Exam Architecture, Timing, Scoring & Answer-Sheet (*Antwortbogen S30*) Strategy

### 1.1 What Is *telc Deutsch A1 (Start Deutsch 1)*?

**telc** (*The European Language Certificates*) is one of Europe's leading non-profit language testing organizations, headquartered in Bad Homburg, Germany. Together with the Goethe-Institut, telc co-developed the official **Start Deutsch 1** examination standard aligned with level **A1** of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (**CEFR / GER**).

Because both institutions built the underlying *Start Deutsch 1* test specifications together, the task formats in **telc Deutsch A1** and **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1** are closely related. However, telc administers its own test papers, uses its own optical answer sheet (**Antwortbogen S30**), and reports your result on its own **60-point scale**.

#### Common Uses of the Certificate (and What to Verify)
* **Spouse & Family Reunification Visa (*Ehegattennachzug*):** Under German residence law, applicants joining a spouse in Germany normally need proof of basic German at level A1. Both telc and Goethe A1 certificates are widely used for this purpose. However, the German embassy, consulate, or local immigration office (*Ausländerbehörde*) handling your file makes the final decision on which certificates and test centers it accepts—always check the current checklist on your specific mission's website before booking.
* **Personal Milestone & Employer Proof:** Many learners take telc A1 at a local *Volkshochschule (VHS)* or licensed language school to certify their foundational proficiency before moving on to A2.
* **What A1 Is NOT Enough For:** Level A1 is the first rung on the ladder. It is **not** sufficient for naturalization (*Einbürgerung*, which generally requires B1), most permanent settlement permits (*Niederlassungserlaubnis*), or regulated healthcare and academic admissions (which require B2 or C1).

### 1.2 Complete Official Timetable & Module Breakdown

The **telc Deutsch A1 (Start Deutsch 1)** examination consists of two mandatory parts: a **Written Examination (*Schriftliche Prüfung*)** and an **Oral Examination (*Mündliche Prüfung*)**. You cannot take a partial module exam (*keine Teilprüfung*)—you must sit both the written and oral parts in the same examination session.

#### Part A: *Schriftliche Prüfung* (Written Exam)
* **Active Task Time:** Approximately **65 minutes**
* **Official Timetable Slot:** **75 minutes** (which includes roughly 10 minutes of administrative formalities, identity verification, and filling out personal details on the *Antwortbogen S30*).
* **Break Policy:** There is **no break** (*keine Pause*) between Listening, Reading, and Writing.

* **Duration:** Approximately **15 minutes** for a group of **up to 4 candidates** (if there are fewer candidates in your slot, the time adjusts slightly).
* **Preparation Time:** **Zero minutes** (*keine Vorbereitungszeit*). You go straight into the examination room.
* **Examiners:** Usually **2 licensed telc examiners**—one acts as the interlocutor (*Prüfer 1*) who guides the tasks and speaks with you, while the second observes and scores quietly (*Prüfer 2*).

### 1.3 Official telc 60-Point Scoring System & Grade Bands

According to the official **telc Start Deutsch 1 Übungstest 1**, telc evaluates and reports the examination on a **60-point scale**, divided equally across the four skills:

* **Hören (Listening):** 15 points maximum (25% of total score)
* **Lesen (Reading):** 15 points maximum (25% of total score)
* **Schreiben (Writing):** 15 points maximum (25% of total score — 5 points for *Teil 1* Formular + 10 points for *Teil 2* Brief/E-Mail)
* **Sprechen (Speaking):** 15 points maximum (25% of total score — 3 points for *Teil 1* + 6 points for *Teil 2* + 6 points for *Teil 3*)
* **Total Maximum Score:** **60 points** (Notice that half-points like *0,5* can occur in Writing and Speaking evaluation!).

#### Official telc Grade Table (*Notenstufen*)

| Total Points Achieved (out of 60) | Percentage Equivalent | Official telc Grade (*Prädikat*) | Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **54,0 – 60,0 Punkte** | 90% – 100% | **sehr gut** (very good) | ✅ **Bestanden** (Passed) |
| **48,0 – 53,5 Punkte** | 80% – 89% | **gut** (good) | ✅ **Bestanden** (Passed) |
| **42,0 – 47,5 Punkte** | 70% – 79% | **befriedigend** (satisfactory) | ✅ **Bestanden** (Passed) |
| **36,0 – 41,5 Punkte** | **60% – 69%** | **ausreichend** (sufficient) | ✅ **Bestanden (Minimum Pass)** |
| **0,0 – 35,5 Punkte** | Under 60% | **teilgenommen** (participated) | ❌ **Nicht bestanden** (Did not pass) |

:::warning Important Note on Passing Rules & Per-Part Minimums
To pass **Start Deutsch 1 / telc Deutsch A1**, you need **at least 36 of 60 points (60%)**. In the official *telc Start Deutsch 1 Übungstest 1* scoring guide, only this overall 36-point threshold is presented on the certificate scale. However, some third-party preparation blogs claim that candidates must also reach 60% separately in the written block and the oral block (which is the rule in certain higher-level B1/B2 telc exams). Because official exam regulations (*Prüfungsordnung*) can be updated by telc, **always verify the current passing rules in the latest official telc *Prüfungsordnung* or ask your test center directly**, and aim for at least 60% in every single module (at least 9 out of 15 points in each skill) so you are 100% safe under any rule!
:::

### 1.4 Side-by-Side Comparison: *telc Deutsch A1* vs. *Goethe-Zertifikat A1*

Many students ask Herr Weber whether they should register for **telc Deutsch A1** or **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1**. Because both tests share the *Start Deutsch 1* heritage, the tasks look remarkably similar—yet the scoring arithmetic on the certificate looks different.

| Feature | **telc Deutsch A1 (Start Deutsch 1)** | **Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Developing Body** | telc gGmbH (Bad Homburg) | Goethe-Institut e.V. (Munich) |
| **CEFR Level** | A1 | A1 |
| **Listening (*Hören*)** | 15 items (Teil 1: 6 MC 2x; Teil 2: 4 R/F 1x; Teil 3: 5 MC 2x) | 15 items (Teil 1: 6 MC 2x; Teil 2: 4 R/F 1x; Teil 3: 5 MC 2x) |
| **Reading (*Lesen*)** | 15 items (Teil 1: 5 R/F emails; Teil 2: 5 A/B ads; Teil 3: 5 R/F signs) | 15 items (Teil 1: 5 R/F emails; Teil 2: 5 A/B ads; Teil 3: 5 R/F signs) |
| **Writing (*Schreiben*)** | Formular (5 items) + Short Message (~30 words, 3 content points) | Formular (5 items) + Short Message (~30 words, 3 content points) |
| **Speaking (*Sprechen*)** | Group of up to 4 (~15 min): Intro + Theme Cards + Request Cards | Group of up to 4 (~15 min): Intro + Theme Cards + Request Cards |
| **Reported Scale on Certificate** | **60-Point Scale** (15 pts per module: 15 + 15 + 15 + 15 = 60) | **100-Point Scale** (Raw 60 points multiplied by 1.66 → 25 pts per module = 100) |
| **Minimum Passing Score** | **36 out of 60 points (60%)** | **60 out of 100 points (60%)** (equivalent to 36 raw points) |
| **Test Availability** | Thousands of licensed *Volkshochschulen (VHS)* and private schools worldwide | Goethe-Institut branches and authorized examination partners worldwide |

**Key Takeaway:** Mathematically, **36 out of 60 points** on telc is the exact same percentage (**60%**) as **60 out of 100 points** on Goethe! If you prepare with this chapter, your skills transfer seamlessly between both exams—just make sure you download the official sample paper (*Übungstest* or *Modellsatz*) for the exact provider you booked.

### 1.5 Crucial Warning: *telc Deutsch A1* vs. *telc Deutsch A1 für Zuwanderer*

Look closely at your booking confirmation email! telc offers **two different A1-level German exams** with similar names:

1. **Start Deutsch 1 / telc Deutsch A1** (The general A1 exam covered in this chapter):
   * Has **4 modules**: *Hören, Lesen, Schreiben, Sprechen*.
   * Does **not** have a separate grammar/vocabulary fill-in-the-blank section (*Sprachbausteine*).
   * Uses the **60-point scale** described above.
2. **telc Deutsch A1 für Zuwanderer** (A specialized variant designed specifically for integration courses in Germany):
   * Includes an extra section called **Sprachbausteine** (grammar and vocabulary gap-fill items inside a letter).
   * Has different timings and point distributions, and the oral exam often takes place in pairs (2 candidates) rather than a group of 4.

**Rule of Thumb:** If you are taking the exam abroad for a visa or taking the standard *Start Deutsch 1 / telc Deutsch A1* at a test center, you are taking the exam covered on this page. If your registration specifically says **für Zuwanderer**, ask your test center for the dedicated *A1 für Zuwanderer* mock test so you also practice the *Sprachbausteine* section.

### 1.6 Mastering the *Antwortbogen S30* (Optical Answer Sheet Strategy)

In the paper-based **telc Deutsch A1** exam, what you write inside your question booklet (*Aufgabenheft*) **does not count** toward your score. Everything is graded by an optical scanner and two human raters reading your **Antwortbogen S30** answer sheet.

Here are Herr Weber's **5 Golden Rules for the Antwortbogen S30**:

1. **Use a Soft Lead Pencil (2B or HB) — Never a Ballpoint Pen for Bubbles:**
   * Official telc instructions require a soft pencil (*Bleistift*) so that if you change your mind, you can erase cleanly (*ausradieren*). Do not bring only a blue or black pen!
2. **How to Mark and Correct Bubbles Properly:**
   * Fill the little oval or box dark and completely (**●**).
   * In *Lesen* and *Hören*, items are marked either **a / b / c** or **+ (Richtig) / – (Falsch)**. Double-check the item number! Item 7 is the first item of *Hören Teil 2*, so you move from three bubbles (a/b/c) to two bubbles (+/–).
   * If you mark two bubbles on the same line and forget to erase the wrong one, the scanner reads it as **0 points**.
3. **The 5-Minute Listening Transfer Window:**
   * While the audio is playing during *Hören*, keep your eyes on your question booklet (*Aufgabenheft*) and circle your answers directly next to the pictures and text.
   * At the end of the *Hören* section (or during the pauses provided on the audio track), transfer your 15 answers cleanly onto the **Antwortbogen S30**. Never lose focus on a live announcement because you were hunting for bubble #8 on the answer sheet!
4. **Don't Leave the Transfer of *Lesen* + *Schreiben* Until Minute 44:**
   * During the 45-minute *Lesen & Schreiben* block, nobody will stop you 5 minutes before the end to say *"Transfer your answers now!"* When the invigilator says *"Stifte weg!"* (Pencils down!), any answer still sitting in your booklet or rough draft is lost forever.
   * **Transfer *Lesen* answers (Items 16–30) and *Schreiben Teil 1* (Formular Items 1–5) onto the Antwortbogen S30 immediately after finishing each section**—before you even start writing your letter in *Schreiben Teil 2*!
5. **Write *Schreiben Teil 2* Directly on the Designated Lined Box of the Answer Sheet (or Leave 8 Full Minutes to Copy):**
   * On page 3 or 4 of the *Antwortbogen S30*, there is a specific lined field for *Schreiben Teil 2*. Plan your 3 bullet points in 90 seconds in the booklet using 3-word notes, then write your clean 30-word email directly onto the *Antwortbogen*.

### 2.1 *Hören Teil 1*: 6 Everyday Dialogues (Played TWICE — Multiple Choice a, b, c)

#### How *Hören Teil 1* Works
* **Number of items:** 6 items (Items 1 to 6), plus an ungraded Example (*Beispiel 0*) played at the very beginning so you can check the room volume.
* **Audio format:** Short, natural two-person dialogues in everyday settings (at a bakery, at a doctor's reception, in a clothing store, in a hotel lobby, at a train ticket counter, or between friends planning an evening).
* **How many times you hear each dialogue:** **TWICE (2x)**.
* **Answer choices:** **a, b, or c** (in the test booklet, these are frequently accompanied by small illustrations—clocks, price tags, food items, clothing, calendar days, or floor numbers).

#### The 3 Classic telc Traps in *Hören Teil 1*

1. **Trap #1: The "First-Mention Distractor" (*Der Erstnennungs-Ablenker*)**
   * Speaker A almost always suggests Option A first. Speaker B then rejects it (*"Nein, das geht leider nicht..."*, *"Heute sind sie im Angebot..."*, *"Leider ist kein Platz mehr frei..."*) and introduces the real answer.
   * **Rule:** Never put your pencil down after the first 5 seconds! Listen for **correction words**: *aber, leider, nein, heute nur, doch lieber, Moment mal, ach so*.
2. **Trap #2: The Price / Quantity Arithmetic Trap**
   * You see three price tags: **a) 1,50 €**, **b) 2,50 €**, **c) 3,00 €**.
   * In the audio, the customer asks for something that costs 1,50 €, then adds a coffee for 1,50 €, and the cashier says: *"Das macht zusammen drei Euro."* Or the regular price is 3,00 €, *aber heute im Sonderangebot für 2,50 €*.
   * **Rule:** Underline the exact question! Does the question ask *"Was kostet der Pullover heute?"* (single item today) or *"Was muss die Frau zusammen bezahlen?"* (total bill)?
3. **Trap #3: The Negation & Word-Order Trap (*nicht, kein, ohne*)**
   * The pictures show: **a) Coffee with milk and sugar**, **b) Coffee with milk, no sugar**, **c) Black coffee**.
   * The speaker mentions all three words (*Kaffee, Milch, Zucker*), saying: *"Einen Kaffee, bitte. Mit etwas Milch, aber bitte **ohne** Zucker."* If you only listen for nouns, you hear *Zucker* and pick **a**. You must listen for **mit** vs. **ohne** and **kein**.

### 2.2 *Hören Teil 2*: 4 Public Announcements (Played ONLY ONCE — *Richtig / Falsch*)

:::danger Critical Exam Alert: *Hören Teil 2* Is Played ONLY ONCE!
In *Hören Teil 2* (Items 7 to 10), you hear **4 short public loudspeaker announcements** (*Durchsagen*) at a train station, airport, department store, or tour boat. Unlike *Teil 1* and *Teil 3*, **each announcement in Teil 2 is played ONLY ONE TIME (1x)**. If your mind wanders for 8 seconds, the question is over.
:::

#### The 5-Second Pre-Listening Keyword Technique
Before each announcement plays, the audio track gives you a brief pause to read the printed statement. Never read passively! Take your pencil and **underline the 2 or 3 high-risk target words** in the statement:
1. **Numbers, Times & Platforms/Gates:** *Gleis 4, Ausgang B, 18:30 Uhr, 2. Stock, 20 Prozent*.
2. **Action Verbs & Status Words:** *kommt später, fährt ab, fällt aus, schließt, wartet*.
3. **Target Group / Location:** *Fahrgäste nach Hamburg, Kinder, Kunden im Erdgeschoss*.

#### The *Hören Teil 2* Synonym & Paraphrase Decoder Table
In *Hören Teil 2*, the printed statement almost never uses the exact same words as the loudspeaker voice. Instead, telc tests whether you recognize everyday German synonyms:

| What You Read in the Statement (*Aufgabenheft*) | What You Hear on the Loudspeaker (*Audio*) | Is It *Richtig* (+) or *Falsch* (–)? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Der Zug kommt später an.* | *„...hat voraussichtlich 25 Minuten **Verspätung**.“* | **Richtig (+)** (*Verspätung* = delay = arrives later) |
| *Der Zug nach Berlin fährt heute nicht.* | *„Der ICE nach Berlin **fällt heute leider aus**.“* | **Richtig (+)** (*fällt aus* = is cancelled = does not run) |
| *Der Zug fährt von Gleis 3.* | *„...fährt heute **nicht** von Gleis 3, **sondern** von Gleis 8.“* | **Falsch (–)** (Platform changed from 3 to 8!) |
| *Sie können noch bis 20 Uhr einkaufen.* | *„Unser Supermarkt **schließt heute bereits um 19 Uhr**.“* | **Falsch (–)** (*schließt bereits um 19 Uhr* = closes early at 7 PM) |
| *Die Passagiere sollen zum Schalter kommen.* | *„...werden gebeten, zum **Informationsschalter** zu kommen.“* | **Richtig (+)** (*werden gebeten* = are requested to) |
| *Man soll im Zug bleiben.* | *„Bitte **alle aussteigen**! Dieser Zug endet hier.“* | **Falsch (–)** (*aussteigen* = get off the train) |

### 2.3 *Hören Teil 3*: 5 Telephone Voicemail Messages (Played TWICE — Multiple Choice a, b, c)

#### How *Hören Teil 3* Works
* **Number of items:** 5 items (Items 11 to 15).
* **Audio format:** 5 monologue messages left on a telephone answering machine (*Anrufbeantworter*) or smartphone mailbox (*Mailbox*). You hear a short telephone beep (*Piepton*) before each message.
* **How many times you hear each message:** **TWICE (2x)**.
* **Answer choices:** **a, b, or c** (testing who called, what changed, which phone number to call back, what to bring, or when an item will be ready).

#### Anatomy of a German Voicemail Message (The 4-Stage Structure)
Almost every voicemail in *Hören Teil 3* follows this exact four-step script:
1. **Greeting & Caller Identity:** *„Hallo Eva, hier ist Stefan...“* or *„Guten Tag Herr Meier, hier spricht Frau Krüger von der Autowerkstatt...“*
2. **The Original Plan or Context (Often Distractor A!):** *„Wir wollten doch heute Abend um 19 Uhr ins Kino gehen...“* or *„Ihre Brille sollte eigentlich am Donnerstag fertig sein...“*
3. **The Problem / Change / Core Message (Usually the Answer!):** *„...aber ich muss heute länger im Büro bleiben. Können wir uns erst um 20:15 Uhr direkt vor dem Restaurant treffen?“*
4. **The Call-Back Number or Action Request (Often tested in a separate item!):** *„Ruf mich bitte unter 0176 - 48 39 20 zurück.“*

#### Special Trap: Phone Number Digit Pairs & Self-Corrections
When a caller leaves a phone number in *Hören Teil 3*, they frequently group digits into pairs (*achtundvierzig – neununddreißig*) OR make a mistake and say: *„...null eins sieben sechs, vier acht, nein Entschuldigung, vier neun...“*. Keep your pencil tip hovering over the three printed numbers during the first play, and verify digit-by-digit on the second play!

## Masterclass 3: Deep Strategy & Worked Rehearsal for *Lesen Teil 1, Teil 2 & Teil 3*

Once the 20-minute *Hören* audio finishes and you have transferred your 15 listening answers to the *Antwortbogen S30*, the invigilator immediately begins the **45-minute combined timer for *Lesen* (Reading) and *Schreiben* (Writing)**.

### 3.1 *Lesen Teil 1*: 2 Short Emails / Notes (5 *Richtig / Falsch* Items)

#### How *Lesen Teil 1* Works
* **Task structure:** You read **2 short everyday texts** (emails, postcards, letters, or house notes from friends, colleagues, or landlords) and evaluate **5 statements** (Items 16 to 20, plus an Example *Beispiel 0*) as **Richtig (+)** or **Falsch (–)**.
* **Sequential Order Secret:** Within each email, the questions almost always follow the chronological order of the paragraphs! Statement 16 is usually answered in sentences 1–3 of Email 1; Statement 17 in the middle; Statement 18 near the end.

#### The High-Frequency *Lesen Teil 1* Synonym & Paraphrase Table
In *Lesen Teil 1*, if a statement uses the exact same words as the email, watch out for a negation or time change. When a statement is **Richtig (+)**, telc almost always uses a **synonym or paraphrase**:

| Expression in the Email / Letter | Paraphrase in the Exam Statement | Logical Relationship |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *am Samstag und am Sonntag* | *am **Wochenende*** | Equivalent (**Richtig**) |
| *mit dem Zug / mit dem ICE* | *mit der **Bahn*** | Equivalent (**Richtig**) |
| *um 10:00 Uhr morgens* | ***vormittags*** (in the morning / forenoon) | Equivalent (**Richtig**) |
| *um 15:00 Uhr* | ***nachmittags*** (in the afternoon) | Equivalent (**Richtig**) |

### 3.2 *Lesen Teil 2*: 5 Website / Newspaper Ad Comparisons (*"Wo finden Sie Informationen?"* — Choose **a** or **b**)

#### How *Lesen Teil 2* Works
* **Task structure:** Items 21 to 25 (5 items, plus an Example *Beispiel 0*).
* **What you see:** For each item, you read a **one-sentence situation** describing what you or an acquaintance want to do, followed by **two short website snippets or classified ads (Option a and Option b)**.
* **Your goal:** Decide which of the two ads (**a** or **b**) matches every condition in the situation!

#### The 15-Second Binary Elimination Technique
Never read both websites from top to bottom like a novel! Both Website **a** and Website **b** are always on the exact same topic (for example, both are about bicycles, both are about language schools, or both are about apartments). The difference always lies in **two contrasting conditions**:

| Typical Contrast Axis | Website / Ad A | Website / Ad B | How to Spot It in 5 Seconds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Action Type** | **Kaufen / Verkaufen** (Buying / Selling new or used items) | **Reparieren / Mieten / Leihen** (Repairing or renting items) | Check the verbs and nouns: *Verkauf, Preis, neu* vs. *Werkstatt, Reparatur, Verleih, pro Tag*. |
| **2. Time / Day / Season** | **Abends / Am Wochenende / Im Winter** | **Vormittags / Werktags (Mo–Fr) / Im Sommer** | Scan for days (*Mo–Fr* vs. *Sa/So*), clock times (*18–21 Uhr* vs. *9–12 Uhr*), or months (*Mai–September* vs. *Dezember–März*). |
| **3. Target Audience** | **Für Kinder / Jugendliche / Familien** | **Nur für Erwachsene / Senioren / Studenten** | Scan for age numbers (*ab 6 Jahren, bis 14 Jahre*) or nouns (*Kinderprogramm, Erwachsenenkurse*). |
| **4. Price / Budget** | **Kostenlos / Günstig / Unter 50 €** | **Teuer / Luxus / Ab 120 €** | Compare the euro symbol **€** or words like *Eintritt frei, gratis, preiswert*. |
| **5. Location / Direction** | **Im Stadtzentrum / In der Innenstadt** | **Am See / Auf dem Land / Am Flughafen** | Scan the address line or location phrases (*Zentrum, Marktplatz* vs. *außerhalb, im Grünen*). |

### 3.3 *Lesen Teil 3*: 5 Public Signs & Door Notices (*Richtig / Falsch*)

#### How *Lesen Teil 3* Works
* **Task structure:** Items 26 to 30 (5 items, plus an Example *Beispiel 0*).
* **What you see:** A short public sign, door notice, opening-hours plaque, or station rule (e.g., *An der Tür einer Arztpraxis*, *Am Eingang zum Schwimmbad*, *Im Bahnhof*), followed by a single statement.
* **Your goal:** Decide whether the statement is **Richtig (+)** or **Falsch (–)**.

#### The Qualifier Trap Dictionary (*Die 10 wichtigsten Signalwörter für Schilder*)
In *Lesen Teil 3*, the whole point of the question almost always hangs on **one single qualifier word** on the sign. Memorize this table before exam day:

| German Signal Word on Sign | Exact English Meaning | Classic Exam Example & Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **nur** | **only** | *Parken **nur** für Kunden* → Only customers may park here (visitors/others may NOT). |
| **erst ab** | **not until / only starting from** | *Warme Küche **erst ab** 18:00 Uhr* → You CANNOT get hot food at 17:00 Uhr! |
| **bis** | **until / up to** | *Urlau**b bis** 15. August* → Closed until Aug 15; open again after. |
| **außer** | **except (for)** | *Täglich geöffnet, **außer** montags* → Open every day **except** Monday (closed on Monday!). |
| **wegen (+ Gen/Dat)** | **because of / due to** | ***Wegen** Umbau geschlossen* → Closed right now due to renovation. |
| **werktags** | **working days (Mon–Sat in Germany, NOT Sunday!)** | *Werktags 8–18 Uhr* → Does NOT include Sunday (*Sonntag*) or public holidays (*Feiertage*)! |
| **täglich** | **daily (every day, Mon–Sun)** | *Täglich 9–20 Uhr* → Includes Saturday and Sunday! |
| **frei** | **free of charge / vacant** | *Für Kinder unter 6 Jahren Eintritt **frei*** → Children under 6 pay **0 €** (nothing). |
| **verboten / untersagt** | **prohibited / forbidden** | *Rauchen und Fotografieren **verboten*** → You must **not** smoke or take photos. |
| **klingeln / melden** | **ring the bell / report to** | *Bitte im 1. Stock **melden*** → Go to the 1st floor reception first. |
