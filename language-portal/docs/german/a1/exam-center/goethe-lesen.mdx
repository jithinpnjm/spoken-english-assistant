# 10.1 Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Reading (Lesen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I understand the official structure, timing, and point distribution of the Goethe A1 Reading exam.
* I can solve **Teil 1** (private emails & notes — True/False).
* I can solve **Teil 2** (matching needs to website ads — A or B).
* I can solve **Teil 3** (reading public signs, door notices, and flyers — True/False).
* I know how to avoid "keyword traps" where the same word appears in the wrong option.

---

## 1. Exam Structure & Timing: The 25-Minute Overview

The Reading part (**Lesen**) takes about **25 minutes** (Lesen and Schreiben together about 45 minutes) and has **15 items** in 3 parts:

| Section | Text Types | Number of Items | Question Type | Time Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Teil 1** | 2 short texts (e.g. personal messages, emails) | 5 items (1–5) | Richtig / Falsch | ~7 minutes |
| **Teil 2** | 5 situations, each with two short ads or web snippets | 5 items (6–10) | "Wo finden Sie Informationen?" – a or b | ~10 minutes |
| **Teil 3** | 5 short signs and notices | 5 items (11–15) | Richtig / Falsch | ~8 minutes |

*Source:* official Goethe A1 Modellsatz. The time recommendations are our suggestion, not official rules. *Scoring:* Hören, Lesen and Schreiben together are worth 75 of the 100 points; you pass the whole exam with at least 60 points.

## 2. Teil 1: Personal Emails & Letters (Items 1–5)

You read two short texts (for example personal messages or emails) from friends or acquaintances and evaluate statements as **Richtig** (True) or **Falsch** (False).

### 🔍 Strategy:
1. **Read the question statement FIRST before reading the text!** Underline key words (names, dates, actions).
2. **Watch out for Negations:** The text might say *„Ich kann leider **nicht** kommen“*, while the statement says *„Er kommt zur Party.“* ➔ **Falsch!**

### Sample Teil 1 Task:
> **Text:**  
> *Liebe Sandra,*  
> *ich habe für Samstag zwei Kinokarten gekauft. Der Film fängt um 20 Uhr an. Wir können uns um 19:30 Uhr vor dem Kino treffen und vorher noch schnell etwas trinken. Hast du Zeit? Ruf mich bitte an!*  
> *Dein Markus*

*Statement 1:* Markus möchte mit Sandra ins Kino gehen.  
➔ **Richtig** (He bought 2 tickets and asks her to join).  
*Statement 2:* Der Film beginnt um 19:30 Uhr.  
➔ **Falsch** (The movie starts at 20:00; they are meeting at 19:30!).

## 3. Teil 2: Website Matching (Items 6–10)

You are given a specific scenario (what a person is looking for) and two website ads (**Website A** and **Website B**). You must select which website offers the correct solution.

### 🔍 Strategy:
* **The "Keyword Trap":** The same word can appear in both options – only one actually fits the need.
  * If a person wants to *buy a cheap used bicycle*, Website A might say *„Fahrradverleih“* (bicycle rental) and Website B might say *„Gebrauchte Fahrräder zu verkaufen“* (used bikes for sale). 
  * If you only scan for the word "Fahrrad", you will guess randomly. You must understand the verb (*kaufen* vs. *leihen*).

## 4. Teil 3: Public Notices & Signs (Items 11–15)

You read 5 short signs from daily life: on shop doors, in supermarkets, on train station walls, or in doctor's waiting rooms.

### Typical Signs (original practice texts):
* **Sign on clinic door:**  
  *„Praxis Dr. Weber: Die Praxis ist vom 01. bis 15. August wegen Urlaub geschlossen. Vertretung: Dr. Sommer.“*  
  *Statement:* Am 10. August kann man zu Dr. Weber in die Praxis gehen. ➔ **Falsch** (Closed 1–15 August; patients go to the substitute, Dr. Sommer).
* **Sign in supermarket:**  
  *„Sehr geehrte Kunden, Kartenzahlung heute wegen technischer Störung leider nicht möglich.“*  
  *Statement:* Man kann heute nur mit Bargeld bezahlen. ➔ **Richtig** (Card terminal down = cash only!).
