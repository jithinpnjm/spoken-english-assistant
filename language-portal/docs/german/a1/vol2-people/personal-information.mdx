# 2.1 Asking for Personal Information (Persönliche Informationen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can ask someone their name, origin, city, languages, and profession.
* I can formulate open questions using German "W-Words" (*Wer, Wie, Wo, Woher, Was*).
* I know that the verb must always take **Position 2** in a question with a question word.
* I can read and understand the standard fields on German forms (*Wohnort, PLZ, Geburtsdatum*).

---

## 1. Moving from Statements to Questions

In Volume 1, you learned how to state facts about yourself:
* *Ich heiße Maria.* (I am called Maria.)
* *Ich komme aus Italien.* (I come from Italy.)

In real life, however, communication is a two-way street. To make friends, network with colleagues, or handle administrative appointments, you must know **how to ask the questions**.

German questions with question words are called **W-Fragen** because, just like in English (Who, What, Where), they all start with the letter **W**.

---

## 2. The Core W-Question Words

| German Word | English Meaning | What it asks for | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wer?** | Who? | Person | *Wer ist das?* (Who is that?) |
| **Wie?** | How / What? | Name, condition, age | *Wie heißen Sie?* / *Wie alt bist du?* |
| **Wo?** | Where? | Static location / city | *Wo wohnst du?* (Where do you live?) |
| **Woher?** | Where from? | Origin / country | *Woher kommen Sie?* (Where do you come from?) |
| **Was?** | What? | Object, profession | *Was sind Sie von Beruf?* (What is your profession?) |
| **Welche?** | Which? | Specific choices | *Welche Sprachen sprichst du?* (Which languages do you speak?) |

---

## 3. The Golden Rule of German Questions: Verb in Position 2

When you ask a question using a W-Word, the sentence structure is completely rigid:

```text
[Position 1: Question Word]   [Position 2: Conjugated Verb]   [Position 3: Subject]   [Rest of sentence]
           Woher                          kommen                       Sie?
            Wo                            wohnst                        du?
           Was                             sind                         Sie                 von Beruf?
```

*The Golden Rule:* **In a W-question the conjugated verb is in Position 2**, right after the question word. Don't put the subject before the verb (❌ *Woher Sie kommen?* is ungrammatical; it must be ✅ *Woher kommen Sie?*).

### Contrast: W-Questions vs. Yes/No Questions (Ja/Nein-Fragen)
What happens if you don't use a question word like *Wo* or *Wer*?
In a **Yes/No question**, the **verb jumps to Position 1**:
* W-Question: *„Wo **wohnen** Sie?“* (Verb in Position 2)
* Yes/No Question: *„**Wohnen** Sie in Berlin?“* (Verb in Position 1! Answer: *„Ja, ich wohne in Berlin.“*)
* W-Question: *„Welche Sprachen **sprichst** du?“* (Verb in Position 2)
* Yes/No Question: *„**Sprichst** du Englisch?“* (Verb in Position 1! Answer: *„Ja, natürlich.“*)

---

## 4. Comparing Formal (Sie) and Informal (du) Questions

Notice how the verb ending changes from **-en** (with *Sie*) to **-st** (with *du*):

| Topic | Informal Question (with a friend/child) | Formal Question (with a stranger/official) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Name** | *Wie heißt **du**?* | *Wie heißen **Sie**?* |
| **Origin** | *Woher kommst **du**?* | *Woher kommen **Sie**?* |
| **Residence** | *Wo wohnst **du**?* | *Wo wohnen **Sie**?* |
| **Languages** | *Welche Sprachen sprichst **du**?* | *Welche Sprachen sprechen **Sie**?* |
| **Profession** | *Was bist **du** von Beruf?* | *Was sind **Sie** von Beruf?* |
| **Age** | *Wie alt bist **du**?* | *Wie alt sind **Sie**?* |

---

## 5. Real-Life Germany: Reading German Forms (Formulare)

In Germany, you will fill out forms at the doctor's office, bank, gym, and city hall. They use specific bureaucratic nouns rather than full questions:

* **der Vorname:** First name (e.g. *Jithin*)
* **der Nachname / Familienname:** Last name / Surname (e.g. *Joseph*)
* **das Geburtsdatum:** Date of birth (written DD.MM.YYYY)
* **das Geburtsland / Herkunftsland:** Country of birth / origin
* **der Wohnort:** City of residence *(⚠️ Danger: Do not write your street address here! This means ONLY the city, e.g. Frankfurt)*
* **die Straße und Hausnummer:** Street name and house number
* **die PLZ (Postleitzahl):** 5-digit German postal code (e.g. *60311*)
* **der Familienstand:** Marital status (*ledig* = single, *verheiratet* = married, *geschieden* = divorced)
