# 2.3 Describing People (Personen beschreiben)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can describe what someone looks like (height, age, hair, eyes).
* I know when to use **sein** (to be) and when to use **haben** (to have).
* I can use the separable verb **aussehen** to ask *"What does he/she look like?"*.
* I understand that German usually talks about hair in the plural (*die Haare*).
* I can describe myself so someone can find me at a station or airport.

---

## 1. Why Describing People Matters in Daily Life

In daily life in Germany, you often need to describe someone:
* Meeting a new colleague or tandem partner at the train station (*„Wie erkenne ich dich?“* — How do I recognize you?).
* Describing your child to a kindergarten teacher (*„Er hat blonde Haare...“*).
* Describing someone to a friend.

To do this, German uses **three different verbs** that beginners often mix up.

---

## 2. The Three Verbs of Description

| Verb | When to use it | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **sein** (to be) | With general adjectives (height, age, build, character) | *Er **ist** groß und freundlich.* (He is tall and friendly.) |
| **haben** (to have) | With physical features (hair, eyes, beard) | *Sie **hat** braune Augen.* (She has brown eyes.) |
| **aussehen** (to look / appear) | Overall impression or asking questions | *Wie **sieht** er **aus**?* (What does he look like?) |

### The "aussehen" Rule
*Aussehen* is a **separable verb**. In a normal sentence, the prefix **aus** breaks off and flies to the very end:
* *„Wie **sieht** sie **aus**?“* (What does she look like?)
* *„Er **sieht** sehr sympathisch **aus**.“* (He looks very likeable.)

---

## 3. Vocabulary: Adjectives in Contrasts

Learning adjectives in opposing pairs makes them twice as easy to remember:

### Height, Age & Build
* **groß** (tall / big)  ⟷  **klein** (short / small)
* **jung** (young)  ⟷  **alt** (old)
* **schlank** (slim)  ⟷  **kräftig** (sturdy / strong build)
* *Note:* *Er ist 1,80 Meter groß.* (He is 1.80 meters tall).

### Hair & Eyes (Haare und Augen)
⚠️ **Grammar Trap:** In English, hair is singular (*"Her hair **is** long"*). In everyday German, people usually use the **plural** *die Haare*:
* ✅ *Sie hat **lange Haare**.* (most common)
* ✅ *Sie hat langes Haar.* (also correct, a little more formal)
* ⚠️ *Ihre Haare **sind** lang.* – with the plural, the verb is plural too (not *ist*).

Common hair and eye descriptions:
* **kurze / lange Haare** (short / long hair)
* **blonde / braune / schwarze / graue Haare** (blonde / brown / black / grey hair)
* **blaue / braune / grüne Augen** (blue / brown / green eyes)
* **eine Brille** (glasses): *Er trägt eine Brille.* (He wears glasses).

### Personality Traits (Der Charakter)
* **nett / freundlich** (nice / friendly)  ⟷  **unfreundlich** (unfriendly)
* **sympathisch** (pleasant / likeable)
* **ruhig** (calm / quiet)  ⟷  **aktiv** (energetic / active)

---

## 4. Real-Life Germany: Picking Someone up at the Station

When meeting someone at a train station or airport for the first time, Germans send a brief, practical description:

> *„Hallo Thomas! Ich stehe am Gleis 7 vor dem Bäcker. Ich bin ca. 1,75 m groß, habe kurze dunkle Haare und trage eine blaue Jacke und eine schwarze Brille.“*

Notice how practical and objective the description is: height, hair, clothing color, glasses.
