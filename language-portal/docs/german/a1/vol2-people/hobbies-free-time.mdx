# 2.5 Hobbies & Free Time (Freizeit und Hobbys)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can talk about my hobbies and what I like doing in my free time.
* I know how to use the adverb **gern** to say "I like to [verb]".
* I can use frequency words (*immer, oft, manchmal, nie*) to say how often I do an activity.
* I can pair hobby nouns with the correct verbs (*Sport machen, Tennis spielen, spazieren gehen*).
* I understand the German tradition of joining a club (*Verein*) and the Sunday walk (*Spaziergang*).

---

## 1. What are Hobbies in German and Why Does "gern" Matter?

In English, you use the verb "to like" for almost everything:
* *"I **like** pizza."* (Liking a thing)
* *"I **like to** swim."* (Liking an activity)

German makes a strict distinction:
To say that you enjoy doing an **action**, you do **not** use a verb for "like". Instead, you simply state the action and add the magic adverb **gern** (gladly / with pleasure) right after the verb:

* *Ich schwimme.* (I swim.)
* ➔ *Ich schwimme **gern**.* (I like to swim.)
* *Ich koche.* (I cook.)
* ➔ *Ich koche **gern**.* (I like to cook.)

If you don't like doing it, simply put **nicht gern**:
* *Ich koche **nicht gern**.* (I don't like to cook.)

---

## 2. Common Hobby Verbs & Collocations

In German, hobbies are almost always expressed with specific verb-noun pairs (collocations):

| German Activity | English Translation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sport machen / treiben** | to do sports / exercise | *Ich mache dreimal pro Woche Sport.* |
| **Fußball / Tennis spielen** | to play football / tennis | *Spielst du gern Fußball?* |
| **spazieren gehen** | to go for a walk | *Wir gehen sonntags im Park spazieren.* |
| **Musik hören** | to listen to music | *Ich höre beim Kochen Musik.* |
| **Freunde treffen** | to meet friends *(Irregular: du triffst, er trifft)* | *Am Freitag treffe ich meine Freunde.* |
| **lesen** | to read *(Irregular: du liest, er liest)* | *Liest du gern Bücher oder Krimis?* |
| **reisen** | to travel | *Meine Eltern reisen sehr gern.* |
| **kochen / backen** | to cook / to bake | *Ich koche gern indisch und italienisch.* |

## 3. How Often Do You Do It? Frequency Adverbs

To explain your lifestyle, place a frequency word directly after the conjugated verb:

```text
immer (always)  ➔  oft (often)  ➔  manchmal (sometimes)  ➔  selten (rarely)  ➔  nie (never)
```

### Examples:
* *Ich spiele **oft** Tennis.* (I often play tennis.)
* *Er geht **manchmal** ins Kino.* (He sometimes goes to the cinema.)
* *Wir sehen **selten** fern.* (We rarely watch TV.)
* *Ich trinke **nie** Alkohol.* (I never drink alcohol.)

*Word order tip:* When you combine frequency with *gern*, frequency usually comes first:
* *„Ich koche **oft gern** mit Freunden.“* (I often like cooking with friends.)

## 4. Real-Life Germany: "Vereine" & Der Sonntagsspaziergang

### 1. The Club Culture (Der Verein)
In Germany, more than 40% of the population belongs to at least one *Verein* (registered non-profit club). There are sports clubs (*Sportvereine*), choir clubs (*Gesangvereine*), hiking clubs (*Wandervereine*), and board game clubs. If you are an expat in Germany, joining a local *Verein* is considered the single best way to make German friends.

### 2. Der Sonntagsspaziergang
Because most shops and supermarkets are closed in Germany on Sundays, the traditional Sunday walk in the park or forest (*der Sonntagsspaziergang*) is an institution. Asking a colleague on Monday:
> *„Haben Sie am Wochenende einen Spaziergang gemacht?“* (Did you go for a walk on the weekend?)
is standard friendly small talk.
