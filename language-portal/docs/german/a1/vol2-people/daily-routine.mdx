# 2.4 Daily Routine & Separable Verbs (Der Tagesablauf)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can describe what I do throughout the day from morning to night.
* I can use recurring time words (*morgens, abends, nachts*).
* I understand how separable verbs (*aufstehen, anrufen, fernsehen*) split in a sentence.
* I can sequence my actions using *zuerst* (first), *dann* (then), and *danach* (afterwards).
* I understand the German cultural concepts of *Feierabend* and *Ruhezeit*.

---

## 1. Times of the Day: What Happens When

When describing a daily routine, German adds a lowercase **-s** to the time of day. This means "regularly" or "every day at that time":

* **morgens** = in the morning (e.g. 06:00 – 10:00)
* **vormittags** = in the late morning (e.g. 10:00 – 12:00)
* **mittags** = at noon / lunchtime (e.g. 12:00 – 14:00)
* **nachmittags** = in the afternoon (e.g. 14:00 – 18:00)
* **abends** = in the evening (e.g. 18:00 – 22:00)
* **nachts** = at night (e.g. 22:00 – 06:00)

*Examples:*
* *Morgens trinke ich Kaffee.* (In the mornings, I drink coffee.)
* *Abends koche ich mit meiner Frau.* (In the evenings, I cook with my wife.)

## 2. The Grammar: Separable Verbs (Trennbare Verben)

Many everyday German verbs have a small prefix glued to the front:
* **auf** + *stehen* = **aufstehen** (to get up / wake up)
* **an** + *rufen* = **anrufen** (to call on the phone)
* **fern** + *sehen* = **fernsehen** (to watch TV)
* **ein** + *kaufen* = **einkaufen** (to go grocery shopping)

### The Rule of the Detaching Prefix
When you conjugate a separable verb in a normal sentence, **the verb splits in two**:
1. The **main base verb** changes to match the person and sits in **Position 2**.
2. The **prefix** detaches and flies to the **very end** of the sentence!

```text
[Position 1]   [Position 2: Base Verb]   [Middle Details]   [End of Clause: Prefix]
     Ich                stehe             um 07:00 Uhr                auf.
     Er                 ruft              seine Mutter                an.
     Wir                kaufen            im Supermarkt               ein.
```

*Mental model:* Think of the prefix as an anchor. It drops to the end of the clause.

### Routine words with articles
| Word | Plural | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **das Frühstück** | die Frühstücke (rare) | breakfast | *Zum Frühstück esse ich Müsli.* |
| **das Mittagessen** | die Mittagessen | lunch | *Das Mittagessen ist um zwölf.* |
| **das Abendessen** | die Abendessen | dinner | *Wir kochen das Abendessen zusammen.* |
| **die Arbeit** | die Arbeiten | work | *Ich fahre mit dem Rad zur Arbeit.* |

## 3. Sequencing Your Day (Zuerst, Dann, Danach)

To connect your actions into a smooth story rather than disjointed sentences, use connectors:
* **zuerst** = *first / to begin with*
* **dann** = *then*
* **danach** = *after that / afterwards*

### ⚠️ The Golden V2 Inversion Rule:
When you start a sentence with a time word or connector (*Morgens, Dann, Danach*), that word takes **Position 1**. 
**The verb MUST still be in Position 2!** That means the subject (*ich, er, wir*) swaps places and sits directly after the verb:

* Normal: *Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.*
* Starting with time: *Morgens **stehe ich** um 7 Uhr auf.* (Verb in Position 2!)
* ❌ *Morgens ich stehe auf.* (Ungrammatical word order)

---

## 4. A Complete Everyday Routine

Here is a natural narrative of a typical German weekday:

> *„Ich stehe morgens um 06:30 Uhr **auf**. Zuerst dusche ich und frühstücke. Um 08:00 Uhr fahre ich mit der Bahn zur Arbeit. Von 08:30 bis 17:00 Uhr arbeite ich im Büro. Mittags esse ich mit Kollegen in der Kantine. Um 17:00 Uhr habe ich **Feierabend**. Danach kaufe ich im Supermarkt **ein**. Abends koche ich, sehe ein bisschen **fern** und um 23:00 Uhr gehe ich ins Bett.“*

## 5. Real-Life Germany: Feierabend & Ruhezeit

### 1. Der Feierabend
*Feierabend* literally translates to "celebration evening", but it means the sacred moment your workday finishes:
* *„Schönen Feierabend!“* is a very common goodbye wish between colleagues at the end of the working day.
* In many German workplaces, people are not expected to answer work calls or emails after *Feierabend* (this depends on the job).

### 2. Die Ruhezeit (Quiet Hours)
In Germany, quiet hours (*Ruhezeiten*) come partly from law and partly from your building's house rules (*Hausordnung*), so details vary:
* **Night quiet:** Usually from 22:00 to 06:00 or 07:00.
* **Sundays & public holidays:** Loud work such as drilling or lawnmowing is generally not allowed.
* Many buildings also have a **midday quiet time** – check your *Hausordnung*.
