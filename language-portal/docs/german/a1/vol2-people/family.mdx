# 2.2 Family & Possessives (Die Familie)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can name family members (parents, children, siblings, grandparents).
* I can use possessive words to say "my" (*mein / meine*) and "your" (*dein / deine*) – and all the others (*sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr*).
* I understand why feminine and plural family members add an **-e** (*meine, deine*).
* I can state my marital status (*ledig, verheiratet, geschieden*).
* I can ask a colleague or friend about their family.

---

## 1. Family Vocabulary: Core & Extended

Family words are among the most natural ways to practice German genders because biological gender usually matches grammatical gender (two important exceptions: **das Kind** and **das Mädchen** are neuter):
* Male family members are **der**: *der Vater, der Bruder, der Sohn*
* Female family members are **die**: *die Mutter, die Schwester, die Tochter*
* Groups and parents are **die (plural)**: *die Eltern, die Kinder, die Geschwister*

### The Core Family (Die Kernfamilie)
* **die Eltern** (parents — always plural!)
  * **der Vater** (die Väter) — *father*
  * **die Mutter** (die Mütter) — *mother*
* **die Geschwister** (siblings — always plural!)
  * **der Bruder** (die Brüder) — *brother*
  * **die Schwester** (die Schwestern) — *sister*
* **die Kinder** (children)
  * **der Sohn** (die Söhne) — *son*
  * **die Tochter** (die Töchter) — *daughter*

### Grandparents & Partners
* **die Großeltern** (grandparents)
  * **der Großvater** (die Großväter) / **der Opa** (die Opas) — *grandfather*
  * **die Großmutter** (die Großmütter) / **die Oma** (die Omas) — *grandmother*
* **der Mann** (die Männer) / **die Frau** (die Frauen) — in everyday speech *mein Mann* = my husband, *meine Frau* = my wife
* **der Ehemann** (die Ehemänner) / **die Ehefrau** (die Ehefrauen) — husband / wife (more formal, forms)
* **der Partner** (die Partner) / **die Partnerin** (die Partnerinnen) — partner

### Extended family (you will hear these)
| Word | Plural | English | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **der Onkel** | die Onkel | uncle | *Mein Onkel wohnt in Kerala.* |
| **die Tante** | die Tanten | aunt | *Meine Tante hat drei Kinder.* |
| **der Cousin / die Cousine** | die Cousins / die Cousinen | cousin | *Mein Cousin studiert in Berlin.* |
| **der Enkel / die Enkelin** | die Enkel / die Enkelinnen | grandson / granddaughter | *Oma besucht ihre Enkel.* |
| **das Baby** | die Babys | baby | *Unser Baby ist sechs Monate alt.* |
| **das Mädchen** | die Mädchen | girl | *Das Mädchen heißt Lea.* |
| **der Junge** | die Jungen (colloquial: Jungs) | boy | *Der Junge ist acht.* |
| **das Einzelkind** | die Einzelkinder | only child | *Ich bin Einzelkind.* |

## 2. The Grammar: "My" (mein) and "Your" (dein)

When you introduce someone in English, you say:
* *"This is **my** father. This is **my** mother."* (The word "my" never changes).

In German, possessives adapt to the gender of the person you are describing:

```text
der-word (masculine)  ➔  mein  /  dein
das-word (neutral)    ➔  mein  /  dein
die-word (feminine)   ➔  meine /  deine   (adds an -e!)
die-word (plural)     ➔  meine /  deine   (adds an -e!)
```

### The Mental Model: The "-e" Memory Trick
Notice the pattern: the article **die** (feminine and plural) ends in **-e** – and so do the possessives that go with it: *di**e** Mutter → mein**e** Mutter*.
* *der* Vater ➔ **mein** Vater / **dein** Vater
* *das* Kind ➔ **mein** Kind / **dein** Kind
* *die* Mutter ➔ **meine** Mutter / **deine** Mutter
* *die* Eltern ➔ **meine** Eltern / **deine** Eltern

---

### All the possessives
Once you know *mein/meine*, the others follow exactly the same pattern – only the start changes:

| Person | Possessive | + der / das word | + die word / plural | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ich | my | **mein** | **meine** | *mein Bruder, meine Eltern* |
| du | your (informal) | **dein** | **deine** | *dein Sohn, deine Tochter* |
| er / es | his / its | **sein** | **seine** | *Tom und sein Vater* |
| sie | her | **ihr** | **ihre** | *Anna und ihr Mann* |
| wir | our | **unser** | **unsere** | *unser Kind, unsere Kinder* |
| ihr | your (group) | **euer** | **eure** (!) | *euer Sohn, eure Tochter* |
| sie | their | **ihr** | **ihre** | *Die Nachbarn und ihre Kinder* |
| Sie | your (formal) | **Ihr** | **Ihre** | *Wie heißt Ihr Mann?* |

### ⚠️ The traps for English speakers
1. **The owner decides the word, the noun decides the ending.** English "his/her" depends on the owner – so does
   German *sein/ihr*. But the **ending** depends on the noun that follows:
   * *Anna und **ihr** Bruder* (her brother – owner Anna → *ihr*; *der Bruder* → no ending)
   * *Tom und **seine** Schwester* (his sister – owner Tom → *sein*; *die Schwester* → *-e*)
2. **ihr** has three meanings: *ihr* = "you all" (*Wo wohnt ihr?*), *ihr* = "her" (*ihr Mann*), *ihr* = "their" (*ihr Haus*).
   **Ihr** with a capital I = "your" (formal): *Ist das Ihr Koffer?*
3. **euer → eure:** the *-e-* in the middle drops when an ending is added: *euer Sohn*, but ***eure*** *Tochter*
   (not ~~*euere*~~).
4. **As an object, masculine gets -en** – exactly like *einen* ([3.9](../vol3-grammar/accusative.mdx)):
   *Ich habe **einen** Bruder. → Ich besuche **meinen** Bruder.* · *Kennst du **ihren** Mann?*

## 3. Talking About Marital Status (Familienstand)

In casual conversation or on official forms, you use these four standard words:
* *Ich bin **ledig**.* (I am single / unmarried.)
* *Ich bin **verheiratet**.* (I am married.)
* *Ich bin **geschieden**.* (I am divorced.)
* *Ich bin **verwitwet**.* (I am widowed.)

To ask someone politely:
* *„Sind Sie verheiratet?“* (Formal: Are you married?)
* *„Bist du verheiratet?“* (Informal: Are you married?)

## 4. Real-Life Germany: Emergency Contacts & Coffee Breaks

### 1. Emergency Contacts (Der Notfallkontakt)
When signing an employment contract or filling out a form at a hospital, you will be asked for your *Notfallkontakt* (emergency contact):
* *„Name des Kontakts: Sandra Joseph“*
* *„Beziehung (Relationship): Meine Ehefrau“*

### 2. Coffee Break Small Talk (die Kaffeepause)
Colleagues in German offices often ask friendly, respectful questions about family during breaks:
* *„Hast du Geschwister?“* — *„Ja, einen Bruder und eine Schwester.“*
* *„Habt ihr Kinder?“* — *„Ja, wir haben eine Tochter. Sie ist zwei Jahre alt.“*
* *„Nein, ich bin Einzelkind.“* (*das Einzelkind* = only child).
