# 2.6 Likes & Preferences (Vorlieben und Vorzüge)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I can clearly distinguish when to use the verb **mögen** (for things/nouns) and when to use **gern** (for actions/verbs).
* I can express that I prefer one thing over another using **lieber**.
* I can state my absolute favorite things using **am liebsten** and the prefix **Lieblings-**.
* I can conjugate the irregular verb *mögen* accurately.

---

## 1. The English "Like" Trap in German

In English, the word "like" is used for almost everything:
* *"I **like** coffee."*
* *"I **like to drink** coffee."*

In German, these represent **two completely different grammatical patterns**:

### Pattern A: Liking a THING or PERSON ➔ Use the verb *mögen*
When you like an object, food, place, or person (a noun), you use the verb **mögen**:
* *Ich **mag** Pizza.* (I like pizza.)
* *Ich **mag** Berlin.* (I like Berlin.)
* *Mein Sohn **mag** Hunde.* (My son likes dogs.)

### Pattern B: Liking an ACTION or ACTIVITY ➔ Use the adverb *gern*
When you like *doing* an activity (a verb), you do **not** use *mögen*. You use the normal action verb followed by **gern**:
* *Ich **trinke gern** Kaffee.* (I like drinking coffee.)
* *Ich **schwimme gern**.* (I like swimming.)
* ⚠️ **Tip:** For activities, the natural everyday form is *„Ich schwimme gern“*. *„Ich mag schwimmen“* is heard in casual speech but sounds less natural – use *mögen* mainly with nouns (*Ich mag Pizza*).

---

## 2. The Three Steps of Liking: gern ➔ lieber ➔ am liebsten

When comparing your preferences, German uses this clean three-step ladder:

```text
gern (gladly / like to)   ➔   lieber (rather / prefer to)   ➔   am liebsten (most of all / favorite)
```

### Examples with Actions:
* *Ich trinke **gern** Tee.* (I like drinking tea.)
* *Ich trinke **lieber** Kaffee.* (I prefer drinking coffee.)
* *Aber **am liebsten** trinke ich Wasser.* (But most of all, I like drinking water.)

### Examples with Choices (Contrast):
* *„Kino oder Theater?“* — *„Ich gehe **lieber** ins Kino.“* (I prefer going to the cinema.)
* *„Fährst du mit dem Bus?“* — *„Nein, ich fahre **lieber** mit dem Fahrrad.“* (I prefer riding my bicycle.)

---

## 3. The "Lieblings-" Magic Prefix

German has a wonderful shortcut for talking about your favorite things. You simply attach **Lieblings-** to any noun:

* **das Essen** (food) ➔ **das Lieblingsessen** (favorite food)
  * *Mein Lieblingsessen ist Pasta.*
* **das Getränk** (drink) ➔ **das Lieblingsgetränk** (favorite drink)
  * *Mein Lieblingsgetränk ist Apfelsaft.*
* **die Stadt** (city) ➔ **die Lieblingsstadt** (favorite city)
* **das Buch** (book) ➔ **das Lieblingsbuch** (favorite book)
* **die Farbe** (color) ➔ **die Lieblingsfarbe** (favorite color)

---

## 4. How to Conjugate "mögen"

The verb *mögen* is irregular in the singular, but follows the exact same "modal pattern" you will learn: **`ich` and `er/sie/es` have NO endings and are identical!**

| Pronoun | mögen (to like things) | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **mag** | *Ich mag Schokolade.* |
| **du** | **magst** | *Magst du Tee?* |
| **er / sie / es** | **mag** | *Er mag keinen Fisch.* |
| **wir** | **mögen** | *Wir mögen das Restaurant.* |
| **ihr** | **mögt** | *Mögt ihr Fußball?* |
| **sie / Sie** | **mögen** | *Mögen Sie Deutschland?* |

---

## 5. Real-Life Germany: Deciding on Lunch with Colleagues

Around 12:00 in German offices, colleagues gather to decide where to go for lunch:
* Colleague 1: *„Gehen wir heute zum Italiener?“* (Shall we go to the Italian place today?)
* Colleague 2: *„Ich mag Pizza, aber heute esse ich **lieber** einen Salat.“* (I like pizza, but today I prefer eating a salad.)
* Colleague 3: *„Gute Idee! Wo gibt es den besten Salat?“*

Notice how *mögen* is used for the food (*Pizza*), and *lieber* is used for the action of eating (*esse ich lieber*).
