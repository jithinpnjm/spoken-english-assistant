# 9.8 Apologizing & Excuses (Sich entschuldigen)

## 🎯 What you will learn
* I know the difference between **Entschuldigung** (excuse me / minor accident) and **Es tut mir leid** (I am truly sorry).
* I can apologize for being late (**Entschuldigen Sie die Verspätung**).
* I know how to accept an apology graciously (**Macht nichts!**, **Kein Problem!**).
* I can apologize in professional and personal contexts in Germany.

---

## 1. The 3 Levels of Apologizing in German

### Level 1: „Entschuldigung!“ (Excuse me / Minor bump)
Used to get someone's attention, squeeze past someone on a crowded train, or if you accidentally bump someone's elbow:
* *„**Entschuldigung!** Darf ich kurz vorbei?“* (Excuse me! May I squeeze past?)
* *„Oh, **Entschuldigung**!“* (Oh, sorry!)

### Level 2: „Es tut mir leid.“ (I am sorry / I regret it)
Used when someone experiences something unfortunate, or when you made a meaningful mistake:
* *„**Es tut mir leid**, dass ich nicht kommen kann.“* (I am sorry that I cannot come.)
* *„**Es tut mir wirklich sehr leid**.“* (I am really very sorry.)

### Level 3: Apologizing for Being Late (Die Verspätung)
Because punctuality is so important in Germany:
* *„**Entschuldigen Sie bitte die Verspätung**.“* (Formal: Please excuse the delay.)
* *„**Entschuldige, dass ich zu spät bin**.“* (Informal: Sorry that I'm late.)

---

## 2. How to Accept an Apology (Entschuldigung annehmen)

When someone apologizes to you, reassure them immediately:

| German Response | English Meaning | When to use it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **„Kein Problem!“** | No problem! | Universal, friendly |
| **„Macht nichts!“** | Doesn't matter! / Never mind! | Very common German phrase |
| **„Nicht so schlimm!“** | Not that bad! / It's fine! | Friendly reassurance |
| **„Schon gut.“** | It's alright. | Calm and resolving |
| **„Das kann passieren.“** | That can happen / Accidents happen | Sympathetic |
