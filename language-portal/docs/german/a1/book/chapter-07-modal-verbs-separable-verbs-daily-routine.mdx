## 1. What are Modal Verbs and Why Do We Need Them? (Explained from Zero)

### The Mental Model: Action Verbs vs. Attitude Helpers

Up until now, almost every German sentence you have built has stated a direct, factual action:
* *Ich schwimme.* (I swim.)
* *Ich koche Suppe.* (I cook soup.)
* *Ich trinke einen Tee.* (I drink a tea.)
* *Wir fahren nach Frankfurt.* (We travel to Frankfurt.)

Those sentences are great for stating facts. Out in real life, though, we don't just state dry facts all day! Most of the time, we want to talk about our **ability**, our **obligations**, our **wishes**, or **rules and permissions** around an action:
* Instead of *"I swim"*, you want to say: *"I **can** swim."* (Ability)
* Instead of *"I eat"*, you want to say: *"I **have to** eat because I am hungry."* (Necessity)
* Instead of *"We travel to Frankfurt"*, you want to say: *"We **want to** travel to Frankfurt."* (Strong plan)
* Instead of *"I drink a tea"*, you want to say: *"I **would like to** drink a tea."* (Polite wish)
* Instead of *"You watch TV"*, you want to say: *"You **are allowed to** watch TV now."* (Permission)
* Instead of *"I take a shower"*, you want to say: *"The doctor said I **should** not take a shower."* (Advice or instruction from someone else)

The little helper verbs in bold (**can, must, want to, would like to, may / be allowed to, should**) are called **Modal Verbs** (*Modalverben*).

Why are they called modal verbs? Because they set the **mode** or **mood** of your sentence! A modal verb rarely acts alone. Instead, it teams up with a second verb (your main action verb, like *schwimmen*, *essen*, *trinken*, or *fahren*) to tell the listener *how* you relate to that action.

Before we meet the 7 modal verbs of German, let us make sure three simple building-block words are crystal clear:
1. **Subject:** The person or thing doing the action (*ich, du, er, sie, wir, meine Mutter, der Arzt*).
2. **Conjugated Verb:** The verb that changes its ending to match the subject (*ich lern**e***, *du lern**st***, *wir lern**en***).
3. **Infinitive (*Infinitiv*):** The original, unchanged dictionary form of a verb that ends in **-en** (*schwimmen, kochen, trinken, fahren, essen, schlafen*).

### Meet the 7 German Modal Verbs: The `W-S-K-D-M-M-M` Family

In class, we use a simple seven-letter memory hook to remember all 7 members of the modal verb family: **`W-S-K-D-M-M-M`** (**w**ollen, **s**ollen, **k**önnen, **d**ürfen, **m**üssen, **m**ögen, **m**öchten).

Let us look at each of the 7 verbs one by one, see what it means in plain English, and study real classroom examples.

#### 1. `können` — can / to be able to (Ability, Skill, or Possibility)
Use **können** when you have the skill or ability to do something, or when something is practically possible for you.
* *Ich **kann** schon auf Deutsch einkaufen gehen.* (I can already go shopping in German.)
* *Meine Mutter **kann** prima kochen.* (My mother can cook wonderfully / super well.)
* *Ich **kann** gut schwimmen.* (I can swim well.)
* ***Kannst** du mir bitte helfen?* (Can you please help me?)
* *Ich **kann** heute leider nicht kommen.* (Unfortunately, I cannot come today.)

:::tip Special Shortcut with Languages: *Ich kann Deutsch!*
When talking about languages or practical skills, Germans often use **können** without a second verb at the end because *sprechen* (to speak) or *machen* (to do) is understood automatically!
* *Ich **kann** Deutsch.* = I can speak German / I know German.
* ***Kannst** du Englisch?* = Do you speak English?
* *Meine Tochter **kann** schon gut Deutsch.* = My daughter already knows German well.

This gives you three clear ways to say **"to know"** in German — never mix them up:
1. **können** = to know a **language or learned skill** (*Er **kann** Deutsch. · **Kannst** du Auto fahren?*)
2. **kennen** (*ich kenne, du kennst, er kennt*) = to know or be familiar with a **person, city, or place** (*Ich **kenne** Frau Weber. · **Kennst** du das Café?*)
3. **wissen** (*ich weiß, du weißt, er weiß*) = to know a **fact or piece of information** (*Ich **weiß** die Adresse nicht. · Das **weiß** ich leider nicht.*)
:::

#### 2. `müssen` — must / to have to (Necessity & Obligation)
Use **müssen** when you have no choice—either because of your own physical need (like hunger or tiredness) or because of an obligation, schedule, or duty.
* *Ich **muss** essen, denn ich habe Hunger.* (I have to eat, because I am hungry.)
* *Du **musst** auf dein Kind aufpassen.* (You must look after / pay attention to your child.)
* *Ich **muss** heute lange arbeiten.* (I have to work long hours today.)
* *Wir **müssen** jetzt gehen, der Bus kommt.* (We have to go now, the bus is coming.)

#### 3. `wollen` — to want to (Strong Plan, Firm Intention, or Determination)
Use **wollen** when you have a firm plan or strong intention to do something.
* *Wir **wollen** nach Frankfurt fahren.* (We want to travel to Frankfurt / We plan to go to Frankfurt.)
* *Er **will** ein neues Auto kaufen.* (He wants to buy a new car.)
* *Was **willst** du am Wochenende machen?* (What do you want to do on the weekend?)

:::danger Watch Out for the Number-One English False Friend: `ich will` ≠ "I will"!
Because German **will** looks and sounds just like English *"will"*, beginners constantly say *"Ich will morgen anrufen"* when they mean *"I will call tomorrow."*
* In German, **ich will** ALWAYS means **"I want"** (from *wollen*)!
* It NEVER means the future tense *"I will"*! If you want to say *"I will call tomorrow"*, simply use the normal present tense with a time word: *Ich rufe morgen an.*
* Also remember: in a café, bakery, or shop, saying *„Ich **will** einen Kaffee!“* sounds rude, bossy, or like a demanding child shouting *"I WANT a coffee!"* For polite requests, always use our next verb: **möchten**!
:::

#### 4. `möchten` — would like to (Polite Wish, Ordering Food, Making Requests)
In formal grammar books, *möchten* is a polite form built from *mögen*, but at A1 you can simply treat **möchten** as your best friend for every polite wish, desire, or restaurant order!
* *Ich **möchte** einen Tee trinken.* (I would like to drink a tea.)
* *Ich **möchte** einen Film sehen.* (I would like to watch a movie.)
* *Ich **möchte** einen Apfel essen.* (I would like to eat an apple.)
* *Ich **möchte** Fußball spielen.* (I would like to play football.)
* *Was **möchten** Sie trinken?* (What would you like to drink?)

Notice that when ordering food or drinks in a restaurant or bakery, you can even drop the verb *trinken* or *essen* at the end if the noun makes it obvious:
* *Ich **möchte** bitte einen Kaffee.* (I would like a coffee, please.)

#### 5. `dürfen` — may / to be allowed to (Permission, Rules, and Laws)
Use **dürfen** whenever you talk about what is permitted by a parent, a teacher, a doctor, a sign, or the law. You also use *Darf ich...?* to ask politely for permission.
* *Du hast deine Hausaufgaben gemacht? Dann **darfst** du jetzt fernsehen!* (You have done your homework? Then you are allowed to watch TV now!)
* ***Darf** ich hier parken?* (May I / Am I allowed to park here?)
* ***Darf** ich Sie etwas fragen?* (May I ask you something?)
* *Hier **darf** man nicht rauchen.* (One is not allowed to smoke here.)

#### 6. `sollen` — should / to be supposed to (Someone Else's Instruction, Order, or Advice)
Use **sollen** when **another person** (a doctor, a boss, a director, a teacher, or a parent) has told you to do something, or when you ask someone what they want you to do (*Soll ich...?*).
* *Der Arzt hat gesagt, ich **soll** nicht duschen.* (The doctor said I am not supposed to / should not shower.)
* *Der Direktor hat gesagt, ich **soll** nach Ernakulam fahren.* (The director said I am supposed to travel to Ernakulam.)
* *Ich **soll** dreimal täglich eine Tablette nehmen.* (I am supposed to take a tablet three times a day — because the doctor told me so.)
* ***Soll** ich das Fenster aufmachen?* (Should I / Would you like me to open the window?)

#### 7. `mögen` — to like (Fondness for Nouns: People, Animals, Food, Music)
Unlike the other six modal verbs, at A1 level **mögen** is almost always used **without a second verb**! You use it directly with a **noun** (chocolate, dogs, coffee, music, football) to say that you like that thing or person.
* *Was **mögen** Sie?* (What do you like?)
* *Ich **mag** Hunde.* (I like dogs.)
* *Ich **mag** Schokolade.* (I like chocolate.)
* ***Magst** du Kaffee oder Tee?* (Do you like coffee or tea?)
* *Mein Bruder **mag** keine Suppe.* (My brother does not like soup.)

### Complete Conjugation Table & 3 Golden Memory Tricks

At first glance, a table with 7 verbs and 6 pronouns (42 boxes!) might look scary. Do not memorize 42 boxes! Once you see the **3 Golden Memory Tricks** below the table, you only need to learn 7 short words.

| Pronoun | können (can) | müssen (must) | wollen (want) | möchten (would like) | dürfen (may) | sollen (should) | mögen (like) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **kann** | **muss** | **will** | **möchte** | **darf** | **soll** | **mag** |
| **du** | kannst | musst | willst | möchtest | darfst | sollst | magst |
| **er / sie / es** | **kann** | **muss** | **will** | **möchte** | **darf** | **soll** | **mag** |
| **wir** | können | müssen | wollen | möchten | dürfen | sollen | mögen |
| **ihr** | könnt | müsst | wollt | möchtet | dürft | sollt | mögt |
| **sie / Sie** | können | müssen | wollen | möchten | dürfen | sollen | mögen |

#### 💡 The 3 Golden Tricks to Master This Table in 2 Minutes:
1. **Trick 1: `ich` and `er/sie/es` are ALWAYS 100% twins!**
   Look at the **ich** row and the **er / sie / es** row in the table above. They are completely identical for every single modal verb:
   * *ich kann = er/sie/es kann* (NEVER ~~er kannt~~!)
   * *ich muss = er/sie/es muss* (NEVER ~~er musst~~!)
   * *ich will = er/sie/es will* (NEVER ~~er willt~~!)
   * *ich darf = er/sie/es darf* (NEVER ~~er darft~~!)
   * *ich soll = er/sie/es soll* (NEVER ~~er sollt~~!)
   * *ich mag = er/sie/es mag* (NEVER ~~er magt~~!)
   * *ich möchte = er/sie/es möchte* (NEVER ~~er möchtet~~!)
   For the six true modals (*können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, sollen, mögen*), neither *ich* nor *er/sie/es* has ANY ending at all! And for *möchten*, both *ich* and *er/sie/es* end in *-e* (*ich möchte, er möchte*).
2. **Trick 2: `du` is just the `ich`-form + `-st`!**
   Once you know the *ich* form, you automatically know the *du* form:
   * *kann* ➔ *du kann**st***
   * *muss* ➔ *du muss**t*** (since *muss* already ends in *-ss*, we just add *-t*!)
   * *will* ➔ *du will**st***
   * *darf* ➔ *du darf**st***
   * *soll* ➔ *du soll**st***
   * *mag* ➔ *du mag**st***
   * *möchte* ➔ *du möchte**st***
3. **Trick 3: The Plural (`wir`, `ihr`, `sie/Sie`) is 100% regular and keeps the dictionary vowel!**
   Notice how the vowel changes in the singular for five of the verbs (*können ➔ kann, müssen ➔ muss, wollen ➔ will, dürfen ➔ darf, mögen ➔ mag*). In the plural (*wir, ihr, sie/Sie*), that vowel change disappears completely! You just use the normal dictionary stem:
   * *wir/sie/Sie*: **können, müssen, wollen, möchten, dürfen, sollen, mögen**
   * *ihr*: **könnt, müsst, wollt, möchtet, dürft, sollt, mögt**

### Crucial Beginner Contrasts (With All Your Notebook Examples!)

Let us zoom in on the biggest meaning traps for English speakers—especially the famous contrast between **nicht müssen** and **nicht dürfen** from your class notes.

#### Contrast 1: `nicht müssen` (Don't Have To / Optional) vs. `nicht dürfen` (Must Not / Forbidden!)

In English, when you say *"You must not cross the street"*, it means **"It is forbidden!"**
Because English *"must"* looks like German *"müssen"*, English speakers naturally translate *"You must not"* as *„Du musst nicht...“* — and that creates a huge misunderstanding in Germany!

Look at what the two negatives actually mean in German:

* **`nicht müssen` / `kein ... müssen` = NOT NECESSARY / OPTIONAL (You don't have to, nobody forces you, it's up to you!)**
  Let us read our notebook examples carefully:
  1. *Am Samstag und Sonntag **müssen** wir **nicht** in die Schule **gehen**.*
     * **Word-by-word:** *Am Samstag und Sonntag* (On Saturday and Sunday) *müssen* (have to) *wir* (we) *nicht* (not) *in die Schule* (to school) *gehen* (go).
     * **Meaning:** On Saturday and Sunday, we **don't have to** go to school! (There is no school on the weekend, so we are free!)
  2. *Muslime **müssen kein** Schweinefleisch **essen**.*
     * **Word-by-word:** *Muslime* (Muslims) *müssen* (have to) *kein* (no) *Schweinefleisch* (pork) *essen* (eat).
     * **Meaning:** Nobody forces them to eat pork; they do not have to eat pork. (Though if you want to state the religious prohibition strictly, *dürfen kein Schweinefleisch essen* is also very clear!).
  3. *Du **musst** morgen **nicht arbeiten**, es ist Feiertag.*
     * **Meaning:** You **don't have to** work tomorrow, it is a public holiday.

* **`nicht dürfen` / `kein ... dürfen` = FORBIDDEN / NOT ALLOWED / PROHIBITED (Against the rules, the law, or someone's strict order!)**
  Now look at our class notebook examples for **nicht dürfen**:
  1. *Bei Rot **darf** man **nicht** über die Ampel **gehen**!*
     * **Word-by-word:** *Bei Rot* (At red / When the light is red) *darf* (is allowed) *man* (one / people) *nicht* (not) *über die Ampel* (across the traffic light) *gehen* (walk).
     * **Meaning:** When the light is red, you **must not / are not allowed to** cross the street!
  2. *Jugendliche unter 16 Jahren **dürfen nicht** Auto **fahren**!*
     * **Word-by-word:** *Jugendliche* (Young people / Teenagers) *unter 16 Jahren* (under 16 years) *dürfen* (are allowed) *nicht* (not) *Auto* (car) *fahren* (drive).
     * **Meaning:** Teenagers under 16 **are not allowed to / must not** drive a car!
  3. *Die Mutter ist krank. Ihr **dürft keine** laute Musik **machen**!*
     * **Word-by-word:** *Die Mutter* (The mother) *ist* (is) *krank* (sick). *Ihr* (You all) *dürft* (are allowed) *keine* (no) *laute Musik* (loud music) *machen* (make).
     * **Meaning:** Mother is sick. You all **must not / are not allowed to** play loud music!

:::info The 5-Second Memory Rule:
* **muss nicht** = *"I don't have to"* (Relax! It's optional!)
* **darf nicht** = *"I must not / I am not allowed to"* (Stop! It's forbidden!)
:::

#### Contrast 2: `müssen` (My Own Need / Fact) vs. `sollen` (Someone Else Said So)
* **müssen:** *Ich **muss** heute zum Arzt gehen, mein Zahn tut weh.* (I have to go to the doctor today; my tooth hurts — my own necessity.)
* **sollen:** *Der Arzt hat gesagt, ich **soll** nicht duschen.* / *Der Direktor hat gesagt, ich **soll** nach Ernakulam fahren.* (Another person—the doctor or the director—gave me this instruction!)

#### Contrast 3: `können` (Can / Able to) vs. `dürfen` (Allowed to)
* **können:** *Mein Sohn **kann** schon Auto fahren.* (He has the physical skill; he knows how to steer and brake.)
* **dürfen:** *Aber er ist erst 15 Jahre alt — er **darf** noch nicht Auto fahren!* (The law does not allow him to drive yet.)

## 2. How to Build Sentences & Questions with Modal Verbs (The Verb Bracket)

### The Mental Model: The "Verb Bracket" (*Satzklammer*)

In English, when you use a modal verb and an action verb, the two verbs sit right next to each other in the middle of the sentence:
* English: *"I **must call** the dentist tomorrow."*
* English: *"My mother **can cook** very well."*

German does something completely different—and once you see the visual picture, you will never forget it. German creates a **Verb Bracket** (like two bookends on a bookshelf) around the middle of the sentence:
1. The **conjugated modal verb** stands proudly in **Position 2** (the left bookend).
2. All the details (time, place, people, objects) sit safely in the **middle**.
3. The **main action verb** goes to the **VERY END of the sentence** in its unchanged dictionary **-en** form (*Infinitiv*) (the right bookend)!

```text
[Position 1: Subject]   [Position 2: Modal Verb]   [Middle: All Other Details]   [VERY END: Action Verb (-en)]
Ich                     muss                       morgen den Zahnarzt           besuchen.
Meine Mutter            kann                       prima                         kochen.
Wir                     wollen                     nach Frankfurt                fahren.
Ich                     möchte                     einen warmen Tee              trinken.
```

---

### The 3 Sentence Patterns with Modal Verbs (Step by Step)

Let us look at how this works in **Statements**, **W-Questions**, and **Yes/No Questions**, using all the real examples from your class notes.

#### Pattern A: Normal Statements (`Position 2` + `End`)
* **Formula:** `[Position 1: Subject or Time] + [Position 2: Conjugated Modal Verb] + [Middle Details] + [VERY END: Infinitive (-en)]`

Look at what happens when we add a modal verb to a normal sentence:
1. **Without Modal Verb:** *Ich **trinke** einen Tee.* (One verb ➔ *trinke* is conjugated in Position 2.)
   **With Modal Verb:** *Ich **möchte** einen Tee **trinken**.* (*möchte* takes Position 2; *trinken* moves to the very end in its *-en* form!)
2. **Without Modal Verb:** *Ich **sehe** einen Film.*
   **With Modal Verb:** *Ich **möchte** einen Film **sehen**.*
3. **Without Modal Verb:** *Ich **esse** einen Apfel.*
   **With Modal Verb:** *Ich **möchte** einen Apfel **essen**.*
4. **Without Modal Verb:** *Ich **spiele** Fußball.*
   **With Modal Verb:** *Ich **möchte** Fußball **spielen**.*
5. **Starting with a Time Phrase in Position 1:**
   Remember from earlier chapters that if you put a time phrase (*Am Samstag und Sonntag*) in Position 1, the conjugated verb STILL stays in Position 2, and the subject (*wir*) moves right after it into Position 3:
   * *[Pos 1: Am Samstag und Sonntag] + [Pos 2: **müssen**] + [Pos 3: wir] + [Middle: nicht in die Schule] + [End: **gehen**].*

---

#### Pattern B: W-Questions with Modal Verbs (`W-Word` + `Position 2` + `End`)
When you ask a W-question (*Wann, Was, Wo, Wohin, Wie viele, Welche...*), the W-question word takes **Position 1**, the conjugated modal verb sits in **Position 2**, the subject sits in **Position 3**, and the main action verb STILL waits patiently at the **VERY END**!

* **Formula:** `[Pos 1: W-Word / Question Group] + [Pos 2: Modal Verb] + [Pos 3: Subject] + [Middle] + [VERY END: Infinitive (-en)]?`

Let us break down the exact doctor's office W-questions from your class notebook:
1. ***Wann musst du den Zahnarzt besuchen?***
   * *Wann* (When — Pos 1) + ***musst*** (must — Pos 2) + *du* (you — Pos 3) + *den Zahnarzt* (the dentist — Middle) + ***besuchen*** (visit — End)?
   * **Meaning:** When do you have to visit the dentist?
2. ***Wie viele Tabletten muss ich heute nehmen?***
   * *Wie viele Tabletten* (How many tablets — entire question group in Pos 1!) + ***muss*** (must — Pos 2) + *ich* (I — Pos 3) + *heute* (today — Middle) + ***nehmen*** (take — End)?
   * **Meaning:** How many tablets do I have to take today?
3. ***Welche Kinderärztin können Sie mir empfehlen?***
   * *Welche Kinderärztin* (Which female pediatrician — Pos 1) + ***können*** (can — Pos 2) + *Sie* (you, formal — Pos 3) + *mir* (to me — Middle) + ***empfehlen*** (recommend — End)?
   * **Meaning:** Which pediatrician can you recommend to me?
4. ***Was möchten Sie trinken?***
   * *Was* (What — Pos 1) + ***möchten*** (would like — Pos 2) + *Sie* (you — Pos 3) + ***trinken*** (to drink — End)?
   * **Meaning:** What would you like to drink?

---

#### Pattern C: Yes/No Questions with Modal Verbs (`Position 1` + `End`)
In a Yes/No question (a question without a W-word), the conjugated modal verb steps right into **Position 1**, the subject follows in **Position 2**, and the main action verb STILL stays at the **VERY END**!

* **Formula:** `[Pos 1: Modal Verb] + [Pos 2: Subject] + [Middle Details] + [VERY END: Infinitive (-en)]?`

Let us look at the everyday Yes/No questions from our notes:
1. ***Kann ich dich am Abend nach 20 Uhr anrufen?***
   * ***Kann*** (Can — Pos 1) + *ich* (I — Pos 2) + *dich am Abend nach 20 Uhr* (you in the evening after 8 PM — Middle) + ***anrufen*** (call — End)?
   * **Natural Answer:** *Ja, gerne, kein Problem!* (Yes, gladly, no problem!)
2. ***Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?***
   * ***Können*** (Can — Pos 1) + *Sie* (you — Pos 2) + *das bitte* (that please — Middle) + ***wiederholen*** (repeat — End)?
   * **Meaning:** Can you please repeat that?
3. ***Darf ich hier rauchen?***
   * ***Darf*** (May — Pos 1) + *ich* (I — Pos 2) + *hier* (here — Middle) + ***rauchen*** (smoke — End)?
   * **Natural Answer:** *Nein, das ist hier leider verboten.* (No, unfortunately that is forbidden here.)

### Short Classroom Dialogue: At the Doctor's Reception (*Beim Arzt*)

Let us read a short, simple dialogue that uses **only** the modal verb patterns we just learned:

> **Patient:** *Guten Tag! Ich bin krank. **Kann** ich heute den Arzt **sprechen**?*
> (Good day! I am sick. Can I speak to the doctor today?)
>
> **Rezeptionistin:** *Guten Tag! **Haben** Sie einen Termin?*
> (Good day! Do you have an appointment?)
>
> **Patient:** *Nein, aber ich habe Fieber und ich **muss** heute einen Arzt **sehen**.*
> (No, but I have a fever and I have to see a doctor today.)
>
> **Rezeptionistin:** *Okay, Sie **können** im Wartezimmer **warten**. Aber Sie **dürfen** hier keinen Kaffee **trinken**.*
> (Okay, you can wait in the waiting room. But you are not allowed to drink coffee here.)
>
> **Patient:** *(15 minutes later, with the doctor)* *Herr Doktor, wie viele Tabletten **muss** ich heute **nehmen**? Und **darf** ich heute **duschen**?*
> (Doctor, how many tablets do I have to take today? And am I allowed to shower today?)
>
> **Arzt:** *Sie **müssen** dreimal täglich eine Tablette **nehmen**. Sie **sollen** heute viel Wasser **trinken** und Sie **sollen** heute **nicht duschen**. Morgen **müssen** Sie **nicht arbeiten** — Sie **sollen** im Bett **bleiben**!*

## 3. Separable Verbs (Trennbare Verben) — The "Pen and Cap" Rule!

### The Mental Model: The Pen and Its Cap!

In English, we constantly add small words like *up, off, on, in, out, back, along* after a basic verb to create a brand-new meaning:
* *stand* ➔ *stand **up*** / *get **up***
* *call* ➔ *call **up***
* *turn* ➔ *turn **on*** / *turn **off***
* *come* ➔ *come **along*** / *come **back***
* *pick* ➔ *pick **up***

German does the exact same thing! Instead of writing the small word after the verb, German glues that little word (*auf-, an-, aus-, ein-, mit-, ab-, zu-, vor-, los-, zurück-, um-, fern-*) onto the **front** of the verb in the dictionary:
* **auf** (up/on) + **stehen** (to stand) = **aufstehen** (to get up out of bed)
* **an** (on/at) + **rufen** (to call) = **anrufen** (to call on the telephone)
* **ein** (in) + **kaufen** (to buy) = **einkaufen** (to shop for groceries)
* **auf** (up) + **machen** (to make/do) = **aufmachen** (to open a door, window, or bottle)
* **zu** (to/shut) + **machen** (to make/do) = **zumachen** (to close or shut a door, window, or book)

These verbs are called **Separable Verbs** (*Trennbare Verben* — from *trennen*, to separate).

Why? Here is the exact mental model from our class notebook:

:::tip The "Pen and Cap" Rule (From Class Notes!)
Think of a separable verb like a **pen with a cap**:
* **In the dictionary (`auf|stehen`):** The cap (**`auf-`**) is sitting neatly on the front of the pen (**`stehen`**) as one single word: **aufstehen**.
* **When you write a normal sentence:** You take the cap (**`auf`**) off the pen! You use the pen (**`stehe`**) in **Position 2** to write your sentence, and you place the cap (**`auf`**) down at the **VERY END of the sentence**!
  * Dictionary pen with cap on: **aufstehen** (to get up)
  * Normal sentence (cap at the end!): *Ich **stehe** um 6 Uhr **auf**.* (I get up at 6 o'clock.)
  * Dictionary pen with cap on: **losgehen** (to set off / leave)
  * Normal sentence (cap at the end!): *Peter **geht** um 9 Uhr **los**.* (Peter sets off at 9 o'clock.)
:::

Look at how clean and consistent this is in a sentence diagram:

```text
[Position 1: Subject]   [Position 2: Base Verb (The Pen)]   [Middle: Time, Place, Object]   [VERY END: Prefix (The Cap!)]
Ich                     stehe                               um 6 Uhr                        auf.
Peter                   geht                                um 9 Uhr                        los.
Der Kurs                fängt                               um 8:30 Uhr                     an.
Wir                     kaufen                              am Samstag im Supermarkt        ein.
Ich                     rufe                                dich heute Abend                an.
```

Even if your sentence has **only two words** (just a subject and a verb), you STILL take the cap off and put it after the verb:
* *Ich **stehe auf**.* (NEVER ~~Ich aufstehe~~!)
* *Wir **kaufen ein**.* (NEVER ~~Wir einkaufen~~!)
* *Kommst du **mit**?* (Are you coming along?)

### How to Build Statements, W-Questions & Yes/No Questions with Separable Verbs

Notice that no matter which type of sentence you build, **the cap (prefix) ALWAYS stays at the very end**! Only the pen (the base verb) moves between Position 2 (in statements and W-questions) and Position 1 (in Yes/No questions):

1. **Statement (Base verb in Position 2, Prefix at End):**
   * *Ich **stehe** jeden Morgen um 6 Uhr **auf**.* (I get up every morning at 6 o'clock.)
   * *Der Film **fängt** um 20 Uhr **an**.* (The movie starts at 8 PM.)
2. **W-Question (W-Word in Position 1, Base verb in Position 2, Prefix at End):**
   * *Wann **stehst** du am Sonntag **auf**?* (When do you get up on Sunday?)
   * *Was **haben** Sie am Sonntag **vor**?* (What do you have planned on Sunday?)
   * *Wann **fährt** der Zug **ab**?* (When does the train depart?)
3. **Yes/No Question (Base verb in Position 1, Prefix at End):**
   * ***Stehst** du um 9 Uhr **auf**?* (Do you get up at 9 o'clock?)
   * ***Kommst** du heute Abend **mit**?* (Are you coming along tonight?)
   * ***Rufst** du mich später **an**?* (Will you call me later?)

### What Happens When You Combine a Separable Verb with a Modal Verb? (The Cap Goes Back on the Pen!)

Now comes the favorite question of every German A1 exam:
**What happens when a sentence has BOTH a modal verb (`müssen, können, wollen, möchten, dürfen, sollen`) AND a separable verb (`aufstehen, anrufen, einkaufen, mitkommen`)?**

Let us use our **Pen and Cap** mental model from your class notebook:
* You only have **ONE** spot in **Position 2** for a conjugated verb.
* When a modal verb (*willst, musst, kannst, möchtest*) enters the sentence, the modal verb is the boss! It takes **Position 2**.
* Because the separable verb is no longer being used to write in Position 2, **you put the cap (`auf-`) right back onto the pen (`stehen`) and place the whole closed pen (`aufstehen`) at the very end of the sentence as ONE single word!**

Compare these side by side:

```text
1. WITHOUT Modal Verb (The pen is in Pos 2, so the cap comes OFF and goes to the end):
   [Pos 1]   [Pos 2: Pen]   [Middle Details]        [VERY END: Cap]
   Ich       stehe          morgen um 6 Uhr         auf.
   Wann      stehst         du am Sonntag           auf?
   Ich       rufe           dich am Abend           an.

2. WITH Modal Verb (Modal verb is in Pos 2, so the cap goes BACK ON the pen at the end!):
   [Pos 1]   [Pos 2: Modal] [Middle Details]        [VERY END: Cap + Pen Together!]
   Ich       muss           morgen um 6 Uhr         aufstehen.
   Wann      willst         du am Sonntag           aufstehen?
   Kann      ich            dich am Abend           anrufen?
```

Look at five more pairs so your eyes and ears lock in the pattern:
* **Normal:** *Du **passt** auf dein Kind **auf**.* (You look after your child.)
  **With Modal:** *Du **musst** auf dein Kind **aufpassen**.* (You must look after your child.)
* **Normal:** *Du **siehst** jetzt **fern**.* (You watch TV now.)
  **With Modal:** *Dann **darfst** du jetzt **fernsehen**!* (Then you are allowed to watch TV now!)
* **Normal:** *Wir **kaufen** am Samstag **ein**.* (We shop on Saturday.)
  **With Modal:** *Wir **wollen** am Samstag **einkaufen**.* (We want to shop on Saturday.)
* **Normal:** *Ich **hole** dich am Bahnhof **ab**.* (I pick you up at the station.)
  **With Modal:** *Ich **kann** dich am Bahnhof **abholen**.* (I can pick you up at the station.)
* **Normal:** *Sie **füllen** zuerst Wasser **ein**.* (First you pour water in.)
  **With Modal:** *Zuerst **müssen** Sie Wasser **einfüllen**.* (First you must pour water in.)

### A Close Relative: The `gehen` + Infinitive Pattern & `kennenlernen`

In everyday German, several super-common activities are built by pairing **`gehen`** (to go) with a second verb in the infinitive:
* **spazieren gehen** = to go for a walk / stroll
* **einkaufen gehen** = to go shopping
* **schwimmen gehen** = to go swimming
* **joggen gehen** = to go jogging
* **essen gehen** = to go out to eat (at a restaurant)
* **schlafen gehen** = to go to sleep / go to bed

How do these phrases behave in a sentence? **They work just like separable verbs!**
1. **Without a modal verb:** Conjugated **gehen** sits in **Position 2**, and the second verb waits at the **VERY END** of the sentence.
2. **With a modal verb:** The modal verb sits in **Position 2**, and **both verbs** wait together at the **VERY END** (with *gehen* last!).

| Activity Phrase | Without Modal Verb (`gehen` in Pos 2) | With Modal Verb (Both Verbs at the End!) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **spazieren gehen** (go for a walk) | *Ich **gehe** jeden Abend **spazieren**.* | *Wir **wollen** am Sonntag **spazieren gehen**.* |
| **einkaufen gehen** (go shopping) | *Sie **geht** jeden Samstag **einkaufen**.* | *Sie **muss** heute noch **einkaufen gehen**.* |
| **schwimmen gehen** (go swimming) | *Am Nachmittag **gehen** wir **schwimmen**.* | ***Kannst** du heute mit uns **schwimmen gehen**?* |
| **joggen gehen** (go jogging) | *Alex **geht** morgens im Park **joggen**.* | *Ich **möchte** morgen früh **joggen gehen**.* |
| **essen gehen** (go out to eat) | *Wir **gehen** heute Abend **essen**.* | ***Wollen** wir heute Abend **essen gehen**?* |
| **schlafen gehen** (go to bed/sleep) | *Ich **gehe** um 23 Uhr **schlafen**.* | *Ich **muss** heute früh **schlafen gehen**.* |

:::tip Bonus Verb: `kennenlernen` (to meet for the first time / get to know)
The verb **kennenlernen** (built from *kennen* + *lernen*) follows the exact same pattern!
* **Without a modal verb:** *Ich **lerne** heute meine neuen Nachbarn **kennen**.* (Today I am meeting my new neighbors for the first time.)
* **With a modal verb (written as one word at the end!):** *Ich **möchte** deine Familie gern **kennenlernen**.* (I would really like to get to know your family.)
:::

### Important Exception: Inseparable Prefixes (`be-, er-, ver-, ent-, emp-, ge-, zer-, miss-`), "It Depends" Prefixes & `frühstücken`

Does *every* long German verb split? **No!**
From your class notebook (Batch 04, Page 51), there is a specific group of short prefixes that are **permanently glued** to their verbs. They have no meaning on their own as prepositions, and when you pronounce the verb, the voice stress is NOT on the prefix, but on the main verb stem (*be-**SU**-chen*, *ver-**STE**-hen*, *er-**KLÄ**-ren*).

#### The Inseparable Prefixes (Never Split!): `be- · er- · ver- · ent- · emp- · ge- · zer- · miss-`

| Prefix | Common A1 Verbs | English Meaning | Correct Sentence (NEVER Splits!) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **be-** | **besuchen** | to visit (a person/place) | *Ich **besuche** heute meine Oma.* (NEVER ~~Ich suche meine Oma be~~!) |
| **be-** | **bezahlen** | to pay | *Wir **bezahlen** die Rechnung mit Karte.* (We pay the bill by card.) |
| **be-** | **bestellen** | to order (food / online) | *Ich **bestelle** eine Pizza.* (I order a pizza.) |
| **be-** | **bekommen** | to receive / get | *Ich **bekomme** morgen ein Paket.* (I am getting a parcel tomorrow.) |
| **be-** | **beginnen** | to begin | *Der Kurs **beginnt** um 9 Uhr.* (Compare separable *an\|fangen*: *Der Kurs **fängt** um 9 Uhr **an**.*) |
| **er-** | **erklären** | to explain | *Der Lehrer **erklärt** die Grammatik.* (The teacher explains the grammar.) |
| **er-** | **erzählen** | to tell (a story) | *Opa **erzählt** eine Geschichte.* (Grandpa tells a story.) |
| **emp-** | **empfehlen** | to recommend | *Welchen Wein **empfehlen** Sie?* (Which wine do you recommend?) |
| **ver-** | **verstehen** | to understand | *Ich **verstehe** den Satz gut.* (I understand the sentence well.) |
| **ver-** | **vergessen** | to forget | *Er **vergisst** oft seinen Schlüssel.* (He often forgets his key.) |
| **ver-** | **verdienen** | to earn (money) | *Sie **verdient** viel Geld.* (She earns a lot of money.) |
| **ver-** | **verkaufen** | to sell | *Mein Onkel **verkauft** sein Auto.* (My uncle is selling his car.) |
| **ge-** | **gefallen** | to please / like | *Die Wohnung **gefällt** mir sehr.* (I like the apartment very much.) |
| **ge-** | **gehören** | to belong to | *Das Buch **gehört** mir.* (The book belongs to me.) |
| **ent-** | **entschuldigen** | to excuse / apologize | *Ich **entschuldige** mich.* (I apologize.) |

:::danger Classic English False Friend: `bekommen` ≠ "become"!
* **bekommen** ALWAYS means **to get / to receive**: *Ich **bekomme** einen Brief.* (I get a letter.)
* If you want to say **"to become"** (for example, *"I become a teacher"*), German uses the verb **werden**: *Ich **werde** Lehrer.*
:::

#### The "It Depends" Prefixes: `durch- · über- · um- · unter- · wieder-`
Five prefixes can be **either separable or inseparable**, depending on the verb (and where you put the voice stress!). At A1, you don't need a complicated theory—just learn these 6 high-frequency verbs as you meet them:

| Prefix | Separable Verb (Stress on Prefix ➔ Splits!) | Inseparable Verb (Stress on Verb Stem ➔ NEVER Splits!) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **um-** | ***um\|steigen*** (to change trains): *Ich **steige** in Köln **um**.*<br/>***um\|ziehen*** (to move house): *Wir **ziehen** im Mai **um**.* | — |
| **über-** | — | ***übernachten*** (to stay overnight): *Wir **übernachten** im Hotel.*<br/>***überweisen*** (to transfer money): *Ich **überweise** heute die Miete.* |
| **unter-** | — | ***unterschreiben*** (to sign a document): *Bitte **unterschreiben** Sie hier.* |
| **wieder-** | — | ***wiederholen*** (to repeat): *Ich **wiederhole** den Satz. / Können Sie das bitte **wiederholen**?* |

:::warning Why Doesn't `frühstücken` (to eat breakfast) Split?
Beginners often look at **frühstücken** (to have breakfast), see the word *früh* (early) at the front, and try to say: ❌ *„Ich stücke um 7 Uhr früh.“*
**Never split `frühstücken`!** Why? Because it is built directly from the noun **das Frühstück** (breakfast). The whole word is one single unit:
* ✅ *Ich **frühstücke** um 7:30 Uhr.* (I eat breakfast at 7:30.)
* ✅ *Wann **frühstückst** du am Sonntag?* (When do you eat breakfast on Sunday?)
:::

## 4. Temporal Adverbs (`zuerst, dann, danach, später, schließlich`) & Describing Your Daily Routine (`Tagesablauf`)

### Why We Need Sequence Words (*Reihenfolge*)

Imagine someone asks you in your speaking exam or in everyday conversation:
* *„Wie sieht Ihr Alltag aus?“* (What does your everyday life look like?)
* *„Was machen Sie jeden Tag?“* (What do you do every day?)

If you start every single sentence with *"Ich... Ich... Ich... Ich..."*, your German sounds robotic:
* *„Ich stehe um 6 Uhr auf. Ich jogge. Ich dusche. Ich gehe zum Kurs.“*

To make your German flow like a real native speaker, we use five simple **Time Sequence Words** (*Temporale Adverbien*) from your class notes to connect one action to the next!

### The 5 Essential Sequence Words (+ `wieder`)

| German Word | Plain English Meaning | When to Use It in Your Routine |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **zuerst** | first / first of all | For the very **first step** in a sequence or routine. |
| **dann** | then / next | For the **next action** that follows. |
| **danach** | after that / afterwards | Great alternative to *dann* so you don't repeat *dann* ten times! |
| **später** | later | For an action that happens **some time later** in the day. |
| **schließlich** *(or **zum Schluss**)* | finally / at the end | For the **final step** of your sequence or evening. |
| **wieder** | again | When you go back to a place or do something **a second time** (*Ich gehe **wieder** zum Goethe-Zentrum*). |

### ⚠️ The Golden Word-Order Rule: Verb Always in Position 2!

Remember the most important rule of German sentence structure: **The conjugated verb is anchored in Position 2!**
When you start a sentence with **Zuerst**, **Dann**, **Danach**, **Später**, **Schließlich**, or a time like **Um 12:30 Uhr**, that word sits in **Position 1**.
Therefore, the **conjugated verb MUST come immediately after it in Position 2**, and the subject (*ich, du, Sie, er*) moves right behind the verb into **Position 3**!

Compare English and German carefully:
* English: *"Then **I jog** in the morning."* (Subject before verb)
* German: *„**Dann jogge ich** morgens.“* (Pos 1: *Dann* ➔ Pos 2: **jogge** ➔ Pos 3: **ich**!)
* English: *"After that **I shower**."*
* German: *„**Danach dusche ich**.“* (Pos 1: *Danach* ➔ Pos 2: **dusche** ➔ Pos 3: **ich**!)
* ❌ **Beginner Mistake:** ~~*Dann ich jogge. Danach ich dusche.*~~ (Here the verb got pushed to Position 3 — never do this!)

Also remember our six handy time-of-day adverbs with **-s** (meaning *"every day at that time / in the morning / afternoon / evening"*):
* **morgens** (in the morning / every morning, e.g. 06:00 – 10:00)
* **vormittags** (in the late morning, e.g. 10:00 – 12:00)
* **mittags** (at noon / lunchtime, e.g. 12:00 – 14:00)
* **nachmittags** (in the afternoon, e.g. 14:00 – 18:00)
* **abends** (in the evening, e.g. 18:00 – 22:00)
* **nachts** (at night, e.g. 22:00 – 06:00)

#### 2. Everyday Morning, Work & Household Chores (Ready-to-Use Verb Chunks)
You can use these common daily expressions right away as ready-made building blocks (we will explore the full grammar of *mich / mir* in Chapter 10!):
* **sich duschen** *(or simply **duschen**)* = to take a shower: *Morgens **dusche ich** (mich).*
* **sich an\|ziehen** = to get dressed: *Dann **ziehe ich mich an**.* (I get dressed.)

### Routine Walkthrough 2: The Coffee-Machine Sequence — Without & With Modal Verbs! (From Your Notebook!)

In your handwritten notebook (Batch 04, Page 60 & Batch 05, Page 61), there is a brilliant exercise that combines **everything** in Chapter 7:
* **Sequence words** (*Zuerst, Dann, Danach, Später, Schließlich*)
* **Separable verbs** (*ein|füllen* = to pour/fill in; *ein|legen* = to insert/put in)
* **Normal verbs** (*stellen* = to place upright; *drücken* = to press; *genießen* = to enjoy)
* **Modal verbs** (*müssen* and *können*)

First, let us learn the 4 kitchen nouns you need for making filter coffee:
* **das Wasser** = the water
* **die Filtertüte** = the paper coffee filter bag
* **der Kaffee** = the coffee (coffee powder/grounds)
* **die Kanne** = the coffee pot / jug
* **die Kaffeemaschine** = the coffee machine

Now look at how we describe making coffee **Version A: WITHOUT Modal Verbs** (where the separable verbs split and throw their cap *ein* to the end) versus **Version B: WITH Modal Verbs** (where *müssen/können* takes Position 2, and the separable verbs glue their cap back on at the end: *einfüllen, einlegen*)!

#### Version A: Coffee-Machine Sequence WITHOUT Modal Verbs (Separable Verbs Split!)

1. ***Zuerst füllen Sie Wasser ein.***
   * **Meaning:** First, you pour water in.
   * **Why:** Verb is **ein|füllen**. *Zuerst* (Pos 1) + **füllen** (Pos 2) + *Sie Wasser* (Middle) + **ein** (Cap at the end!).
2. ***Dann legen Sie die Filtertüte ein.***
   * **Meaning:** Then, you insert the filter bag.
   * **Why:** Verb is **ein|legen**. *Dann* (Pos 1) + **legen** (Pos 2) + *Sie die Filtertüte* (Middle) + **ein** (Cap at the end!).
3. ***Danach füllen Sie Kaffee ein.***
   * **Meaning:** After that, you put coffee (grounds) in.
   * **Why:** Verb is **ein|füllen**. *Danach* (Pos 1) + **füllen** (Pos 2) + *Sie Kaffee* (Middle) + **ein** (Cap at the end!).
4. ***Später stellen Sie die Kanne in die Kaffeemaschine.***
   * **Meaning:** Later / Next, you place the pot into the coffee machine.
   * **Why:** Normal verb **stellen** in Position 2.
5. ***Dann drücken Sie Start.***
   * **Meaning:** Then, you press Start.
   * **Why:** Normal verb **drücken** in Position 2.
6. ***Schließlich genießen Sie den Kaffee.***
   * **Meaning:** Finally, you enjoy the coffee!
   * **Why:** Inseparable verb **genießen** (*ge-* never splits!) in Position 2.

#### Version B: Coffee-Machine Sequence WITH Modal Verbs (`müssen` & `können` — Cap Back on the Pen!)

Now watch what happens when we add **müssen** (must) and **können** (can) in Position 2! Every separable verb (**einfüllen, einlegen**) puts its cap back on and goes to the very end as ONE single word:

1. ***Zuerst müssen Sie Wasser einfüllen.***
   * **Meaning:** First, you must pour water in.
   * **Structure:** *Zuerst* (Pos 1) + **müssen** (Pos 2) + *Sie Wasser* (Middle) + **einfüllen** (End — glued together!).
2. ***Dann müssen Sie die Filtertüte einlegen.***
   * **Meaning:** Then, you must insert the filter bag.
   * **Structure:** *Dann* (Pos 1) + **müssen** (Pos 2) + *Sie die Filtertüte* (Middle) + **einlegen** (End — glued together!).
3. ***Danach müssen Sie Kaffee einfüllen.***
   * **Meaning:** After that, you must put coffee in.
   * **Structure:** *Danach* (Pos 1) + **müssen** (Pos 2) + *Sie Kaffee* (Middle) + **einfüllen** (End — glued together!).
4. ***Später können Sie die Kanne in die Kaffeemaschine stellen.***
   * **Meaning:** Next, you can place the pot into the coffee machine.
   * **Structure:** *Später* (Pos 1) + **können** (Pos 2) + *Sie die Kanne in die Kaffeemaschine* (Middle) + **stellen** (End).
5. ***Dann müssen Sie Start drücken.***
   * **Meaning:** Then, you must press Start.
   * **Structure:** *Dann* (Pos 1) + **müssen** (Pos 2) + *Sie Start* (Middle) + **drücken** (End).
6. ***Schließlich können Sie den Kaffee genießen.***
   * **Meaning:** Finally, you can enjoy the coffee!

#### Side-by-Side Comparison Table (Coffee Machine Master Check)

| Step | Without Modal Verb (Prefix Splits to End!) | With Modal Verb (Stays Together as 1 Word at End!) |

### Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid in Chapter 7

1. **Adding `-t` to `er/sie/es` with Modal Verbs:**
   * ❌ *Meine Mutter kannt prima kochen. / Er musst arbeiten. / Der Arzt sagt, er sollt nicht duschen.*
   * ✅ *Meine Mutter **kann** prima kochen. / Er **muss** arbeiten. / Der Arzt sagt, er **soll** nicht duschen.*
2. **Leaving the Action Verb in the Middle (English Word Order):**
   * ❌ *Ich möchte trinken einen Tee. / Wir wollen fahren nach Frankfurt.*
   * ✅ *Ich **möchte** einen Tee **trinken**. / Wir **wollen** nach Frankfurt **fahren**.*
3. **Forgetting to Take the Cap Off a Separable Verb in a Normal Sentence:**
   * ❌ *Ich aufstehe um 6 Uhr. / Peter losgeht um 9 Uhr.*
   * ✅ *Ich **stehe** um 6 Uhr **auf**. / Peter **geht** um 9 Uhr **los**.*
4. **Splitting a Separable Verb When a Modal Verb Is Present:**
   * ❌ *Wann willst du stehst auf? / Wann willst du stehen auf?*
   * ✅ *Wann **willst** du **aufstehen**?*
5. **Splitting an Inseparable Verb (`be-, er-, ver-, emp-`) or `frühstücken`:**
   * ❌ *Ich suche heute den Zahnarzt be. / Ich stücke um 7 Uhr früh.*
   * ✅ *Ich **besuche** heute den Zahnarzt. / Ich **frühstücke** um 7 Uhr.*
6. **Putting the Subject Before the Verb After `Zuerst, Dann, Danach, Später`:**
   * ❌ *Dann ich jogge morgens. Danach ich dusche.*
   * ✅ *Dann **jogge ich** morgens. Danach **dusche ich**.*
