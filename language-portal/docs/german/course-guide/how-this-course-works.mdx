# How This Course Works

## Who this course is for

You speak English, you live in Germany (or will soon), and you need German **now** – at the bakery, the
doctor's reception, the Bürgeramt, your child's Kita, work. You probably also want an official certificate:
first **Goethe-Zertifikat A1 (Start Deutsch 1)**, later **A2**, and one day **B1**.

This course assumes you are a **genuine beginner**. You do not need to know any grammar words in advance –
each one is explained the first time it appears.

## Teach in English, train in German

At A1, explanations are in English so that you understand *why* German works the way it does. But every
exercise asks you to **produce German**: say it, write it, fix it, say it again. As you move to A2 and B1,
more of the instructions switch to German.

## What every lesson tries to give you

1. **What is this?** – the idea in plain English.
2. **Why do I need it?** – where it shows up in daily life in Germany.
3. **How does it work, and why?** – the rule and the reason behind it.
4. **The pattern** – a reusable sentence frame you can fill with your own words.
5. **Real German** – examples with English meaning.
6. **Your turn** – guided exercises that move from choosing the answer to building your own sentences.
7. **Real life** – a scenario from life in Germany.
8. **Exam link** – only when there is a real connection to the Goethe exam.

Some lessons are short (the alphabet), some are long (modal verbs). That is on purpose: each topic gets the
depth it needs.

## The correction loop – the most important habit

Knowing the right answer after you see it is not the same as being able to say it. So every exercise in this
course follows one loop:

```text
You answer  →  you check  →  you understand the mistake  →  you rewrite or say the corrected sentence  →  next
```

When you check an answer (or when the AI teacher corrects you), you will see feedback like this:

```text
You wrote:          Ich habe ein Termin.
Result:             Almost correct.
Corrected German:   Ich habe einen Termin.
Why:                "Termin" is masculine (der Termin). After "haben" the object is accusative, so "ein" becomes "einen".
Mistake type:       article + accusative
Now:                Say or write the full corrected sentence once more.
```

**Always do the last step.** Writing or saying the corrected sentence once is what moves it into your memory.

### Keep a mistake log
Write down mistakes you make more than once, with the corrected sentence. Most beginners repeat the same
five or six mistakes (article after *haben*, verb not in position 2, second verb not at the end, *nicht* vs
*kein*, du/Sie mix-ups). The [Mistake Log & Repair](../parallel-systems/progress-dashboard.mdx) page has a
repair drill for each.

## How to use the AI practice boxes

Most lessons end with one **AI Practice** box. The AI plays a role (a receptionist, a colleague, a baker) and
also acts as your teacher: it has one learning goal for the conversation, corrects you in English, gives you
the corrected German, and asks you to repeat it before continuing.

- Answer in German, even if it is only a few words.
- If you don't understand, practise the repair phrases: *Wie bitte?*, *Noch einmal, bitte.*, *Langsamer, bitte.*
- The AI practises **communication**. In doctor or pharmacy roleplays it will not diagnose you or tell you which
  medicine to take – for real health questions, speak to a real doctor or pharmacist (emergency: **112**).
- In office/administration roleplays it teaches the *language* of the process. Rules, fees and deadlines change –
  always check your own letter or ask the office.

## Where to start

- **Completely new?** Start with the [Recommended Learning Path](./learning-path.mdx). It tells you which chapter to
  do next across all volumes.
- **Just arrived in Germany?** Do the [Survival Kit](../a1/vol1-foundations/survival-kit.mdx) first.
- **Preparing for the A1 exam?** Work through the path, then use Volume 10 (Exam Center).

| Level | What you can do | Status in this portal |
