# F. Listening Practice Lab (Hören) — Strategies, Ear Training & 18 Audio Tasks

Welcome to your **Interactive A1 Listening Practice Lab (*Hören*)**! Many beginners feel nervous about listening in German because spoken conversations, train station loudspeakers, and voicemail recordings move at real-life speed—and unlike reading a page, you cannot pause a train announcement in real life!

Here is the secret that immediately takes the fear out of **Goethe A1 / telc A1 Hören** and real-life German listening: **You do NOT need to understand every word a speaker says.** At the A1 level, almost every listening task tests whether you can catch **one specific piece of practical information**—a number, a price, a clock time, a day of the week, a platform number, a place, or what action someone asks you to do—while ignoring the distractors around it.

In this interactive workbook, **every single exercise has a built-in `<GermanAudio />` player**, and **every transcript is hidden inside a `<details>` box** so you can train your ears first—just like in the real exam!

For full exam scoring details, also visit [Goethe A1 Hören](../a1/exam-center/goethe-hoeren.mdx) and [telc A1 Full Practice](../a1/exam-center/telc-full-practice.mdx).

## 1. The 7-Step Listening Loop & How A1 *Hören* Works

Listening improves fastest when you **listen before you read**. If you look at the written transcript first, you are practicing reading, not listening!

### Follow This 7-Step Listening Loop for Every Audio Drill

```text
Step 1: Read the QUESTION and OPTIONS first (before clicking Play!).
        Ask yourself: "Am I listening for a time? A price? A day? A place? An object?"
Step 2: Predict the German words you might hear for each option
        (e.g., if the options are 3:30, 4:00, and 4:30, prime your ears for "halb vier", "vier Uhr", "halb fünf").
Step 3: Click the Audio Player and listen WITHOUT opening the transcript!
        (Listen TWICE for Teil 1 and Teil 3; try listening only ONCE for Teil 2!)
Step 4: Write down or pick your answer (a, b, c or Richtig / Falsch).
Step 5: Open the answer box to check your answer and read the German + English transcript.
Step 6: Spot the TRAP: Find the exact words that eliminated the wrong options.
Step 7: Shadowing: Play the audio one final time and speak the key sentence out loud along with the speaker!
```

### How the 3 Parts of `Goethe / telc A1 Hören` Work

Real life in Germany and the official A1 *Hören* exam test the exact same three situations:

| Exam Part | What You Hear | How Many Times Played? | Question Format | What You Must Catch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hören Teil 1** | 6 short everyday conversations (in a bakery, clothing shop, doctor's office, street, café, or home). | **Played TWICE (2×)** | Multiple choice (**a, b, or c**) | Prices, times, clothing sizes, food/drink orders, transport, or meeting plans. |
| **Hören Teil 2** | 4 short loudspeaker announcements (at a train station, airport, ferry port, or supermarket). | **Played ONCE ONLY (1×)!** | **Richtig** (True) or **Falsch** (False) | Platform changes (*Gleis*), delays (*Verspätung*), gates (*Ausgang*), or special offers. |
| **Hören Teil 3** | 5 telephone voicemail messages (from a doctor's practice, friend, colleague, landlord, or repair shop). | **Played TWICE (2×)** | Multiple choice (**a, b, or c**) | Rescheduled appointments, callback phone numbers, reasons for cancelling, or what to bring. |

## 2. The 5 Classic Listening Traps & How to Beat Them

Exam writers use the exact same **five listening traps** year after year. Once you know how these traps are built, you will hear them coming from a mile away!

### Trap 1: The "First-Mention Distractor" (Wait for the Final Agreement!)
In almost every A1 dialogue or voicemail, **the very first day, time, price, or item mentioned is a trap!**
* **How it works:** Speaker A suggests an option (*„Gehen wir am Montag ins Kino?“*), and Speaker B says no and suggests a different option (*„Nein, am Montag habe ich keine Zeit. Geht es am Mittwoch?“ — „Ja, super!“*).
* **❌ Beginner Mistake:** Hearing the word *Montag* in the first 3 seconds, immediately checking box **a) Montag**, and tuning out.
* **✅ How to Beat It:** Keep your pencil hovering until you hear words of agreement like **„Ja, gut!“**, **„Super, das passt!“**, **„Machen wir so!“**, or the correction word **„sondern“**!

### Trap 2: The `halb` Clock Trap & 24-Hour Times ([Chapter 4](../a1/book/chapter-04-sentence-structure-verbs-negation-time.mdx) & [Chapter 5](../a1/book/chapter-05-questions-time-prepositions-weather.mdx))
In English, *"half past three"* looks back at 3. In German, **halb** looks **forward** to the next hour (*halfway TO the next hour*)!
* **halb vier** = halfway to 4 = **3:30** (**NEVER 4:30!** *4:30* is **halb fünf**!)
* **halb acht** = **7:30** | **halb elf** = **10:30** | **halb eins** = **12:30**
* Also watch out for:
  * **Viertel nach drei** = **3:15** (*15:15 Uhr*)
  * **Viertel vor vier** = **3:45** (*15:45 Uhr*)
  * **kurz vor / kurz nach** = just before / just after (*kurz vor acht* = 7:58)
  * **24-Hour Subtract-12 Rule:** When an announcement says **16:30 Uhr**, subtract 12 (`16 - 12 = 4`) ➔ that is **4:30 PM** (*halb fünf am Nachmittag*)!

### Trap 3: The `-zehn` vs. `-zig` and Reversed-Digit Number Trap ([Chapter 3](../a1/book/chapter-03-pronouns-plurals-numbers-dates.mdx))
German numbers have two famous ear traps:
1. **Teens (`-zehn`) vs. Tens (`-zig`):**
   * **dreizehn (13)** vs. **dreißig (30)**
   * **vierzehn (14)** vs. **vierzig (40)**
   * **fünfzehn (15)** vs. **fünfzig (50)**
   * **sechzehn (16)** vs. **sechzig (60)**
   * **siebzehn (17)** vs. **siebzig (70)**
   * *Ear Clue:* **-zehn** has a long **"ay + n"** (*tsayn*); **-zig** has a short, clipped **"-tsich"**!
2. **Ones-Before-Tens Reversal (`21` to `99`):**
   * German says the **ones digit FIRST** and the **tens digit SECOND**:
   * Hearing *„**vier**und**zwanzig**“* = four-and-twenty = **24** (NOT 42! *42* is *zweiundvierzig*).
   * Hearing *„**fünf**und**dreißig**“* = five-and-thirty = **35** (NOT 53!).

### Trap 4: The Separable Prefix at the Very End of the Sentence ([Chapter 7](../a1/book/chapter-07-modal-verbs-separable-verbs-daily-routine.mdx))
In German, the tiny word at the **very end of the sentence** completely flips the meaning of the verb! If you stop listening before the last word, you will get the exact opposite meaning:

| Verb Stem in Position 2 | Prefix at the End = Meaning A | Opposite Prefix at the End = Meaning B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Der Zug **fährt** um 8 Uhr …* | **… ab.** = **departs / leaves** | *(Der Zug **kommt** um 8 Uhr **an** = **arrives**)* |
| *Sie **steigen** am Hauptbahnhof …* | **… ein.** = **get on / board** | **… aus.** = **get off / exit** (or **… um** = **transfer trains**) |
| *Der Supermarkt **macht** um 20 Uhr …* | **… auf.** = **opens** | **… zu.** = **closes** |
| *Der Deutschkurs **fängt** um 9 Uhr …* | **… an.** = **starts / begins** | *(Der Kurs **hört** um 12 Uhr **auf** = **ends**)* |
| *Das Konzert **findet** heute …* | **… statt.** = **takes place** | *(Das Konzert **fällt** heute **aus** = **is cancelled**)* |

### Trap 5: Negation & Correction Words (`nicht …, sondern …` / `leider` / `doch`)
Train your ears to perk up immediately whenever you hear any of these signal words:
* **„nicht …, sondern …“** (*not X, but rather Y*): *„Wir treffen uns **nicht** um 9 Uhr, **sondern** um 11 Uhr.“* (9:00 is cancelled; 11:00 is the winner!)
* **„leider“** (*unfortunately*): Almost always cancels the sentence it is in! (*„Am Montag geht es **leider** nicht.“*)
* **„kein / keine“** (*no / not any*): *„Ich trinke **keinen** Kaffee, ich nehme lieber einen Tee.“*
* **„doch“** (*yes, on the contrary / after all*): Used to overturn a negative assumption or change a plan (*„Ich nehme **doch** den blauen Pullover.“*).

Mal sehen: Das Fischgericht für dreizehn Euro fünfzig und ein großes Mineralwasser für drei Euro. Das macht zusammen sechzehn Euro fünfzig. Hier ist ein Zwanzig-Euro-Schein. Machen Sie bitte achtzehn Euro! Vielen Dank! Hier sind zwei Euro zurück. Einen schönen Abend noch!" label="Play Dialogue 6 (Listen up to 2×)" />

<details>
  <summary>Show Answer, Full Transcript & Trap Analysis</summary>

  **Correct Answer: b) 18,00 Euro**

  **Full Transcript:**
  * **Gast:** *Herr Ober, ich möchte bitte bezahlen!*
  * **Kellner:** *Sehr gern. Hat es Ihnen geschmeckt?*
  * **Gast:** *Ja, der Fisch und der Salat waren ausgezeichnet!*
  * **Kellner:** *Mal sehen: Das Fischgericht für 13,50 € und ein großes Mineralwasser für 3,00 €. **Das macht zusammen 16,50 €.***
  * **Gast:** *Hier ist ein 20-Euro-Schein. **Machen Sie bitte achtzehn Euro!***
  * **Kellner:** *Vielen Dank! Hier sind 2 Euro zurück. Einen schönen Abend noch!*

  **Why the Traps Fail (Classic German Restaurant Tipping Rule!):**
  * **Option a (16,50 €)** is the bill before tip (*das macht zusammen 16,50 €*).
  * **Option c (20,00 €)** is the banknote (*Zwanzig-Euro-Schein*) the guest hands over.
  * When a German guest says **„Machen Sie bitte 18 Euro!“** (*"Make it 18 euros, please!"*), **18,00 €** is the total amount they pay (and they get 2 € change back)! See [Chapter 0: Survival Kit](../a1/book/chapter-00-survival-kit.mdx) and [Chapter 13](../a1/book/chapter-13-comparisons-demonstratives-groceries-bank.mdx).
</details>

## 6. Hören Teil 3 Workshop — 6 Telephone Voicemail Messages (Played Twice, Multiple Choice a/b/c)

In **Hören Teil 3**, you listen to voicemail messages left on an answering machine (*Anrufbeantworter / Mailbox*). Each message is played **twice**, and you choose **a, b, or c**.

---

### Voicemail 1: Doctor's Practice Rescheduling an Appointment (Praxis Dr. Berg)
**Question 1:** *Wann soll Frau Novak in die Praxis kommen?* (When should Ms. Novak come to the practice?)
* **a)** Am Montag um 9:00 Uhr
* **b)** Am Dienstag um 9:00 Uhr
* **c)** Am Dienstag um 11:00 Uhr

<GermanAudio text="Guten Tag, Frau Novak, hier ist die Praxis Doktor Berg. Ihr Termin am Montag um neun Uhr geht leider nicht, weil Doktor Berg auf einer Konferenz ist. Können Sie bitte am Dienstag kommen? Aber nicht um neun Uhr, sondern um elf Uhr. Bitte rufen Sie uns kurz zurück. Danke und auf Wiederhören!" label="Play Voicemail 1 (Listen up to 2×)" />

<details>
  <summary>Show Answer, Full Transcript & Trap Analysis</summary>

  **Correct Answer: c) Am Dienstag um 11:00 Uhr**
  * **Transcript:** *„Guten Tag, Frau Novak, hier ist die Praxis Dr. Berg. Ihr Termin **am Montag um 9 Uhr geht leider nicht**, weil Dr. Berg auf einer Konferenz ist. Können Sie bitte **am Dienstag** kommen? Aber **nicht um 9 Uhr, sondern um 11 Uhr**. Bitte rufen Sie uns kurz zurück. Danke!“*

* **c)** Man kann es leider nicht mehr reparieren.

<details>
  <summary>Show Answer, Full Transcript & Trap Analysis</summary>

Wir **wollten** Ihr Fahrrad eigentlich schon heute fertig machen, aber wir mussten ein neues Teil für die Bremse bestellen. **Deshalb ist Ihr Fahrrad erst morgen Nachmittag ab 16 Uhr fertig.** Die Reparatur kostet insgesamt 35 Euro.“*
