# J. Flashcards & Spaced Repetition System — Method & 150 Interactive A1 Flip-Cards

Welcome to your **Flashcards & Spaced Repetition Hub**! Why do so many adult learners study fifty German words on Sunday, feel proud on Sunday evening, and forget forty-two of them by Wednesday morning? Because human memory is designed to delete new information unless you **retrieve it right before you are about to forget it**.

Even worse, many beginners make flashcards that look like this:
* **Front:** *Tisch*
* **Back:** *table*

When you see *Tisch* on a sign, you recognize that it means "table." But the next day, when you want to tell your landlord *"The table is broken"* or *"I need a new table,"* you freeze: *Is it der Tisch, die Tisch, or das Tisch? Is it ein Tisch or einen Tisch? What is the plural—Tische or Tischen?*

On this page, you will learn how to build **high-power German flashcards** that train active speaking rather than passive recognition, how to run a **10-minute 5-Box Spaced Repetition System (Leitner Method)** that fits into a busy workday, and you can immediately test yourself right here in your browser with **150 interactive A1 flip-cards** across 5 complete decks!

## 1. How to Build a German Flashcard That Actually Works

### 1.1 The Golden Anatomy of a German Flashcard

A great German flashcard always tests you in the **Active Production Direction** (**English + Real-Life Situation ➔ Full German Output**). Instead of recognizing a single German word, you force your brain to produce the article, the noun, the plural, and a real sentence you can say tomorrow.

Here is the exact blueprint for a high-power German flashcard:

* **FRONT (What you see and must respond to in German):**
  > **appointment** — *"Situation: You are standing at the doctor's reception desk and need to book a visit."*
* **BACK (What you say out loud before flipping to check):**
  > 🔵 **der Termin, die Termine** *(masculine — der-word)*
  > **Full Sentence:** *Ich brauche **einen** Termin.* (I need an appointment.)
  > **Word-by-Word:** *Ich* (I) + *brauche* (need) + *einen* (a — masculine accusative) + *Termin* (appointment).
  > ⚠️ **Common Mistake Trap:** Never say ❌ *Ich brauche ein Termin*! After *haben, brauchen, suchen, möchten, es gibt*, masculine *der/ein* changes to **den/einen** ([Chapter 10](../a1/book/chapter-10-accusative-case-pronouns-professions.mdx)).

**Why this 4-part format works so well:**
1. **You produce German from English (Front ➔ Back):** Active recall (*"How do I say this in German?"*) builds speaking reflexes three times faster than passive recognition (*"What does this German word mean?"*).
2. **The article and plural are welded to the noun:** You never learn *Termin* alone. You learn *der Termin, die Termine* as a single rhythmic rhythm (*der Ter-MIN, die Ter-MI-ne*).
3. **The full sentence gives you a ready-made speaking block:** Tomorrow morning at the clinic, you do not have to calculate grammar rules from scratch—you already know the complete block *Ich brauche einen Termin*.
4. **The Mistake Warning protects you from exam traps:** Highlighting the exact trap (*einen* instead of *ein*) stops fossilized errors before they start.

### 1.2 The Blue / Red / Green Color-Coding Trick for Gender

German has three grammatical genders ([Chapter 2](../a1/book/chapter-02-articles-gender-days-phrases.mdx)): masculine (**der**), feminine (**die**), and neuter (**das**). Because your visual memory is much stronger than abstract memory, **color-coding your flashcards** helps you "see" the gender of a noun in your mind's eye when you speak:

| Gender | Article | Color Code | Visual Memory Trick | Example on Card |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine** | **der** | 🔵 **Blue ink / Blue card** | Imagine a **bright blue sky** or freezing blue ice around every *der*-word. | 🔵 **der Bahnhof, die Bahnhöfe** *(train station)* |
| **Feminine** | **die** | 🔴 **Red ink / Pink card** | Imagine **warm red fire** or a red rose on every *die*-word. | 🔴 **die Wohnung, die Wohnungen** *(apartment)* |
| **Neuter** | **das** | 🟢 **Green ink / Green card** | Imagine **fresh green grass** growing on every *das*-word. | 🟢 **das Formular, die Formulare** *(official form)* |
| **Plural** | **die (Pl.)** | 🟡 **Yellow / Gold** | Plural always uses **die** in Nominative/Accusative! | 🟡 **die Kinder** *(the children)* |

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** Even if you use a digital app like Anki or Quizlet instead of paper index cards, add the emoji circle (🔵 **der**, 🔴 **die**, 🟢 **das**) right in front of every noun! After two weeks, your brain will automatically recall *"Wohnung has a red dot—so it is die Wohnung!"*

### 1.3 Mix These 6 Card Types in Your Deck

If every card in your deck is just a noun, you will know lots of words for objects, but you still won't be able to connect them into smooth sentences. Mix these **six card types** so your deck trains vocabulary, grammar, and speaking simultaneously:

| Card Type | Front (Your Prompt) | Back (What You Produce Aloud) | What Skill It Trains |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Noun + Gender + Plural** | *apartment* (at the *Bürgeramt*) | 🔴 **die Wohnung, die Wohnungen** — *Ich suche eine Wohnung.* | Article, plural, and everyday usage ([Chapter 2](../a1/book/chapter-02-articles-gender-days-phrases.mdx)) |
| **2. Plural Drill** | 🔵 *der Bahnhof ➔ Plural?* | **die Bahnhöfe** (*Umlaut + -e*) | Fast plural formation ([Chapter 3](../a1/book/chapter-03-pronouns-plurals-numbers-dates.mdx)) |
| **3. Verb Trio (Present + Past)** | *to pick up (a child / parcel)* | **abholen** ➔ *du holst ab, er holt ab, er hat abgeholt* — *Ich hole Maya um 15 Uhr ab.* | Separable verbs & spoken past ([Chapters 7](../a1/book/chapter-07-modal-verbs-separable-verbs-daily-routine.mdx) & [9](../a1/book/chapter-09-perfekt-past-tense-apartment-weekend.mdx)) |
| **4. Sentence Pattern Card** | *Pattern: need + appointment* | **Ich brauche einen Termin.** (❌ *ein Termin* ➔ ✅ *einen Termin*) | Accusative case reflex ([Chapter 10](../a1/book/chapter-10-accusative-case-pronouns-professions.mdx)) |
| **5. Cloze (Fill-in-the-Blank)** | *Ich fahre ___ ___ Bus zur Arbeit.* (by bus) | **mit dem** (*Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus zur Arbeit.*) | Prepositions + Dative contractions ([Chapter 12](../a1/book/chapter-12-dative-case-prepositions-directions.mdx)) |
| **6. Survival & Roleplay Phrase** | *Politely ask someone to repeat* | **Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?** | Instant speaking chunks ([Chapter 0](../a1/book/chapter-00-survival-kit.mdx) & [Redemittel](./redemittel-phrases.mdx)) |

### 1.4 What to Put in Your Deck First (And What to Leave Out!)

Do **not** download a giant 5,000-word German deck from the internet! Giant pre-made decks are full of words you don't need at A1, and reviewing hundreds of unfamiliar cards feels like a chore. Build a lean, personal deck in this exact order:

1. **Priority 1:** The 30 sentences from the [Survival Kit (Chapter 0)](../a1/book/chapter-00-survival-kit.mdx) as phrase cards (see **Deck 1** below).
2. **Priority 2:** The high-frequency words from the [Vocabulary Vault](./vocabulary-vault.mdx) for the chapter you are studying this week (see **Decks 2, 3, and 4** below).
3. **Priority 3:** Every sentence you got wrong more than once in your [Mistake Log & Repair Clinic](./progress-dashboard.mdx). Turning your own mistakes into flashcards is the fastest way to raise your exam score!
4. **Priority 4:** The core irregular and vowel-change verb forms (*sein, haben, fährt, schläft, spricht, nimmt, liest, sieht, isst, gegessen, gegangen, geblieben*) and official Goethe/telc A1 exam traps (see **Deck 5** below).

## 2. The 5-Box Spaced Repetition System (Leitner Method)

How often should you review your cards? If you review every card every single day, your deck will soon have 300 cards and take an hour a day. **Spaced Repetition** solves this: you review *new or difficult* cards every day, and push *easy, well-known* cards further and further into the future.

You can use a free Spaced Repetition app (such as **Anki**, which schedules cards automatically) **or** a physical shoebox divided into **5 compartments (The Leitner 5-Box System)**:

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    THE 5-BOX LEITNER SCHEDULE                           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  📦 BOX 1: Review EVERY DAY                                             │
│     • All new cards start here.                                         │
│     • Got it 100% right (including article & plural)? ➔ Move to Box 2!  │
│     • Hesitated or got the article wrong? ➔ Keep in Box 1!              │
│                                                                         │
│  📦 BOX 2: Review EVERY 2–3 DAYS (e.g., Tue, Thu, Sun)                  │
│     • Correct aloud without peeking? ➔ Promote to Box 3!                │
│     • Wrong? ➔ Demote all the way back to Box 1!                        │
│                                                                         │
│  📦 BOX 3: Review ONCE A WEEK (e.g., Every Sunday)                      │
│     • Correct? ➔ Promote to Box 4!                                      │
│     • Wrong? ➔ Demote back to Box 1!                                    │
│                                                                         │
│  📦 BOX 4: Review EVERY 2 WEEKS (1st & 15th of the month)               │
│     • Correct? ➔ Promote to Box 5!                                      │
│     • Wrong? ➔ Demote back to Box 1!                                    │
│                                                                         │
│  📦 BOX 5: Review ONCE A MONTH (Mastery Check)                          │
│     • Correct after 30 days? ➔ Card is MASTERED for long-term memory!   │
│     • Wrong? ➔ Demote back to Box 1 for a fresh cycle!                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2.1 The Golden Rules of Your Daily 10-Minute Routine

| Rule | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Budget 10–15 minutes a day** | Consistency beats marathon sessions! Reviewing 10 minutes every morning with your coffee or on the U-Bahn/S-Bahn is 5x more effective than studying for 90 minutes once on Sunday. |
| **2. Maximum 10 new cards per day** | Adding 30 new cards in one day creates a "review avalanche" three days later. Cap new cards at **5 to 10 per day** (35–70 solid cards per week = 500+ rock-solid A1 words in two months!). |
| **3. Say the answer OUT LOUD before flipping** | Never just think *"Oh yeah, I know that"* and flip the card! Your mouth muscles need training too. Say the article, noun, plural, and example sentence aloud before you check the back. |
| **4. Strict grading: Wrong article = Wrong card!** | If you say *das Wohnung* instead of *die Wohnung*, do not tell yourself *"Close enough!"* Put the card back in **Box 1**. Your brain will remember the correction next time because it wants that card to move to Box 2. |

### 2.2 Four Smart Ways to Shuffle & Filter Your Reviews

When you want to spice up your review sessions during the week, try these four review angles:
* **By Real-Life Situation:** Before a doctor's visit, pull out only your *Doctor & Health* cards ([Chapter 14](../a1/book/chapter-14-imperative-body-parts-health-doctor.mdx)). Before going to the *Bürgeramt*, review your *Forms & Offices* cards ([Chapter 16](../a1/book/chapter-16-buergeramt-anmeldung-post-official-letters.mdx)).
* **By Gender (Article Power Drill):** Pull out 15 mixed noun cards and sort them onto three piles on your desk: **Left = der**, **Middle = die**, **Right = das**.
* **Mistakes First:** Every Friday, review only the cards that dropped back into **Box 1** this week.
* **Exam Word Groups:** Before practicing in the [Exam Center](../a1/exam-center/telc-overview.mdx), review numbers, clock times, days, months, dates, prices, and official notice boards.

### Deck 4: The Top 25 A1 Opposites, Time Expressions & Preposition Chunks (Cards 101–125)

In Goethe and telc A1 reading and listening exams ([Exam Center](../a1/exam-center/telc-overview.mdx)), the recording or notice almost never uses the exact same word as the test question—instead, it uses an **opposite** or a **synonym** (*teuer = nicht billig*, *geöffnet ≠ geschlossen*). Master these 25 high-frequency pairs and chunks!

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 101 [Opposites — Price]:</strong> How do you say <em>expensive</em> vs. <em>cheap / affordable</em> in German?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **teuer** *(expensive)* ↔ **billig / günstig** *(cheap / good value)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Die Wohnung im Zentrum ist zu **teuer**, aber die Wohnung in Offenbach ist **günstig**.* ([Chapter 6](../a1/book/chapter-06-adjectives-opposites-colors.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 102 [Opposites — Shop Signs]:</strong> How do you say <em>open</em> vs. <em>closed</em> on a shop door or exam notice?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **geöffnet / offen** *(open)* ↔ **geschlossen / zu** *(closed)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Die Post ist am Samstag bis 12 Uhr **geöffnet**, aber am Sonntag **geschlossen**.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 103 [Opposites — Speed & Volume]:</strong> <em>fast</em> ↔ <em>slow</em> AND <em>loud</em> ↔ <em>quiet</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **schnell** *(fast)* ↔ **langsam** *(slow)* | **laut** *(loud)* ↔ **leise** *(quiet)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Der ICE ist **schnell**. Die Musik beim Nachbarn ist zu **laut**, bitte seien Sie etwas **leiser**!*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 104 [Opposites — Time]:</strong> <em>early</em> ↔ <em>late</em> AND <em>on time</em> ↔ <em>delayed</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **früh** *(early)* ↔ **spät** *(late)* | **pünktlich** *(on time)* ↔ **unpünktlich / zu spät** *(late)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Ich stehe unter der Woche **früh** auf. Der Bus kommt heute leider **zu spät**.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 105 [Opposites — Availability]:</strong> <em>free / available</em> ↔ <em>occupied / taken / busy</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **frei** *(free/available)* ↔ **besetzt / belegt** *(occupied/taken)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Entschuldigung, ist dieser Platz noch **frei**? — Nein, der Platz ist leider **besetzt**.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 106 [Opposites — Apartments]:</strong> <em>big</em> ↔ <em>small</em> AND <em>bright</em> ↔ <em>dark</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **groß** *(big/tall)* ↔ **klein** *(small/short)* | **hell** *(bright)* ↔ **dunkel** *(dark)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Das Schlafzimmer ist **groß** und **hell**, aber der Keller ist **dunkel**.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 107 [Opposites — Condition]:</strong> <em>broken / out of order</em> ↔ <em>whole / working</em> AND <em>sick</em> ↔ <em>healthy</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **kaputt / außer Betrieb** *(broken / out of order)* ↔ **in Ordnung / ganz** *(working)* | **krank** *(sick)* ↔ **gesund** *(healthy)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Der Fahrstuhl ist **außer Betrieb** (kaputt). Gute Besserung, werd schnell wieder **gesund**!*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 108 [Opposites — Rules]:</strong> <em>allowed / permitted</em> ↔ <em>forbidden / prohibited</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **erlaubt** *(allowed)* ↔ **verboten** *(forbidden)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Im Bahnhof ist Rauchen **verboten** (= Man **darf** hier **nicht** rauchen).*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 109 [Train Announcements]:</strong> <em>departure</em> ↔ <em>arrival</em> AND <em>entrance</em> ↔ <em>exit</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** 🔴 **die Abfahrt** *(departure)* ↔ 🔴 **die Ankunft** *(arrival)* | 🔵 **der Eingang** *(entrance)* ↔ 🔵 **der Ausgang** *(exit)*
* 💬 **Example:** ***Abfahrt** um 14:12 Uhr von Gleis 3, **Ankunft** in Köln um 15:05 Uhr.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 110 [Directions]:</strong> <em>left</em>, <em>right</em>, and <em>straight ahead</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **links** *(left)*, **rechts** *(right)*, **geradeaus** *(straight ahead)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Gehen Sie hier **geradeaus** und dann an der Ampel **links**.* ([Chapter 12](../a1/book/chapter-12-dative-case-prepositions-directions.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 111 [5-Day Time Ladder]:</strong> Say in order: <em>the day before yesterday, yesterday, today, tomorrow, the day after tomorrow</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **vorgestern** ➔ **gestern** ➔ **heute** ➔ **morgen** ➔ **übermorgen**
* 💬 **Example:** ***Gestern** war ich krank, **heute** geht es mir besser, und **morgen** gehe ich wieder arbeiten.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 112 [Parts of the Day]:</strong> How do you say <em>in the morning, at noon, in the afternoon, in the evening, at night</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **am Morgen** / **am Vormittag** ➔ **am Mittag** ➔ **am Nachmittag** ➔ **am Abend** ➔ ⚠️ **in der Nacht**!
* ⚠️ **Trap:** Every part of the day uses **am**, EXCEPT night: **in der Nacht** ([Chapter 5](../a1/book/chapter-05-questions-time-prepositions-weather.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 113 [Frequency Ladder]:</strong> Say in order from 100% to 0%: <em>always, often, sometimes, rarely, never</em></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **immer** *(100%)* ➔ **oft** *(75%)* ➔ **manchmal** *(50%)* ➔ **selten** *(15%)* ➔ **nie** *(0%)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Ich trinke **immer** Kaffee am Morgen, aber ich trinke **nie** Alkohol.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 114 [Sequence Connectors]:</strong> How do you connect your daily routine: <em>first, then, after that, later</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **zuerst** *(first)* ➔ **dann** *(then)* ➔ **danach** *(after that)* ➔ **später** *(later)*
* ⚠️ **Word Order Rule:** When you start a sentence with *Zuerst, Dann, Danach*, the **Verb MUST stay in Position 2** (*Zuerst **trinke** ich Kaffee, dann **fahre** ich ins Büro*).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 115 [Time Chunks]:</strong> How do you say <em>on the weekend, on Monday, at 8 o'clock, in July</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **am Wochenende**, **am Montag**, **um acht Uhr**, **im Juli**
* 💡 **Rule:** **um** = clock time | **am** = days, dates, parts of day, *am Wochenende* | **im** = months & seasons.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 116 [Duration Chunks]:</strong> How do you say <em>from Monday to Friday</em> and <em>from 9 to 17 o'clock</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **von Montag bis Freitag** | **von 9 bis 17 Uhr** (or **ab 9 Uhr** = *starting from 9 o'clock*)
* 💬 **Example:** *Unsere Praxis ist **von Montag bis Freitag von 8 bis 18 Uhr** geöffnet.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 117 [Home Chunks]:</strong> What is the difference between <em>zu Hause</em> and <em>nach Hause</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **zu Hause** = *at home* (static location, *Wo?*) | **nach Hause** = *(going) home* (movement, *Wohin?*)
* 💬 **Example:** *Ich bin jetzt **zu Hause**.* vs. *Ich gehe um 18 Uhr **nach Hause**.* ([Chapter 8](../a1/book/chapter-08-past-tense-war-hatte-transport-appointments.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 118 [Transport Chunks]:</strong> How do you say <em>by bus, by train, by U-Bahn/S-Bahn, on foot</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **mit dem Bus** (🔵), **mit dem Zug** (🔵), **mit der U-Bahn / S-Bahn** (🔴), **zu Fuß** *(on foot!)*
* ⚠️ **Trap:** Never say ❌ *mit Fuß*! On foot is always **zu Fuß** (*Ich gehe zu Fuß*).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 119 [Destination Chunks]:</strong> How do you say <em>to the doctor, to the train station, to the post office, to work</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **zum Arzt**, **zum Bahnhof** (*zu + dem = zum*) | **zur Post**, **zur Arbeit**, **zur Schule** (*zu + der = zur*)
* 💬 **Example:** *Heute Morgen gehe ich **zum Arzt** und danach **zur Post**.* ([Chapter 12](../a1/book/chapter-12-dative-case-prepositions-directions.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 120 [Location Chunks]:</strong> How do you say <em>at the doctor's, at the hairdresser's, at work (company name)</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **beim Arzt**, **beim Friseur** (*bei + dem = beim*) | **bei Siemens / bei der Arbeit**
* 💬 **Example:** *Ich kann jetzt nicht sprechen, ich bin gerade **beim Arzt**.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 121 [Countries & Cities]:</strong> How do you say <em>going to Berlin / to Germany / to India</em> vs. <em>to Switzerland / to Turkey / to the USA</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **nach Berlin / nach Deutschland / nach Indien** (no article!) vs. **in die Schweiz / in die Türkei / in die USA** (countries with an article use **in**!).
* 💬 **Example:** *Wir fliegen im Sommer **nach Indien** (oder **in die Schweiz**).*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 122 [Liking Things]:</strong> How do you use <em>gern / lieber / am liebsten</em> to say what you like, prefer, and like best?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **gern** *(like to)* ➔ **lieber** *(prefer to)* ➔ **am liebsten** *(like best of all)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Ich trinke **gern** Tee, aber ich trinke **lieber** Kaffee, und **am liebsten** trinke ich Espresso.* ([Chapter 13](../a1/book/chapter-13-comparisons-demonstratives-groceries-bank.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 123 [Accusative Prepositions]:</strong> How do you say <em>for me / for you</em> and <em>without sugar / without milk</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **für mich / für dich / für Sie** | **ohne Zucker / ohne Milch**
* 💡 **Rule:** **für** and **ohne** ALWAYS take the Accusative case (*Das Geschenk ist **für meinen Bruder***).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 124 [Coordinating Connectors]:</strong> What are the 5 A1 connectors that take Position 0 (so the verb stays right after the subject)?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **und** *(and)*, **aber** *(but)*, **oder** *(or)*, **denn** *(because)*, **sondern** *(but rather — after negation)*
* 💬 **Example:** *Ich bleibe heute im Bett, **denn** ich **bin** krank.* ([Chapter 15](../a1/book/chapter-15-letters-emails-telephone-masterclass.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 125 [Reason & Result]:</strong> What is the difference in word order between <em>deshalb</em> (therefore) and <em>weil</em> (because)?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **deshalb** = Verb comes **immediately after** *deshalb* (*Ich bin krank, **deshalb komme** ich heute nicht*).
  * **weil** = Verb is kicked to the **very end** (*Ich komme heute nicht, **weil** ich krank **bin***).
</details>

### Deck 5: The Top 25 telc & Goethe A1 Exam Trap Cards (Cards 126–150)

These 25 cards target the **exact traps** designed by exam writers in [Goethe & telc A1 Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking](../a1/exam-center/telc-overview.mdx). Review Deck 5 the week before every mock exam!

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 126 [Clock Time Trap #1]:</strong> What time is <strong>halb vier</strong> in German? (3:30 or 4:30?)</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **3:30** (*three-thirty* / *15:30 Uhr*)!
* ⚠️ **Why It's a Trap:** In German, *halb* ALWAYS looks forward to the **next hour**: *halb vier* = halfway **to** four = **3:30**! Never confuse it with English "half past four" ([Chapter 5](../a1/book/chapter-05-questions-time-prepositions-weather.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 127 [Clock Time Trap #2]:</strong> Convert <strong>Viertel vor acht</strong> and <strong>Viertel nach acht</strong> into digital 24-hour times.</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Viertel vor acht** = **7:45** (*19:45 Uhr* — a quarter before 8).
  * **Viertel nach acht** = **8:15** (*20:15 Uhr* — a quarter after 8).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 128 [Clock Time Trap #3]:</strong> In a train announcement, you hear <strong>siebzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig</strong>. What time is that on a 12-hour clock, and how do you say it in casual German?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **17:45 = 5:45 PM** (Subtract 12 from 17 ➔ 5:45 PM). In casual German: **Viertel vor sechs**.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 129 [Listening Number Trap #1]:</strong> How do you hear the difference between <strong>fünfzehn (15)</strong> and <strong>fünfzig (50)</strong>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **15 = fünfzehn** (*FUENF-tsayn* — ends with a clear **-N** sound, long *ee*).
  * **50 = fünfzig** (*FUENF-tsikh* — ends with a soft *-ikh* / *-ish* sound).
* 💡 Same rule for **13 vs. 30** (*dreizehn / dreißig*), **14 vs. 40** (*vierzehn / vierzig*), **16 vs. 60** (*sechzehn / sechzig*), **17 vs. 70** (*siebzehn / siebzig*) ([Chapter 3](../a1/book/chapter-03-pronouns-plurals-numbers-dates.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 130 [Listening Number Trap #2]:</strong> You hear a price or room number: <strong>achtundzwanzig (28)</strong> vs. <strong>zweiundachtzig (82)</strong>. How do German 2-digit numbers work?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** German says the **ONES digit first**, then *und*, then the **TENS digit**!
  * *acht-und-zwanzig* = eight-and-twenty = **28**.
  * *zwei-und-achtzig* = two-and-eighty = **82**.
* 💡 **Exam Trick:** When writing down numbers in *Hören*, keep your pencil hovered slightly to the right, write the first digit you hear on the right, and put the tens digit in front of it!
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 131 [Listening Trap #3]:</strong> In an exam dialogue, the speaker says: <em>"Nein, am Dienstag geht es nicht. Gehen wir lieber am Donnerstag!"</em> Which day is the correct answer?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **Donnerstag (Thursday)**!
* ⚠️ **Why It's a Trap:** In *Hören Teil 1 & 3*, the **first** day, time, or price mentioned is almost always a **distractor** that gets rejected or corrected (*Nein, doch lieber … / Ach nein, Moment …*). Always listen until the end of the dialogue!
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 132 [Modal Verb Trap]:</strong> What is the difference between <strong>Du musst nicht kommen</strong> and <strong>Du darfst nicht kommen</strong>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Du musst nicht kommen** = *You **don't have to** come (it's optional / not necessary).*
  * **Du darfst nicht kommen** = *You **must not / are not allowed to** come (it's forbidden!).*
* ⚠️ **Why It's a Trap:** English speakers translate "must not" as *nicht müssen*—which is 100% wrong in German! ([Chapter 7](../a1/book/chapter-07-modal-verbs-separable-verbs-daily-routine.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 133 [Case Question Words]:</strong> What is the difference between <strong>Wer?</strong>, <strong>Wen?</strong>, and <strong>Wem?</strong></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Wer?** = *Who?* (Nominative subject — *Wer kommt heute? ➔ **Der** Chef.*)
  * **Wen?** = *Whom?* (Accusative object — *Wen rufst du an? ➔ **Den** Chef.*)
  * **Wem?** = *To whom?* (Dative recipient — *Wem gehört das Auto? ➔ **Dem** Chef.*)
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 134 [Two-Way Preposition Trap]:</strong> What is the difference between <strong>Wo?</strong> and <strong>Wohin?</strong></summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Wo?** = *Where (static location)?* ➔ Uses **Dative** (*Ich bin **im** Kino / **in der** Küche / **auf dem** Tisch*).
  * **Wohin?** = *Where to (movement/destination)?* ➔ Uses **Accusative** (*Ich gehe **ins** Kino / **in die** Küche / **auf den** Tisch*).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 135 [Date Preposition Trap]:</strong> How do you say and write <em>"on May 5th"</em> in a German email or form?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** Written: **am 5. Mai** (with a dot after the number!) | Spoken: **am fünften Mai** (*am + -ten* for 1–19, *am + -sten* for 20–31).
* ⚠️ **Trap:** Today is *der fünfte Mai*, but "**on** May 5th" is always **am fünften Mai** ([Chapter 3](../a1/book/chapter-03-pronouns-plurals-numbers-dates.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 136 [Schreiben Teil 1 Form Trap #1]:</strong> On an exam registration form, what is the difference between <strong>Vorname</strong>, <strong>Nachname / Familienname</strong>, and <strong>Geburtsort</strong>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Vorname:** First / given name (*e.g., Priya*).
  * **Nachname / Familienname:** Last name / surname (*e.g., Sharma*).
  * **Geburtsort:** Place/city of birth (do not confuse with **Wohnort** = current city of residence!).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 137 [Schreiben Teil 1 Form Trap #2]:</strong> What are the four options for <strong>Familienstand</strong> (marital status) on a German form?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **ledig** = single (never married)
  * **verheiratet** = married
  * **geschieden** = divorced
  * **verwitwet** = widowed
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 138 [Schreiben Teil 1 Form Trap #3]:</strong> On a hotel or travel form, what is the difference between <strong>bar</strong>, <strong>Kreditkarte</strong>, and <strong>Überweisung</strong>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **bar / Barzahlung** = in cash
  * **Kreditkarte / EC-Karte** = by credit / debit card
  * **Überweisung** = by bank transfer ([Chapter 13](../a1/book/chapter-13-comparisons-demonstratives-groceries-bank.mdx))
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 139 [Schreiben Teil 2 Letter Greeting Trap]:</strong> Give the exact greeting (*Anrede*) for: 1) a formal man (*Herr Weber*), 2) a formal woman (*Frau Weber*), 3) a male friend (*Tim*), 4) a female friend (*Anna*).</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  1. Formal man: **Sehr geehrter Herr Weber,** *(ends in **-er**!)*
  2. Formal woman: **Sehr geehrte Frau Weber,** *(ends in **-e**!)*
  3. Male friend: **Lieber Tim,** *(ends in **-er**!)*
  4. Female friend: **Liebe Anna,** *(ends in **-e**!)*
  *(Unknown formal team: **Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,**)* ([Chapter 15](../a1/book/chapter-15-letters-emails-telephone-masterclass.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 140 [Schreiben Teil 2 Punctuation Trap]:</strong> After <em>"Liebe Maria,"</em> — does the first word of the next line start with a CAPITAL letter or a lowercase letter? And do you put a comma after <em>"Viele Grüße"</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  1. After the comma in *Liebe Maria,*, the first word of your message starts with a **lowercase letter** (unless that word is a noun or formal *Sie*):
     *Liebe Maria,*
     ***i**ch komme am Samstag …*
  2. After the closing (**Mit freundlichen Grüßen** or **Viele Grüße**), there is **NO comma** in German!
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 141 [Schreiben Teil 2 Scoring Trap]:</strong> What is the single most expensive mistake in telc/Goethe A1 *Schreiben Teil 2*?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **Skipping one of the 3 bullet points (*Leitpunkte*)!**
* 💡 **Rule:** Every writing task has 3 bullet points (worth up to 3 points each). Even if your grammar is simple, write **at least one clear sentence for every single bullet point**, plus a greeting and a closing!
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 142 [Possessive Trap]:</strong> Why is <em>"Das ist Maria und sein Mann"</em> wrong, and how do you fix it?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** In German, the choice between **sein** (*his*) and **ihr** (*her*) depends on the **OWNER**, and the ending (*-e / -en*) depends on the **NOUN that follows**:
  * Owner is Maria (female) ➔ **ihr**
  * Following noun is *der Mann* (masculine nominative) ➔ no ending: ✅ **Das ist Maria und ihr Mann.**
  * If Maria talks about her daughter (*die Tochter*): ✅ **ihre Tochter** ([Chapter 11](../a1/book/chapter-11-possessives-family-clothing-shopping.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 143 [Pronoun Gender Trap]:</strong> You point to a table (🔵 *der Tisch*) and want to say <em>"It costs 50 euros. I'll buy it."</em> Which pronouns must you use instead of <em>es</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** Because 🔵 *der Tisch* is masculine, "it" is **er** (subject) and **ihn** (object):
  * ✅ ***Er** kostet 50 Euro. Ich kaufe **ihn**.*
  * For 🔴 *die Tasche*: ***Sie** kostet 20 Euro. Ich kaufe **sie**.*
  * For 🟢 *das Buch*: ***Es** kostet 10 Euro. Ich kaufe **es**.* ([Chapters 3](../a1/book/chapter-03-pronouns-plurals-numbers-dates.mdx) & [10](../a1/book/chapter-10-accusative-case-pronouns-professions.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 144 [Sprechen Teil 1 Trap]:</strong> In the speaking exam, the examiner holds up a card that says <strong>Buchstabieren</strong> and <strong>Telefonnummer</strong>. What two sentences must you say immediately?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  1. *„Mein Familienname ist Sharma. Ich buchstabiere: **S – H – A – R – M – A**.“*
  2. *„Meine Telefonnummer ist **null – eins – sieben – sechs …**“*
* 💡 Practice spelling your own surname and street name using the German alphabet ([Chapter 1](../a1/book/chapter-01-basics-alphabet-greetings.mdx)) until it takes less than 5 seconds!
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 145 [Sprechen Teil 2 Trap]:</strong> In *Sprechen Teil 2*, you draw a card with the theme <strong>Einkaufen</strong> and the word <strong>Sonntag</strong>. How do you ask your partner a question and answer theirs?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Question:** *„Kann man in Deutschland am Sonntag im Supermarkt einkaufen?“* or *„Wo kaufst du am Sonntag Brötchen?“*
  * **Answer:** *„Nein, am Sonntag sind die Supermärkte geschlossen, aber die Bäckerei am Bahnhof ist geöffnet.“*
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 146 [Sprechen Teil 3 Trap]:</strong> In *Sprechen Teil 3*, you draw a picture card showing a **glass of water** or an **open window**. How do you make a polite request and respond?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Polite Request:** *„Können Sie mir bitte ein Glas Wasser geben?“* / *„Können Sie bitte das Fenster aufmachen (oder zumachen)?“*
  * **Polite Response:** *„Ja, gern! Bitte schön.“* (or *„Ja, natürlich, kein Problem.“*) ([Chapter 17](../a1/book/chapter-17-everyday-roleplays-requests-complaints-emergencies.mdx)).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 147 [Dative Body & Feeling Trap]:</strong> How do you say <em>"How are you?"</em>, <em>"I am doing well"</em>, and <em>"I am cold"</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **Wie geht es Ihnen?** (formal) / **Wie geht es dir?** (informal)
  * **Mir geht es gut, danke.** (❌ Never say *Ich bin gut*!)
  * **Mir ist kalt / Mir ist warm.** (❌ Never say *Ich bin kalt*!)
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 148 [Verb with No Preposition Trap]:</strong> How do you say <em>"I am waiting for the bus"</em> vs. <em>"I am looking for the train station"</em>?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * *to wait for* = **warten auf (+ Accusative)**: *Ich warte **auf den Bus**.*
  * *to look for* = **suchen (+ direct Accusative, NO *für*!)**: *Ich suche **den Bahnhof**.* (❌ Never say *Ich suche für den Bahnhof*!).
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 149 [Floor Trap in Germany]:</strong> On a directory sign in a German building, what do <strong>EG</strong>, <strong>1. OG</strong>, and <strong>UG</strong> mean?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:**
  * **EG (*Erdgeschoss*)** = Ground floor (1st floor in US/India).
  * **1. OG (*1. Obergeschoss* / *1. Stock*)** = First floor **above** the ground floor (2nd floor in US/India!).
  * **UG (*Untergeschoss* / *Keller*)** = Basement.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🃏 <strong>Card 150 [Lesen True/False Trap]:</strong> A website says: <em>"Kartenreservierung nur telefonisch möglich."</em> The test statement says: <em>"Sie können die Tickets im Internet reservieren."</em> Is that **Richtig** or **Falsch**?</summary>

* ✅ **Answer:** **Falsch**!
* ⚠️ **Why:** Watch out for little signal words like **nur** (*only*), **nicht** (*not*), **kein** (*no*), **ab** (*starting from*), **bis** (*until*), and **außer** (*except*). *Nur telefonisch* means **only by telephone**, not online! ([Goethe Lesen](../a1/exam-center/goethe-lesen.mdx)).
</details>
