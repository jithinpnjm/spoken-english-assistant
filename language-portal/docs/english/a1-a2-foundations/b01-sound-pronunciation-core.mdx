# Sound and Pronunciation Core

**Level:** A1-A2 · **Goal:** say short English chunks clearly enough that people understand you the first time.

Pronunciation is **not** about sounding like a native speaker. It is about being understandable: clear sounds, the right stress, natural pauses, and the right melody for questions. Speaking fast does not make you fluent — speaking *clearly* does.

## The pattern

> **Listen → notice one sound or stress point → say a short chunk slowly → repeat it naturally.**

Work on one thing at a time. A short sentence said clearly twice is better practice than a long sentence said once.

## Sounds that cause the most misunderstandings

| Sound pair | How to make it | Practise with |
|---|---|---|
| **/v/ vs /w/** | /v/: top teeth touch the bottom lip, voice on. /w/: round your lips, no teeth. | *very well, west village, we value your work* |
| **/θ/ and /ð/ (th)** | Tongue tip lightly between the teeth, then blow (think) or voice (this). | *three, thanks, think / this, there, other* |
| **/p/ vs /f/** | /p/: lips close and pop. /f/: teeth on lip, air flows. | *pan / fan, pull / full* |
| **/iː/ vs /ɪ/** | /iː/ is long and smiling; /ɪ/ is short and relaxed. | *sheep / ship, leave / live, seat / sit* |

### Past-tense endings: three sounds

The *-ed* ending is spelled one way but pronounced three ways. Getting it right makes your past tense audible:

| Sound | After | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| /t/ | voiceless sounds (p, k, s, sh, ch, f) | worked, stopped, watched, fixed |
| /d/ | voiced sounds and vowels | called, played, cleaned, joined |
| /ɪd/ (extra syllable) | *t* or *d* | wanted, needed, tested, decided |

## Model sentences

- *My name is Alex. That's A-L-E-X.*
- *Could you repeat that, please?*
- *I live in Berlin.*
- *Where is the station?* ↘
- *Is this seat free?* ↗
- *I need a little more time.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I speak fast to sound fluent.* | *I speak clearly to sound fluent.* | Fluency is clarity plus rhythm, not speed. |
| *I no pause.* | *I need to pause between ideas.* | Use *need to* + verb, and pause between idea chunks. |
| All words equally strong | *I NEED a TAXI.* | English stresses the meaning words; small words (*a, to, the*) are weak. |
| *wery vell* | *very well* | /v/ uses teeth on lip; /w/ uses rounded lips. |
| *I work-ed* (two syllables) | *I worked* /wɜːkt/ | Only *t*/*d* endings add a syllable. |

## Practice drills

1. **Slow, then natural.** Say each model sentence slowly, then again at normal speed with one pause.
2. **Minimal pairs.** Read a pair aloud (*ship / sheep*); ask a partner or the coach which one you said.
3. **Stress marking.** Write a sentence and underline the stressed words before you say it.
4. **Question melody.** Ask the same question twice: once as yes/no (*Do you live here?* ↗), once as wh- (*Where do you live?* ↘).

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] spell my name, email address, and street aloud without stopping
- [ ] hear and say the difference between *v* and *w*, and *ship* and *sheep*
- [ ] stress the important words in a short sentence
- [ ] use rising intonation for yes/no questions and falling intonation for wh- questions
- [ ] pronounce *-ed* endings correctly in *worked, called, wanted*
