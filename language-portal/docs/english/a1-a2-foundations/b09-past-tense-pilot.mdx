# Past Simple

**Level:** A1-A2 · **Goal:** talk about finished actions and situations — yesterday, last week, years ago — without slipping back into the present.

*"Yesterday I go to office"* is the single most common beginner error. This module fixes it step by step: first *was/were*, then regular and irregular verbs, then negatives and questions, then connected stories.

## The whole system on one card

| | be (states) | other verbs (actions) |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | *I / he / she / it **was** · you / we / they **were*** | *I **worked**. She **went**.* |
| Negative | *I **wasn't** · they **weren't*** | *I **didn't work**. She **didn't go**.* |
| Question | ***Was** it good? **Were** you there?* | ***Did** you **work**? Where **did** she **go**?* |

The key rule: **in negatives and questions, *did* carries the past — the main verb goes back to the base form.**
*didn't **go*** (not *didn't went*) · *Did you **buy**…?* (not *Did you bought…?*)

## Lesson 1 — Was / were for past states

Use *was/were* for past **situations**: where you were, how you felt, what something was like.

- *I was at home yesterday.* · *She was tired after work.* · *We were in Berlin last weekend.* · *They were late because the train was delayed.*

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I am at home yesterday.* | *I was at home yesterday.* | *yesterday* is past → *was*. |
| *We was late.* | *We were late.* | *were* with we/you/they. |
| *She were tired.* | *She was tired.* | *was* with I/he/she/it. |

**Drill:** change to past — *I am busy today.* · Answer: *Where were you last Saturday?*

## Lesson 2 — Regular past verbs (-ed)

For most verbs add **-ed**. The form is the same for every person.

- *I worked from home yesterday.* · *She cleaned the kitchen.* · *We watched a movie last night.* · *He called his friend after lunch.*

Spelling: *stop → stopped, plan → planned* (double) · *live → lived* (+d) · *study → studied* (y → ied) · *play → played*.
Pronunciation: *worked* /t/, *called* /d/, *wanted* /ɪd/.

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Yesterday I work from home.* | *Yesterday I worked from home.* | Finished time → past form. |
| *She cleaneded the room.* | *She cleaned the room.* | Add -ed only once. |
| *He call me yesterday.* | *He called me yesterday.* | *call* is regular → *called*. |

## Lesson 3 — Common irregular past verbs

Many everyday verbs do not use -ed. Learn them as **chunks**, not a list: *went home, ate dinner, took the bus, bought groceries, saw a doctor, came late.*

| base | past | base | past | base | past |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| go | went | have | had | get | got |
| eat | ate | make | made | come | came |
| see | saw | take | took | give | gave |
| buy | bought | think | thought | bring | brought |
| do | did | say | said | tell | told |
| find | found | feel | felt | leave | left |
| know | knew | put | put | run | ran |

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *I goed to office.* | *I went to the office.* |
| *We eated dinner.* | *We ate dinner.* |
| *She buyed groceries.* | *She bought groceries.* |

## Lesson 4 — Past negative: didn't + base verb

- *I didn't go to the office yesterday.* · *She didn't call me.* · *We didn't eat outside.* · *He didn't finish the task.*
- For *be*: *It wasn't expensive. They weren't at home.*

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I didn't went to work.* | *I didn't go to work.* | After *didn't*, base verb. |
| *She didn't called me.* | *She didn't call me.* | No double past. |
| *We no ate dinner.* | *We didn't eat dinner.* | Negatives need *didn't*. |

## Lesson 5 — Past questions with did

- Yes/no: ***Did** you **go** to work yesterday? — Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.*
- Wh-: *Where **did** you **eat** lunch? What **did** you **buy**?*
- For *be*: *Were you tired? Was the meeting useful?*

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *Did you went to work?* | *Did you go to work?* |
| *Where you went yesterday?* | *Where did you go yesterday?* |
| *What did you bought?* | *What did you buy?* |

## Lesson 6 — Past time expressions

*yesterday, yesterday morning, last night, last week, last year, two days ago, in 2020, when I was a child*

These words anchor a **finished** time, so they need the past simple — not the present perfect.

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I have visited them last week.* | *I visited them last week.* | Finished time → past simple. |
| *Yesterday I go shopping.* | *Yesterday I went shopping.* | *yesterday* needs a past verb. |
| *I came here before two years.* | *I came here two years ago.* | Count back from now with *ago*. |

## Lesson 7 — Past tense for work and travel

Work and travel stories need a clear **sequence** of finished events.

- *I joined the meeting at ten.* · *I reached the station late.* · *The train arrived fifteen minutes late.* · *I missed the connection, so I took the next train.*

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *I reach office late yesterday.* | *I reached the office late yesterday.* |
| *Train was delay.* | *The train was delayed.* |
| *I miss the meeting yesterday.* | *I missed the meeting yesterday.* |

## Lesson 8 — A "yesterday" story

A simple story has three parts — **first, then, finally** — and stays in the past from start to end.

> *Yesterday morning I took the train. It was crowded, but I reached the office on time. First, I finished two reports. Then I had lunch with a colleague. Finally, I came home and cooked dinner.*

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Yesterday I go office then I came home.* | *Yesterday I went to the office, then I came home.* | Keep every verb in the past. |
| *First I went, then I am cooking.* | *First I went out, then I cooked.* | Don't switch tense mid-story. |
| *I was sleep early.* | *I slept early.* / *I went to bed early.* | Action → past verb, not *was* + verb. |

## Lesson 9 — Past-tense correction lab

Find and fix the mistake, then say the correct sentence aloud:

1. *I go to the shop yesterday.* → *I went to the shop yesterday.*
2. *Did you bought anything?* → *Did you buy anything?*
3. *We didn't went outside.* → *We didn't go outside.*
4. *They was tired after work.* → *They were tired after work.*
5. *She buyed a new phone.* → *She bought a new phone.*

## Lesson 10 — Speaking checkpoint

Speak for **45-60 seconds** about yesterday or last weekend. Include:

- one sentence with *was/were*
- three regular and two irregular verbs
- one thing you *didn't* do
- one question you ask the listener with *did*
- a time word (*yesterday, last…, …ago*)

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] use *was/were* with the right subject
- [ ] use regular and common irregular past forms
- [ ] make negatives with *didn't + base verb* and questions with *did + base verb*
- [ ] use *yesterday, last…, …ago* with the past simple (not the present perfect)
- [ ] tell a five-sentence story without switching back to the present
