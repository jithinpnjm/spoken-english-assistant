# Presentations and Storytelling

**Level:** B2-C1 · **Goal:** present an idea or project with a clear opening, visible structure, evidence, and a memorable conclusion — and handle questions calmly.

Presentation English guides the listener on a **journey** instead of giving them disconnected details. Listeners can't scroll back, so you have to tell them where you are going, where you are, and where you've been.

## The pattern

> **Opening → agenda → main points with evidence → takeaway → next step.**
>
> *Good morning. Today I'll explain why we should move our reporting to the new platform (opening). I'll cover three things: the problem, the solution, and the results from the pilot (agenda). … To summarise, the pilot cut report time by 60% (takeaway). I'm asking for approval to roll it out in Q3 (next step).*

## Signposting phrases

| Stage | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Opening | *Good morning, everyone. Today I'll be talking about… · The purpose of this presentation is to…* |
| Agenda | *I'll cover three main areas: … · I've divided the talk into…* |
| First point | *Let's start with… · To begin with, …* |
| Moving on | *Moving on to… · Let's now turn to… · That brings me to my second point.* |
| Adding | *In addition, … · What's more, …* |
| Contrasting | *However, … · That said, … · On the other hand, …* |
| Emphasising | *The key point here is… · It's worth noting that… · Crucially, …* |
| Example | *For instance, … · Take the example of…* |
| Summarising | *To summarise, … · So, the three things to remember are…* |
| Closing | *In conclusion, I recommend… · Thank you — I'm happy to take questions.* |

## Explaining charts verbally

- *This chart shows monthly active users over the last year.*
- *As you can see, there's a sharp increase in March, followed by a plateau.*
- *Revenue rose steadily / dropped slightly / peaked in June / remained stable.*
- *The blue line is this year; the grey line is last year.*
- *I'd like to draw your attention to this gap here.*

Describe the **message** first (*usage doubled after the launch*), then the detail.

## The STAR method for impact stories

| Step | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | Where and when? | *Last year our release process took two days and often failed.* |
| **T**ask | What was your responsibility? | *I was asked to make releases safer and faster.* |
| **A**ction | What did *you* do? (use *I*) | *I automated the checks and introduced staged rollouts.* |
| **R**esult | What changed? Numbers if possible. | *Release time fell to two hours and failed releases dropped by 80%.* |

## Q&A recovery phrases

- *Let me make sure I understand the question — are you asking about…?*
- *That's a fair challenge. Here's how I see it…*
- *I don't have that data to hand. I'll check and get back to you today.*
- *That's slightly outside the scope of today, but I'm happy to discuss it afterwards.*
- *Could we hold that thought until the end? I'll cover it in the last section.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *I will tell about my project.* | *I'll explain my project and the impact it had.* | *tell* needs an object; *explain* + purpose. |
| *First point is problem, second solution, like that.* | *I'll cover three points: the problem, the solution, and the result.* | Clean agenda sentence. |
| *That's all only.* | *To summarise, the project improved reliability and reduced manual effort.* | End with a conclusion. |
| *We did many things.* (in STAR) | *I designed…, I automated…* | Show *your* actions. |
| *The graph is going up.* | *Usage rose sharply after the March launch.* | Precise trend verb + cause. |

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] open with a clear purpose and agenda
- [ ] signpost every transition
- [ ] describe a chart by its message first
- [ ] tell a STAR story with measurable results
- [ ] close with a call to action and handle difficult questions
