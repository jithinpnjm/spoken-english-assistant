# Negotiation and Influence

**Level:** B2-C1 · **Goal:** negotiate scope, timeline and resources without sounding aggressive or passive — by framing trade-offs, constraints and alternatives.

Negotiation English protects **quality, timeline and scope** while keeping collaboration professional. The goal is not to win an argument — it is to reach a **workable agreement**. The most useful sentence in this module is conditional: *We can do X **if** we adjust Y.*

## The pattern

> **Proposal → trade-off → constraint → alternative → alignment question.**
>
> *I can support the June date (proposal) if we reduce the first release to the core features (trade-off). With the current team, we can't do full scope safely by then (constraint). Alternatively, we keep full scope and launch in July (alternative). Which matters more to you — the date or the scope? (alignment)*

## Conditional bargaining language

- *We **can** do that **if** we get one more engineer.*
- *We **could** launch earlier **provided that** QA signs off by Monday.*
- ***As long as** we keep the rollback option, I'm comfortable with that.*
- ***If** you **could** give us the data by Friday, we **would** be able to deliver on the 20th.*
- ***Unless** we cut scope, the date isn't realistic.*

## Model sentences

- *I can support that timeline if we reduce the scope of the first release.*
- *The trade-off is that moving faster may increase validation risk.*
- *Given the current dependency, I'd recommend a phased rollout instead.*
- *Could we agree on the minimum scope for this week and move the rest to the next iteration?*
- *I understand the priority, but I don't think we can commit to both speed and full scope safely.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *This deadline is impossible.* | *I'm concerned that this deadline isn't realistic with the current scope.* | Professional concern tied to a cause. |
| *We cannot do this, no resources.* | *With the current capacity, we can either reduce scope or extend the timeline.* | Offer options instead of only refusing. |
| *You decide, I don't know.* | *My recommendation is a phased rollout because it reduces risk.* | Take ownership with a recommendation. |
| *OK, OK, we will try.* (under pressure) | *I'll confirm by tomorrow once I've checked with the team.* | Don't agree to what you can't deliver. |
| *If you will give us more time, we can finish.* | *If you give us more time, we can finish.* | No *will* in the *if*-clause. |

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] propose, and frame a trade-off with *if / provided that / unless*
- [ ] push back politely and offer at least one alternative
- [ ] disagree with someone senior without sounding defensive
- [ ] summarise an agreement clearly
