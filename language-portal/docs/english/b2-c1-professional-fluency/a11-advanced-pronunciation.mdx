# Advanced Pronunciation

**Level:** B2-C1 · **Goal:** be easy to understand in fast meetings and calls — with correct stress on technical words, confident intonation, and natural connected speech.

At this level, listeners rarely misunderstand single sounds. They struggle with **stress** (*deVELopment* vs *DEvelopment*), **rhythm** (every word equally strong), and **intonation** (a statement that sounds like a question). These are the fixes with the biggest impact on clarity.

## Lessons in this module

### 1 — Word stress in technical vocabulary

| Word | Stress | Word | Stress |
|---|---|---|---|
| development | de**VEL**opment | analysis | a**NAL**ysis |
| analytics | ana**LYT**ics | architecture | **AR**chitecture |
| infrastructure | **IN**frastructure | configuration | configu**RA**tion |
| category | **CAT**egory | technology | tech**NOL**ogy |
| photograph / photography | **PHO**tograph / pho**TOG**raphy | economy / economic | e**CON**omy / eco**NOM**ic |
| record (noun) / record (verb) | **REC**ord / re**CORD** | present (noun) / present (verb) | **PRES**ent / pre**SENT** |

Words ending in *-tion, -sion, -ic, -ical, -ity* usually stress the syllable **just before** the ending: *configuRAtion, techNOLogy → technoLOgical, reliaBILity*.

### 2 — Sentence stress for contrast

Move the stress to change the meaning:

- *I didn't say **HE** broke it.* (someone else did)
- *I didn't say he **BROKE** it.* (he did something else)
- *It's not the **COST** that worries me — it's the **TIME**.*

### 3 — Intonation for confidence

Statements, decisions and recommendations **fall** at the end. A rising end makes them sound like questions — uncertain.

- *I recommend option B.* ↘ (confident) vs *I recommend option B?* ↗ (unsure)
- Lists: rise, rise, fall — *We need design ↗, testing ↗, and approval ↘.*

### 4 — Linking and connected speech

Native speakers link words, so "words" disappear in fast speech. Learning the patterns helps both listening and speaking:

- consonant + vowel: *turn_it_off* → "tur-ni-toff" · *pick_it_up*
- same consonant: *next_time*, *big_game* (one longer sound)
- /t/ /d/ + /j/: *would you* → "wou-dju", *did you* → "di-dju", *don't you* → "don-chu"

### 5 — Weak forms

Small grammar words are usually **unstressed** and reduced to /ə/ (schwa):

| Word | Strong | Weak (normal) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| to | /tuː/ | /tə/ | *I need tə go.* |
| for | /fɔː/ | /fə/ | *It's fə you.* |
| can | /kæn/ | /kən/ | *I kən help.* (but *Yes, I CAN.*) |
| was | /wɒz/ | /wəz/ | *It wəz late.* |
| and | /ænd/ | /ən/ | *salt ən pepper* |

### 6 — Pausing for clarity

Pause after the subject when it is long, between list items, and before the key point: *The team that built the payment service / has moved to another project.*

### 7 — Difficult consonants

- **/v/ vs /w/** — *very well, virtual workshop, video, website*
- **th** — *three, thirty, through, the other, weather*
- **consonant clusters** — *months, texts, asked, sixth, strengths* (don't add extra vowels: *istation* → *station*)
- **final consonants** — say the end of the word: *worked, needs, asked*

### 8 — Indian-English pronunciation repair points

Indian English is a legitimate, widely understood variety. These are the points that most often cause misunderstandings with international listeners:

| Feature | Adjust towards |
|---|---|
| /v/ and /w/ merged | separate them: teeth-lip /v/, rounded /w/ |
| /t/ /d/ retroflex, /θ/ → /t̪/ | softer /t/ /d/; tongue between the teeth for *th* |
| syllable-timed rhythm (all syllables equal) | stress-timed rhythm: strong stressed syllables, reduced weak ones |
| stress on the wrong syllable (*deVELopment* said *DEvelopment*) | check stress of key technical words in a dictionary |
| added vowels before *s*-clusters (*iskool*) | *school, station, speak* |

### 9 — German-context names and places

If you live or work in Germany, English conversations are full of German names. Say them in a German way inside English sentences:

| Written | Say (approx.) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| München / Munich | *MÜN-shen* / English *MYOO-nik* | Use the English name in English: *Munich* |
| Köln / Cologne | *KÖLN* / *kə-LOHN* | English name: *Cologne* |
| Straße | *SHTRAH-seh* | ß = double s |
| Jürgen | *YÜR-gen* | *J* = English *y* |
| Weber | *VAY-ber* | *W* = /v/ |
| Stuttgart | *SHTOOT-gart* | *St* at the start = *sht* |

### 10 — Pronunciation checkpoint

Record a one-minute technical explanation. Check: stress on 10 key technical words, falling intonation on your recommendation, and linked speech in three common phrases.
