# Advanced Fluency

**Level:** B2-C1 · **Goal:** speak at length — in meetings, interviews and presentations — with controlled pace, few fillers, clear structure, and the ability to recover when you lose a word.

At C1, fluency is **control**, not speed. A fluent speaker pauses deliberately, signals structure, stresses what matters, rephrases smoothly when a word won't come, and keeps going after a correction.

## The four habits

### 1. Pause between chunks, not inside them

> *The main risk / is the vendor dependency. // If they slip, / we slip. // So I'd suggest / a fallback option.*

A half-second pause after a key point sounds confident. A pause in the middle of a phrase (*the main… risk… is the…*) sounds hesitant.

### 2. Replace fillers with useful thinking time

| Instead of… | Say… |
|---|---|
| *um, uh, erm* | a silent pause |
| *like, basically, actually, you know* (as fillers) | nothing — or a precise word |
| *what to say…* | *Let me think about that for a second.* |
| *how do you call it…* | *What's the word… · It's a kind of…* |
| *I mean… I mean…* | *In other words, …* / *To put it another way, …* |

### 3. Signpost your structure

*There are two reasons. First, … Second, … · The key point is… · Let me give you an example. · So, to sum up, …*

Structure words give you **planning time** and make you easier to follow.

### 4. Rephrase instead of stopping

When the word won't come, **describe it and move on**:

- *It's the process where we check the code before merging — the code review.*
- *We need someone who takes responsibility for the whole thing — an owner.*

## Useful thinking-aloud language

- *My initial thought is… but let me think it through.*
- *There are a couple of ways to look at this.*
- *If I had to choose, I'd say…*
- *I'm not 100% sure, but my best guess is…*
- *That's a good question — let me break it down.*

## Common mistakes

| Habit | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *And then… and then… and also…* | *First… Second… Finally…* | Signposts beat chains of *and*. |
| Stopping when a word is missing | Paraphrase and continue | The listener needs the meaning, not the exact word. |
| Speaking faster to sound fluent | Slower, with pauses at chunk boundaries | Speed without pauses is harder to follow. |
| Apologising for every mistake | Correct once, briefly, and continue | Over-apologising draws attention to errors. |
| Equal stress on every word | Stress the key word: *We need a DECISION today.* | Stress carries meaning in English. |

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] speak for two minutes with clear structure and signposting
- [ ] use silent pauses and thinking phrases instead of fillers
- [ ] paraphrase a missing word and keep going
- [ ] self-correct once and continue without losing the thread
- [ ] use stress to show contrast and emphasis

## Homework

Pick a work topic. Record a two-minute explanation on three consecutive days. Each day, count your fillers and try to cut them by a third.
