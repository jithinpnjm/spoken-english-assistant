# Professional Register

**Level:** B2-C1 · **Goal:** upgrade casual or unclear wording into professional, respectful, action-oriented English — and choose the right level of directness for the audience.

**Register** is the tone and word choice you use for a particular audience. Professional register is **clear, concise, respectful and action-oriented**. It is *not* the same as formal or complicated: *Could you review this by Friday?* is professional; *I would be most grateful if you could kindly peruse the attached at your earliest convenience* is not — it's just long.

## The pattern

> **Context → point → reason → request or next action.**
>
> *Quick update on the migration (context): we're two days behind (point) because the vendor API changed (reason). I'd like to move the go-live to Thursday — can you confirm by 3 p.m.? (request)*

## The directness dial

| Too direct | Professional | Too indirect |
|---|---|---|
| *You are wrong, this won't work.* | *I see your point, but I have a concern about this approach.* | *I was just maybe wondering if possibly there might be a small issue…* |
| *Send it fast.* | *Could you send it by 2 p.m.?* | *Whenever you get a chance, if it's not too much trouble…* (for something urgent) |
| *I can't do, too much dependency.* | *I can't complete it yet because two dependencies are unresolved.* | *It's a bit complicated at the moment.* |

Aim for the middle: **polite in form, specific in content**.

### Softening tools

| Tool | Example |
|---|---|
| Modal verbs | *This **could** cause delays. We **might** want to revisit this.* |
| Softening adverbs | *This is **slightly** over budget. It's **a bit** risky.* |
| Tentative openers | ***I wonder if** we could… **It might be worth** checking…* |
| Questions instead of statements | *Could we test it first?* instead of *We must test it first.* |
| Past tense for distance | ***I was wondering** if you had time to…* |
| *not + positive word* | *That's **not ideal**.* (instead of *That's bad.*) · *I'm **not entirely convinced**.* |

## Model sentences

- *Could you clarify the expected timeline for this task?*
- *I understand the urgency, but I'm concerned about the release risk.*
- *The task is on track, and I'll share another update by 4 p.m.*
- *I recommend validating this in staging before production.*
- *Unfortunately, the vendor has delayed delivery by a week. To limit the impact, we've moved testing forward.*

## Bad news: the four-part structure

1. **Signal it:** *Unfortunately… / I'm afraid… / I have some bad news about…*
2. **State it plainly:** *We won't hit the 15th.*
3. **Reason in one sentence:** *The vendor API changed on Monday.*
4. **What you're doing / options:** *We can ship a reduced version on the 15th or the full version on the 22nd. I recommend the 22nd.*

Don't bury the news under three paragraphs of context.

## Escalation without drama

- *I'd like to escalate this because it needs a decision I can't make.*
- *We've tried A and B without success. We now need help from the platform team.*
- *If we don't resolve this by Wednesday, the launch is at risk.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *You are wrong, this won't work.* | *I see your point, but I have a concern about this approach.* | Disagree with the idea, not the person. |
| *Send it fast.* | *Could you send it when you have a chance — ideally before 2?* | Polite request with a clear time. |
| *I can't do, too much dependency.* | *I can't complete it yet because there are unresolved dependencies.* | Full sentence, specific reason. |
| *Kindly do the needful.* | *Could you please update the record?* | Say the actual action; *do the needful* is regional and vague. |
| *Please revert.* | *Please reply / get back to me.* | *revert* = return to a previous state outside South Asia. |
| *As per my last email…* (to a senior) | *Just following up on my email from Monday.* | *As per my last email* often reads as irritated. |
| *There is a small small issue.* | *There's a minor issue.* | Precise adjective instead of repetition. |

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] structure a message as context → point → reason → next action
- [ ] adjust directness without becoming vague
- [ ] deliver bad news in four clear parts
- [ ] frame a decision and a risk for a senior audience
- [ ] escalate calmly with facts and a deadline
