# Sentence Structure — Reference

English relies on **word order** to show who does what to whom: *The dog bit the man* ≠ *The man bit the dog*. The basic order is **Subject – Verb – Object**.

## The five basic patterns

| Pattern | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | S + V | *The baby cried. He arrived.* |
| 2 | S + V + O | *She reads books. They built a house.* |
| 3 | S + linking verb + complement | *She is a doctor. He seems tired. The soup tastes good.* |
| 4 | S + V + indirect O + direct O | *She gave him a book.* = *She gave a book to him.* |
| 5 | S + V + O + complement | *They elected her president. I found it useful.* |

Linking verbs: *be, seem, appear, become, feel, look, smell, sound, taste, remain, stay.*

## Questions

**Yes/no:** move the auxiliary before the subject; if there is none, use *do / does / did*.
*She is happy → Is she happy? · He plays → Does he play? · She called → Did she call?*

**Wh-:** *wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb*: *What did she say? Where do you live? Why are you late?*

**Subject questions** (the *who/what* is the subject): no *do*: *Who called you? What happened?*

**who vs whom:** answer with *he* → *who*; with *him* → *whom* (formal). *Who called? — He did.* · *Whom did you call? — I called him.* In everyday speech, *who* is used for both.

**Indirect questions:** statement order after a polite opener:
*Where is the station? → Could you tell me **where the station is**?*
*What time does it open? → Do you know **what time it opens**?*
*Is the meeting cancelled? → I'd like to know **if the meeting is cancelled**.*

**Question tags:** positive statement → negative tag, and vice versa:
*She's a teacher, isn't she? · They don't know, do they? · You can drive, can't you? · He never lies, does he? · I'm late, aren't I? · Let's go, shall we?*

## Negation

- *do/does/did + not + base verb*: *She doesn't like tea.* (never *doesn't likes*)
- *be + not*: *I'm not tired. They weren't late.*
- *have + not* (perfect): *I haven't finished.*
- *modal + not*: *can't, won't, shouldn't, mustn't, couldn't.*

**One negative only:** *I don't know nothing* ✗ → *I don't know anything* ✓ / *I know nothing* ✓. After a negative verb, use *any-*: *She didn't go anywhere.*

## Adjective order

**Opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose → noun**

*a lovely little old wooden table · a big red car · a beautiful Japanese garden · an old brown leather bag · new running shoes*

In real speech, two or three adjectives is plenty.

## Adverb placement

**Frequency adverbs** (*always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never*):
- before the main verb: *I usually walk to work.*
- after *be*: *She is always on time.*
- after the first auxiliary: *He has never been abroad. You should always check.*

**Manner → place → time** at the end of a clause: *He drove carefully to work yesterday.*

**Degree adverbs** go before the word they modify: *quite difficult, really fast, almost ready, too expensive, good enough.*

**Time adverbs** can also go at the front: *Yesterday, I saw Maria.*

## Connecting ideas

**Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS):** *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* — comma before them when joining two full clauses: *It was late, but we kept working.*

**Subordinating conjunctions:**

| Job | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reason | *because, since, as* | *I stayed in because it was cold.* |
| Contrast | *although, even though, while, whereas* | *Although it rained, we enjoyed it.* |
| Time | *when, while, before, after, until, as soon as* | *Call me when you arrive.* |
| Condition | *if, unless, provided that, as long as* | *Unless you hurry, we'll be late.* |
| Purpose | *so that, in order to* | *I left early so that I wouldn't miss the train.* |
| Result | *so … that, such … that* | *It was so hot that we couldn't sleep.* |

Subordinate clause first → comma: *Although it was raining, we went out.* Main clause first → usually no comma.

## Relative clauses

| Defining (no commas) | Non-defining (commas) |
|---|---|
| identifies which one; essential | adds extra information; removable |
| *who, which, that, whose, where, when* | *who, which, whose, where, when* — **never *that*** |
| *The man who called you is my brother.* | *My brother, who lives in Dublin, is visiting.* |

**Reduced relatives:** *The man (who is) standing by the door… · The report (that was) submitted late…*

## Common word order mistakes

| # | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *I like very much English.* | *I like English very much.* |
| 2 | *He goes always to the gym.* | *He always goes to the gym.* |
| 3 | *I speak English good.* | *I speak English well.* |
| 4 | *What you are doing?* | *What are you doing?* |
| 5 | *Do you know where is the station?* | *Do you know where the station is?* |
| 6 | *The car red is mine.* | *The red car is mine.* |
| 7 | *She told to me the truth.* | *She told me the truth.* |
| 8 | *I don't know nothing.* | *I don't know anything.* |
| 9 | *He is a man very intelligent.* | *He is a very intelligent man.* |
| 10 | *I have seen yesterday a film.* | *I saw a film yesterday.* |
| 11 | *My car, that I bought last year, …* | *My car, which I bought last year, …* |
| 12 | *The woman who she helped me…* | *The woman who helped me…* |
