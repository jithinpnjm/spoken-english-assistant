# Tenses — Reference

Tense tells the listener **when** something happens and **whether it still matters now**. *I eat lunch* (habit), *I'm eating lunch* (right now), *I've eaten lunch* (done — don't bring me food) are three different messages.

Before you speak, ask three questions:
1. **When** — past, now, or future?
2. **State or action?** States (*know, like, own*) are not normally continuous.
3. **Is there a time word?** A finished time (*yesterday, in 2020*) → past simple. No time, or relevant now → present perfect.

## Present simple — habits, facts, schedules

| | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | *work* | *don't work* | *Do … work?* |
| he / she / it | *works* | *doesn't work* | *Does … work?* |

**Uses:** routines (*She wakes up at 6.*), facts (*Water boils at 100 °C.*), timetables (*The train leaves at 9.*), instructions (*You turn left and go straight on.*).
**Signal words:** *always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, on Mondays, twice a week.*
**Spelling of -s:** *works · watches, goes, fixes · studies · plays · has.*

## Present continuous — now, temporary, arranged

*am / is / are + verb-ing*

**Uses:** right now (*I'm talking to a client.*), temporary (*She's staying with her sister this week.*), change in progress (*Prices are rising.*), fixed arrangements (*We're meeting the client at 3 tomorrow.*).
**Signal words:** *now, right now, at the moment, currently, this week, Look!*
**Spelling:** *make → making · run → running · lie → lying.*

### Stative verbs — normally not continuous

| Category | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Mind | *know, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean, think (= have an opinion)* |
| Feelings | *like, love, hate, prefer, want, need, wish* |
| Senses | *see, hear, smell, taste* (involuntary) |
| Possession | *have (= own), own, belong, contain* |
| Other | *seem, appear, cost, weigh, include* |

Some change meaning: *I **think** it's fine* (opinion) vs *I'm **thinking** about it* (considering) · *I **have** a car* vs *I'm **having** lunch*.

## Present perfect — past with a link to now

*have / has + past participle*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Experience (no time given) | *I've visited Paris twice.* |
| Result now | *She's broken her leg* (so she can't walk). |
| Recent (*just, already, yet*) | *I've just sent it. Have you finished yet?* |
| Unfinished period (*for, since*) | *He's lived here for 10 years / since 2015.* |

**Position:** *ever, never, already, just* between *have* and the participle; *yet* at the end.
**Common participles:** *gone, been, seen, done, written, taken, given, eaten, known, spoken, chosen, broken, forgotten, begun, flown, worn.*
**Test:** does it matter **now**, with no finished time? → present perfect. Just a record of the past? → past simple.

### Present perfect continuous

*have / has been + -ing* — an activity from the past until now, focus on duration: *I've been working here since 2020. We've been waiting for an hour.*

## Past simple — finished, at a known time

Regular *-ed* (*worked, played*); irregular forms must be learned (*went, saw, had*). Negatives and questions: *didn't + base verb*, *Did … + base verb?*

**Signal words:** *yesterday, last week, in 2020, … ago, when I was young, at 3 p.m.*

| Base | Past | Base | Past |
|---|---|---|---|
| go | went | see | saw |
| have | had | do | did |
| say | said | get | got |
| make | made | come | came |
| know | knew | think | thought |
| take | took | give | gave |
| find | found | tell | told |
| feel | felt | become | became |
| leave | left | put | put |
| run | ran | bring | brought |

**Spelling:** *stop → stopped · love → loved · study → studied · play → played.*

## Past continuous — background in progress

*was / were + -ing*

- Background + interruption: *I **was cooking** when the phone **rang**.*
- Two actions at the same time: *I was reading while she was cooking.*
- Setting a scene: *It was raining and everyone was rushing home.*

*while* typically introduces the longer, ongoing action; *when* often introduces the short interrupting one. *While I slept, he arrived* is grammatical, but *While I was sleeping, he arrived* is the more natural way to show background.

## Past perfect — the earlier past

*had + past participle*: *When I arrived, the meeting **had** already **started**.* Use it only when you need to show that one past event happened **before** another.

## The future

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will** | decision made now, offer, promise, general prediction | *I'll carry that. It'll probably rain.* |
| **going to** | plan decided before; prediction from evidence | *We're going to visit Rome. Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.* |
| **present continuous** | fixed arrangement | *I'm seeing the dentist at 10.* |
| **present simple** | timetable | *The flight leaves at 7:45.* |

**Decision path:** decided just now → *will*. In the calendar with a time → present continuous. Decided before / evidence now → *going to*. Neutral prediction → *will*.

## Common tense mistakes

| # | Wrong | Right | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *I am knowing the answer.* | *I know the answer.* | stative verb |
| 2 | *I have been here since 3 years.* | *I have been here for 3 years.* | *for* + duration, *since* + point |
| 3 | *I did not went there.* | *I did not go there.* | *did* + base verb |
| 4 | *She don't know.* | *She doesn't know.* | *doesn't* with he/she/it |
| 5 | *I have met him yesterday.* | *I met him yesterday.* | finished time → past simple |
| 6 | *If I will go, I will call you.* | *If I go, I will call you.* | no *will* in the *if*-clause |
| 7 | *He is my best friend since school.* | *He has been my best friend since school.* | past → now: present perfect |
| 8 | *I am working here since 2020.* | *I have been working here since 2020.* | past → now: present perfect continuous |
| 9 | *I will to call you.* | *I will call you.* | no *to* after *will* |
| 10 | *She is go to study medicine.* | *She is going to study medicine.* | *be going to* + verb |

**since vs for:** *since* + a point in time (*since 2020, since Monday, since I was a child*); *for* + a length of time (*for three years, for two weeks*).
