# Preposition Patterns

**Level:** B1-B2 · **Goal:** eliminate translation errors by learning prepositions as fixed chunks with their verbs, nouns, or adjectives.

Prepositions (*in, on, at, to, for, with*) are the glue of the English language. They are also the hardest part of the language to master because **they rarely translate directly from your mother tongue**. 

In some languages, you might "discuss about" a topic. In English, you just "discuss" it. If you try to guess prepositions based on logic, you will often be wrong. At B1-B2, the goal is to stop thinking about prepositions as individual words, and start learning them as **fixed patterns or chunks**.

## The Core Foundations: Time and Place

Before we look at complex verbs, let's lock down the basics of time and space.

### Time (The Triangle Rule)
Think of an inverted triangle. We go from general (wide) to specific (narrow).
- **IN (Wide):** Centuries, Years, Months, Parts of the day. (*In 2024, In July, In the morning*)
- **ON (Medium):** Days, Dates. (*On Monday, On July 4th, On my birthday*)
- **AT (Narrow):** Specific clock times, Exact moments. (*At 3 PM, At noon, At the moment*)

*Tech Exception:* We say "in the weekend" (UK) or "on the weekend" (US). Both are fine.

### Place (The Dimension Rule)
- **IN (3D Space / Enclosed):** A room, a city, a country. (*In London, In the office, In the meeting room*)
- **ON (2D Surface / Lines):** A desk, a floor, a street. (*On the desk, On the 2nd floor, On Oxford Street*)
- **AT (1D Point / Location):** A specific point on a map. (*At the entrance, At the reception, At the station*)

*Tech Exception:* Virtual spaces use **ON**: *On the internet, On a call, On the server, On Slack, On GitHub*.

## Deep Dive: High-Frequency Workplace Verb Patterns

These are the verbs that cause 90% of preposition errors in corporate English. Memorize the whole chunk.

### Verbs with NO Preposition (Direct Objects)
Do **not** add a preposition after these words:
- **Discuss [topic]:** *We discussed the budget.* (NOT discussed about)
- **Contact [person]:** *Please contact the client.* (NOT contact to)
- **Emphasize [point]:** *I want to emphasize this rule.* (NOT emphasize on)
- **Access [data]:** *I can't access the server.* (NOT access to the server - note: 'access' as a noun DOES take 'to', e.g., "I have access to the server").
- **Answer [person/thing]:** *Answer the email.* (NOT answer to)

### Verbs with fixed prepositions
- **Depend ON:** *It depends on the weather.*
- **Focus ON:** *We need to focus on performance.*
- **Apologize FOR (a thing) TO (a person):** *I apologize for the delay to the client.*
- **Complain ABOUT:** *They complained about the bug.*
- **Agree WITH (a person) ON (a topic):** *I agree with you on this issue.*
- **Respond TO:** *I will respond to the email.*
- **Provide [someone] WITH [something]:** *We provided them with the data.*

## Deep Dive: Noun + Preposition Patterns

Nouns also lock onto specific prepositions. Using the wrong one immediately sounds unnatural.

- **An increase/decrease IN:** *We saw an increase in traffic.* (NOT of traffic)
- **A reason FOR:** *What is the reason for the delay?*
- **An advantage/disadvantage OF:** *The advantage of this approach is speed.*
- **A solution TO:** *We need a solution to this problem.* (NOT for this problem)
- **Damage TO:** *The bug caused damage to the database.*

## Deep Dive: Adjective + Preposition Patterns

When describing feelings or states, adjectives have fixed partners.

- **Responsible FOR:** *I am responsible for deployment.*
- **Interested IN:** *She is interested in AI.*
- **Capable OF:** *The system is capable of handling the load.*
- **Similar TO:** *This feature is similar to the old one.*
- **Aware OF:** *Are you aware of the new policy?*

## Common mistakes and how to fix them

| Wrong (Direct Translation) | Right (English Pattern) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *It depends of the budget.* | *It depends on the budget.* | Depend always takes 'on'. |
| *I will explain you the code.* | *I will explain the code to you.* | Explain cannot take a direct person object. It must be "explain [thing] to [person]". |
| *We are working in this project.* | *We are working on this project.* | Work 'on' a task/project. Work 'in' a department/city. |
| *I am looking forward to see you.* | *I am looking forward to seeing you.* | The 'to' here is a preposition, so it must be followed by a noun or an -ing verb (gerund). |
| *Let's discuss about the plan.* | *Let's discuss the plan.* | Discuss takes a direct object. |

## Practice drills

1. **The 'No Preposition' Drill:** Create a sentence for each: *discuss, contact, access, answer*.
2. **The 'In/On/At' Calendar Test:** Say out loud: *The meeting is ___ Monday, ___ 3 PM, ___ October.*
3. **The 'To' Trap:** Fill in the blank: *I need to explain the new architecture ___ the client.*
4. **Pattern Matching:** Complete these common workplace phrases:
   - *I am responsible ___ the backend.*
   - *We saw an increase ___ sales.*
   - *We need a solution ___ this bug.*

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] use IN, ON, and AT correctly for time and place, including virtual spaces (ON a call).
- [ ] avoid adding prepositions to *discuss, contact, answer, access*.
- [ ] use the correct preposition with key adjectives (*responsible for, aware of, capable of*).
- [ ] correctly use the structure *explain [something] to [someone]*.
- [ ] use the gerund (-ing) after *look forward to*.
