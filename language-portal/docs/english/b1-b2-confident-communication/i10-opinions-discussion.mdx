# Opinions and Discussion

**Level:** B1-B2 · **Goal:** share opinions politely and keep a discussion going beyond one sentence.

Opinion English lets you say **what** you think, **why**, and **how you respond** to someone else — respectfully. The structure matters more than fancy vocabulary: an opinion without a reason sounds weak; a disagreement without a softener sounds rude.

## The pattern

> **Opinion phrase → reason → example → question back (or soft contrast).**
>
> *I think public transport is the better option (opinion) because it's cheaper and less stressful (reason). For example, my commute by train costs half what I paid for parking (example). What about you — do you drive? (question back)*

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Give an opinion | *I think… · In my opinion… · Personally, I feel… · From my point of view…* |
| Strong opinion | *I'm convinced that… · I strongly believe…* |
| Agree | *I agree. · Exactly. · That's a good point. · I couldn't agree more.* |
| Partly agree | *I agree up to a point, but… · That's true, but on the other hand…* |
| Disagree politely | *I see what you mean, but… · I'm not sure I agree. · I see it a bit differently.* |
| Pros and cons | *The main advantage is… · One drawback is… · On the plus side… · On the downside…* |
| Suggest | *How about…? · Why don't we…? · We could… · What if we…?* |
| Defend | *Let me explain why I think so. · The reason I say that is…* |

## Model sentences

- *I think public transport is convenient because it's cheaper than driving.*
- *In my opinion, morning practice is better because I can focus more.*
- *I agree with you, but I also think we need more time.*
- *I'd rather take the train than fly — it's less stressful.*
- *What do you think about this option?*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *According to me, this is good.* | *In my opinion, this is good.* | *according to* is for other people's views / sources. |
| *I am agree.* | *I agree.* | *agree* is a verb. |
| *I like because cheap.* | *I like it because it's cheap.* | Full clause after *because*. |
| *You are wrong.* | *I see it a bit differently.* | Disagree with the idea, not the person. |
| *I prefer to cook than eat out.* | *I prefer cooking to eating out.* / *I'd rather cook than eat out.* | *prefer X **to** Y*; *would rather X than Y*. |
| *Why we don't go tomorrow?* | *Why don't we go tomorrow?* | Question word order. |

## Practice drills

1. **Four-part opinion:** give your view on remote work / city vs countryside / cash vs card with opinion → reason → example → question.
2. **Agree, partly agree, disagree:** respond three ways to *"Everyone should learn to code."*
3. **Pros and cons:** two of each for a decision you're facing.
4. **Negotiate:** agree on a weekend plan with a partner who wants something different.

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] give an opinion with a reason and an example
- [ ] agree, partly agree, and disagree politely
- [ ] compare options and explain a preference
- [ ] suggest, negotiate and reach a compromise
- [ ] defend a view without sounding aggressive
