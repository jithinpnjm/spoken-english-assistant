# Storytelling and Fluency

**Level:** B1-B2 · **Goal:** tell a short story — about yesterday, a trip, a family event, or a work incident — in connected sentences, with fewer fillers and pauses.

A story is the best fluency exercise there is: it forces you to connect sentences, control past tenses, and keep going. A clear story has **time and place, what happened, what you did, the result, and how you felt or what you learned**.

## The pattern

> **Set the scene → what happened → what you did → result → feeling or lesson.**

| Part | Tense | Language |
|---|---|---|
| Scene / background | past continuous, *was/were* | *It was a Friday evening. We were driving back from Munich.* |
| Main events | past simple | *Suddenly, the car started making a strange noise.* |
| Earlier events | past perfect | *We had checked the tyres that morning.* |
| Result | past simple | *In the end, we waited two hours for a tow truck.* |
| Feeling / lesson | past simple / present | *I was exhausted. Now I always check the oil before long trips.* |

### Sequence markers

*At first… · Then… · After that… · Suddenly… · Meanwhile… · Eventually… · In the end… · Finally…*

Avoid *and then… and then… and then…*.

### Fluency habits

- **Speak in chunks** of 3-7 words with small pauses between them — not word by word: *Last weekend / we drove to the Alps / to visit some friends.*
- **Replace fillers** (*um, uh, like, basically, actually, you know*) with a silent pause or a useful gap-filler: *Let me think… · The thing is… · What happened was…*
- **Self-correct and continue:** *We went on Sunday — sorry, on Saturday — and…*

## Model story

> *Yesterday I went to the supermarket because we needed some groceries. At first, I couldn't find the gluten-free bread, so I asked a staff member for help. She told me it was in the frozen section — I'd never looked there! It took more time than I expected, but I was happy to finish the shopping before the rain started. Next time, I'll check the store map first.*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Yesterday I go supermarket and buy things.* | *Yesterday I went to the supermarket and bought some things.* | Finished time → past forms. |
| *Then after I ask help.* | *Then I asked for help.* | *ask **for** help*; one sequence word is enough. |
| *I was happy because finished.* | *I was happy because I'd finished it.* | Clause after *because*. |
| *When I was reaching, the train left.* | *When I arrived, the train had already left.* | Earlier event → past perfect. |
| *Actually basically I mean it was like good.* | *It was useful because it helped me understand the problem.* | Replace fillers with content. |

## Practice drills

1. **Five-part story:** tell a story about a small problem using the five parts above.
2. **Filler count:** record 60 seconds, count your fillers, then retell with half as many.
3. **Chunk marking:** write your story and mark pauses with `/`. Read it aloud with those pauses.
4. **Retell faster, not longer:** tell the same story in 60 seconds, then in 40, keeping all the key events.

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] tell a one-minute story with a clear beginning, middle and end
- [ ] use past continuous for background and past simple for events
- [ ] use varied sequence markers instead of *and then*
- [ ] reduce fillers and pause between chunks
- [ ] tell a two-minute personal experience with a lesson learned

## Homework

Record the same two-minute story on three different days. Keep the facts; improve the connectors, tenses and pauses each time.
