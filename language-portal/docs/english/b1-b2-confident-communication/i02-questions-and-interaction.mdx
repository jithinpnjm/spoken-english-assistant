# Questions and Interaction

**Level:** B1-B2 · **Goal:** ask grammatically correct questions and keep a conversation moving with follow-ups, clarifications and polite interruptions.

A conversation is not a series of answers. Good speakers **ask**: to get information, to clarify, to show interest, and to check understanding. Question word order is also one of the most frequent errors at B1 — *What you are doing?* marks your English immediately.

## The pattern

> **(Question word) + auxiliary + subject + main verb + rest?**

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Yes/no | auxiliary + subject + verb | ***Did** you **check** the logs?* |
| Wh- | wh- + auxiliary + subject + verb | *When **does** the meeting **start**?* |
| Subject question | who/what + verb (no *do*) | ***Who** called you?* · ***What** happened?* |
| Indirect | polite opener + **statement order** | *Could you tell me **where the station is**?* |
| Tag | statement + opposite tag | *You're coming, **aren't you**? It isn't ready, **is it**?* |

No auxiliary in the statement? Use **do / does / did**: *He works here → Does he work here?*

## Model sentences

- *Could you explain the requirement again?*
- *What time does the meeting start?*
- *Did you check the latest logs?*
- *How long will this task take?*
- *Do you know who's responsible for the release?*
- *So you mean we should deploy tomorrow, not today — is that right?*

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *What you are doing?* | *What are you doing?* | Auxiliary before the subject. |
| *You checked the logs?* | *Did you check the logs?* | Use *did* for past questions (a rising statement sounds surprised, not neutral). |
| *When meeting start?* | *When does the meeting start?* | *does* for present simple with it/he/she. |
| *Who did call you?* | *Who called you?* | *who* is the subject → no *did*. |
| *Can you tell me where is the station?* | *Can you tell me where the station is?* | Indirect questions use statement order. |
| *You are coming, no?* | *You're coming, aren't you?* | Use a proper question tag. |
| *Would you mind to send it?* | *Would you mind sending it?* | *mind* + -ing. |

## Practice drills

1. **Flip it:** turn statements into questions — *He works remotely. / They finished it. / It will take two days.*
2. **Indirect upgrade:** *Where is the office?* → *Could you tell me where the office is?* Do five.
3. **Follow-up chain:** answer a question, add one detail, and ask one question back.
4. **Clarify:** respond to vague instructions (*"Just fix it soon"*) with two clarifying questions.

## Checkpoint: I can…

- [ ] form yes/no, wh-, and subject questions correctly
- [ ] ask follow-up and clarifying questions naturally
- [ ] use indirect questions and *Would you mind + -ing* for politeness
- [ ] interrupt, confirm understanding, and repair a misunderstanding politely
