# Professional Emails

Emails are often your first impression. A good work email is **short, structured, and clear about what you need** — the same *context → point → reason → next action* shape from [Professional Register](../b2-c1-professional-fluency/a02-professional-register.mdx).

## Structure

1. **Subject line** that says what the email is for.
2. **Greeting.**
3. **One line of context** — why you're writing.
4. **The point** — facts, decision, or request.
5. **The action** — who does what, by when.
6. **Close and sign-off.**

> **Subject:** Action required: approve Q3 budget by Thursday
>
> Hi Anna,
>
> Following our call on Monday, I've attached the updated Q3 budget.
>
> The total is €12,000 below the first draft because we moved the conference to Q4.
>
> Could you approve it by Thursday so we can place the orders before the price increase?
>
> Thanks,
> Alex

## Formal vs informal

| Formal | Neutral / informal |
|---|---|
| *Dear Ms Weber,* | *Hi Anna,* |
| *I hope this email finds you well.* | *Hope you're well.* |
| *I am writing to enquire about…* | *I wanted to ask about…* |
| *Please find attached…* | *I've attached…* |
| *I would be grateful if you could…* | *Could you…?* |
| *I look forward to hearing from you.* | *Speak soon. / Let me know what you think.* |
| *Kind regards, / Yours sincerely,* | *Best, / Thanks,* |

*Yours sincerely* when you used the person's name; *Yours faithfully* (UK) after *Dear Sir or Madam*. In most modern work email, *Kind regards* or *Best regards* is safe.

## Openings

- *Thank you for your email.*
- *Following up on our conversation yesterday, …*
- *As discussed in today's meeting, …*
- *I'm reaching out because…* (first contact)
- *I was referred to you by…*

## Useful phrases by purpose

| Purpose | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Reason for writing | *I'm writing to confirm / request / inform you that…* |
| Attachment | *Please find the report attached. / I've attached the report.* |
| Request | *Could you please review this and send your feedback by Friday?* |
| Late reply | *Apologies for the delayed response.* |
| Acknowledge | *Thanks for sending this over.* |
| Clarify | *Just to clarify, the deadline is the 15th, not the 20th.* |
| Chase | *I just wanted to follow up on my email from Monday.* |
| Bad news | *Unfortunately, we won't be able to… Instead, we can…* |
| Close | *Please let me know if you have any questions.* |

## Subject lines that work

- *Action required: review contract by Thursday*
- *FYI: updated remote-working policy*
- *Request: budget approval for Q3 campaign*
- *Meeting: quarterly review — 14 March, 2 p.m.*
- *Following up: design proposal*

## Mistakes to avoid

| Avoid | Instead |
|---|---|
| Empty subject line | Always state the purpose |
| *Dear Sir/Madam* when you know the name | *Dear Ms Weber* / *Hi Anna* |
| *Please revert / Kindly do the needful* | *Please reply / Could you update the record?* |
| *As per my last email…* | *Just following up on my email from Monday…* |
| *ASAP* | *by 3 p.m. today / by Friday* |
| Three paragraphs before the request | Put the request in the first 3-4 lines |
| Mentioning an attachment you forgot | Attach first, then write |
