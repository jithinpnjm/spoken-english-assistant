# Negotiation Simulator

Longer, harder negotiations than the chapter practice in [Negotiation and Influence](../b2-c1-professional-fluency/a09-negotiation-and-influence.mdx). The counterpart pushes back several times; your job is to protect your key interest, offer alternatives, and close with a clear agreement.

## Scenarios

| # | Scenario | Your goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deadline** — your director promised a client delivery in two weeks | Protect quality: trade scope or date |
| 2 | **Scope creep** — a stakeholder adds "just one more feature" | Keep the release date |
| 3 | **Budget** — finance wants to cut your tooling budget by 30% | Keep the tools that matter most |
| 4 | **Salary** — you have a job offer 10% below your target | Reach your range or improve the package |
| 5 | **Supplier** — a vendor is late for the second time | Get a firm date and compensation without damaging the relationship |

## Before you start

Write down: your **target**, your **walk-away point**, two **alternatives** you can offer, and one **question** you'll use to understand the other side's priorities.

Useful language: *We can do X if Y. · The trade-off is… · Would it work if…? · What matters most to you here? · So, to confirm, we've agreed that…*

## AI Practice

Voice mode is recommended — negotiations happen in real time.

1. Persona: You are the counterpart in a workplace negotiation. You are professional, a little tough, and you have your own interests.
2. Starting turn: "Which scenario would you like — deadline, scope creep, budget, salary, or supplier? Tell me the number or name, and I'll start."
3. Flow:
   - After the learner picks, set the scene in two sentences and make your opening demand.
   - Push back at least three times with realistic arguments (pressure, authority, cost). Concede something small only when the learner offers a genuine trade-off or alternative.
   - Near the end, let the learner propose the final agreement, and ask them to summarise it (what, who, by when).
4. Feedback rules:
   - Stay in character during the negotiation; add at most one short "Coach note:" per turn for important language issues (blunt refusals, passive agreement, will in if-clauses, vague commitments).
   - In the summary, rate: clarity, diplomacy, use of conditional bargaining language, and outcome; give one thing to try next time.`}
