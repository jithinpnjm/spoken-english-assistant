# How the English Course Works

This track is a **spoken-English** course. The goal is not to pass a grammar test, but to say what you mean clearly, correctly, and naturally — in daily life, at work, and in interviews.

## Course map

| Course | Level | Who it is for | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Beginner Foundations** | A1-A2 | You still build most sentences word by word | Sounds, *be*, sentence order, present/past/future basics, everyday functions |
| **2. Confident Communication** | B1-B2 | You can talk, but mistakes and hesitation slow you down | Tense control, questions, longer sentences, articles, prepositions, modals, workplace English, roleplay |
| **3. Professional Fluency** | B2-C1 | You work in English and want precision, register, and influence | Advanced grammar, professional register, technical communication, meetings, presentations, interviews, negotiation |

Each chapter is one **module**. Inside each module the lessons are ordered: each lesson builds on the one before it, and the last lesson is a **checkpoint** that mixes everything.

:::tip Where should I start?
- If *"Yesterday I go to office"* sounds fine to you, start at **1.1**.
- If you can tell a story but mix tenses and drop articles, start at **2.1 Tense Control**.
- If you work in English every day, start at **3.2 Professional Register** and use **2.4** (articles) and **2.5** (prepositions) as repair modules.
:::

## The lesson method

Every lesson — on the page and with the AI coach — follows the same teaching order:

1. **Intro** — what the pattern means and when you need it.
2. **Model** — example sentences and one *wrong vs right* pair.
3. **Guided practice** — you produce one sentence with a frame or word bank.
4. **Correction** — what was good, the corrected sentence, why it changed, a more natural version, one micro rule.
5. **Rewrite / repeat** — you produce the corrected sentence yourself before moving on.
6. **Free practice** — a short conversation or roleplay on the same topic.
7. **Summary** — rule recap, two correct examples, one mistake to watch, small homework.

Two rules matter more than anything else:

- **Teach before testing.** You never have to produce a pattern you have not seen modelled.
- **No correction without a rewrite.** Reading a correction does almost nothing. Saying or writing the corrected sentence yourself is what changes your habits.

## Using the AI practice coach

Most chapters end with an **AI Practice** block. The page teaches the lesson; the coach (Sky) runs the practice:

- **Start text practice** — type your answers. Every mistake gets a correction card (corrected sentence, more natural version, rule) and you rewrite it before continuing.
- **Start voice practice** — speak with the coach through your microphone. The coach corrects you out loud and asks you to repeat the corrected sentence. Use headphones to avoid echo; the microphone is muted while the coach is speaking.
- **Finish & get summary** — ends the block with a recap and a homework task built from *your* mistakes.

The **?** button in the corner opens a tutor you can ask about anything on the current page.

:::note Privacy
Your messages and voice audio are sent to Google's Gemini API to generate the coach's replies. Nothing is stored by this site after you leave the page.
:::

## How to track your own progress

The site does not keep an account for you, so keep a simple **mistake log** (a notebook or a note on your phone):

| Date | What I said | Correct version | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 May | I have visited Paris last year. | I visited Paris last year. | Finished time (*last year*) → past simple |

Review it with the spacing described in [2.12 Intermediate Review](./b1-b2-confident-communication/i12-intermediate-review.mdx): a mistake you made many times comes back tomorrow; a mistake you have fixed three times in a row comes back in two weeks.
