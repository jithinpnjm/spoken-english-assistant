# Speaking Confidence: Vocabulary and Meanings

Learn each expression with its meaning and a sentence you could genuinely say. Choose a few useful items,
say the examples aloud, then make the examples true for you. You do not need to memorise every item.

## A1–A2: everyday conversation

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Natural spoken example |
|---|---|---|
| **in my free time** | during the time when I am not working or studying | *In my free time, I like going for walks.* |
| **spend time** | use time doing something or being with someone | *I spend a lot of time with my family.* |
| **enjoy + -ing** | like doing an activity | *I enjoy cooking at the weekend.* |
| **be interested in** | want to learn about or know more about something | *I'm interested in photography.* |
| **usually** | on most days or in most situations | *I usually take the bus to work.* |
| **prefer A to B** | like A more than B | *I prefer tea to coffee.* |
| **a bit / a little** | a small amount; often softens a statement | *I'm a little tired today.* |
| **Could you say that again?** | a polite request to repeat something | *Sorry, could you say that again?* |
| **Let me think** | a phrase that gives you a moment before you answer | *Let me think. I'd choose the blue one.* |
| **What is … like?** | asks for a description of a person, place, or experience | *What's your new job like?* |

### Build a short answer

> *I usually **spend time** outdoors at the weekend. I **enjoy** walking because it helps me relax. **What about you?***

## B1–B2: keep an exchange moving

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Natural spoken example |
|---|---|---|
| **a follow-up question** | a question asked after an answer to get more detail | *That's interesting. Can I ask a follow-up question?* |
| **clarify** | make an idea or instruction easier to understand | *Could you clarify what you mean by “urgent”?* |
| **elaborate** | add more information or explain an idea further | *Could you elaborate on the last point?* |
| **relate to** | understand or share a similar experience | *I can relate to that—I changed jobs last year too.* |
| **run into a problem** | unexpectedly experience a problem | *We ran into a problem with the booking.* |
| **sort something out** | find a way to solve or organize something | *I'll check the details and sort it out this afternoon.* |
| **manage to** | succeed in doing something, often when it was difficult | *We managed to finish before the deadline.* |
| **turn out** | develop or be discovered in a particular way | *The meeting turned out better than I expected.* |
| **meanwhile** | during the same period that something else happens | *I'll check the figures. Meanwhile, could you review the slides?* |
| **in the end** | finally, after everything has happened or been considered | *We compared both options and, in the end, chose the simpler one.* |
| **What I mean is …** | introduces a clearer or corrected explanation | *What I mean is, we need one more day to test it.* |
| **as far as I know** | a way to limit a statement to the information you have | *As far as I know, the office closes at five.* |

### Notice the difference

- **At first** describes the beginning: *At first, I found the new system confusing.*
- **In the end** describes the final result: *In the end, I learned how to use it.*
- **Eventually** means after some time or difficulty: *The support team eventually fixed the issue.*

## B2–C1: express ideas with precision

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Natural spoken example |
|---|---|---|
| **weigh up** | consider the advantages and disadvantages before deciding | *We need to weigh up the cost against the time we could save.* |
| **trade-off** | a situation where gaining one benefit means giving up another | *The trade-off is faster delivery but less time for testing.* |
| **tentative** | not final; open to change | *This is a tentative plan—we'll confirm it after the review.* |
| **on balance** | after considering the different points, overall | *On balance, I think the second option is more reliable.* |
| **acknowledge** | show that you recognize a fact, concern, or contribution | *I want to acknowledge the extra work the team put in.* |
| **address a concern** | respond to a worry or issue directly | *Before we decide, let's address the concern about support costs.* |
| **feasible** | possible and practical to do | *The timeline is ambitious, but it may be feasible with another engineer.* |
| **compelling** | convincing and likely to attract attention or support | *She made a compelling case for changing the process.* |
| **a caveat** | a condition or limitation to keep in mind | *I agree with the proposal, with one caveat: we need to test it first.* |
| **rephrase** | express the same idea in different words | *Let me rephrase that: the launch is delayed, but the product is still on track.* |
| **to be specific** | used before giving exact details | *To be specific, we need approval by Thursday afternoon.* |
| **from my perspective** | introduces how something looks from your point of view | *From my perspective, the main risk is the short testing window.* |

### Use with care

These expressions suit discussion and professional contexts. Choose plain language when it is clearer:

- *We should **weigh up** the options* → consider both options before deciding.
- *There is a **trade-off*** → we gain one thing but give up another.
- *The plan is **tentative*** → the plan may change.
- *The proposal is **feasible*** → the proposal is practical and possible.

## Make the vocabulary active

1. Choose three expressions that fit your life or work.
2. Say each example aloud, then replace its details with your own.
3. Use one expression in a short answer and one in a follow-up question.
4. Revisit the expressions tomorrow and later in the week; try to recall the meaning before rereading it.
5. Notice tone: expressions such as **weigh up** and **on balance** fit a discussion; a simpler phrase may be better in a quick, casual exchange.
