# Workplace Phrases

A phrase bank to support [Workplace English](../b1-b2-confident-communication/i07-workplace-english.mdx), [Meeting Skills](../b2-c1-professional-fluency/a04-meeting-and-discussion-skills.mdx) and [Presentations](../b2-c1-professional-fluency/a07-presentations-and-storytelling.mdx). Pick five a week, say them aloud, and use two in real conversations.

## Core workplace vocabulary and meanings

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Natural example |
|---|---|---|
| **attend a meeting** | be present at a planned work discussion | *I have to attend a meeting at ten.* |
| **meet a deadline** | finish something by the agreed time | *We need another reviewer to meet the deadline.* |
| **follow up** | contact someone again to check progress or get a response | *I'll follow up with the supplier tomorrow.* |
| **chase a reply** | politely ask again because someone has not replied | *Could you chase a reply from the client?* |
| **sign off on** | formally approve something | *The director needs to sign off on the budget.* |
| **delegate** | give a task or responsibility to another person | *She delegated the research to two team members.* |
| **agenda** | a list of topics for a meeting | *The agenda has three items today.* |
| **action item** | a task someone agrees to complete after a meeting | *My action item is to send the revised plan.* |
| **minutes** | the written record of what was discussed and agreed in a meeting | *I'll share the meeting minutes this afternoon.* |
| **run over** | continue longer than the scheduled time | *The presentation may run over by five minutes.* |
| **wrap up** | finish a meeting, task, or discussion | *Let's wrap up with the main decisions.* |
| **be tied up** | be busy and unavailable | *I'm tied up until lunch, but I can call you after.* |
| **push back a meeting** | move a meeting to a later time | *Could we push the meeting back to 3 p.m.?* |
| **bring a meeting forward** | move a meeting to an earlier time | *They brought the review forward by a day.* |
| **run into an issue** | unexpectedly experience a problem | *We ran into an issue during testing.* |
| **on the same page** | sharing the same understanding or plan | *Before we finish, let's check we're on the same page.* |

## Office basics

**Roles:** CEO, CFO, CTO, line manager (your direct boss), team lead, intern, contractor / freelancer, HR, PA (personal assistant).
**Departments:** Finance, Sales, Marketing, IT, Legal / Compliance, Operations, Customer Support, R&D.

**Daily tasks:**
- *to attend a meeting · to meet a deadline · to chase someone* (follow up when they haven't replied)
- *to cc someone · to sign off on something* (approve) · *to delegate*
- *to be on annual leave* (UK) / *on vacation* (US) · *to work from home (WFH)* · *to submit a report*

## Scheduling

- *I'd like to set up a call to discuss the timeline.*
- *Are you free on Thursday afternoon? Would 2 p.m. work for you?*
- *I'll send you a calendar invite.*
- *Could we push the meeting back by 30 minutes?* (later) / *bring it forward* (earlier)
- *I'm afraid I have a conflict then — could we reschedule?*
- *I'm tied up this afternoon.* (busy)

## Running and following up a meeting

| Purpose | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Open | *Let's get started. Today's agenda covers three points.* |
| Hand over | *I'll hand over to Sara to present the figures.* |
| Park a topic | *Can we come back to that later? · Let's take this offline.* |
| Time | *We're running over — let's wrap up.* |
| Summarise | *To summarise what we've agreed…* |
| Follow-up | *I'll circulate the minutes by end of day. Your action item is… by Friday.* |

*Minutes* here means the written notes of a meeting.

## Calls and video meetings

- *Speaking.* (answering: *Could I speak to James?* — *Speaking.*)
- *Could you hold for a moment?*
- *I'm afraid she's not available right now. Can I take a message?*
- *You're breaking up a little — could you repeat that?*
- *Sorry, you're on mute.*
- *Let me just pull that up.* (find a document)
- *Can everyone see my screen?*

## Asking for help and giving instructions

- *Would you be able to help me with this?*
- *I'm a bit stuck on this — do you have a moment?*
- *Could you talk me through the process?*
- *Who's the best person to speak to about…?*
- *No rush — whenever you have a spare moment.*
- *First, open the spreadsheet… Once you've done that, send it to me for review.*
- *If you run into any issues, just let me know.*

## Feedback

| Positive | Constructive |
|---|---|
| *This is really well done.* | *One thing to think about for next time…* |
| *You handled that brilliantly.* | *It would be even stronger if you…* |
| *I really liked how you structured this.* | *I noticed… — have you considered…?* |

## Formal vocabulary for reports

| Everyday | Formal |
|---|---|
| get | obtain, receive, acquire |
| show | demonstrate, indicate, illustrate |
| use | utilise, employ, implement |
| need | require |
| look at | examine, analyse, assess, evaluate |
| find out | determine, establish |
| do | conduct, carry out, undertake |
| help | facilitate, support, enable |

**Report transitions:** *Furthermore · In addition · However · Nevertheless · As a result · Consequently · Therefore · Overall · On balance · It is recommended that…*

**Report structure:** Executive summary → Introduction → Findings → Analysis → Recommendations → Conclusion.

## 40 professional phrases — with tone notes

| Phrase | Use / note |
|---|---|
| *Just to keep you in the loop…* | sharing an update |
| *I wanted to touch base about…* | informal check-in |
| *Looping in Priya for visibility.* | adding someone to an email thread |
| *Please disregard my previous email.* | correcting a mistake |
| *Just following up on my email from Monday.* | polite chase — prefer this to *As per my previous email*, which often reads as irritated |
| *Could you get back to me by Friday?* | asking for a reply — outside South Asia, avoid *Please revert*, which normally means "go back to a previous state" |
| *Let's take this offline.* | discuss separately |
| *Can we circle back to that?* | return to it later |
| *Let's put a pin in that.* | pause an idea |
| *To build on what Sara said…* | adding to a point |
| *I want to flag something before we move on.* | raising a concern |
| *Can you walk us through that?* | ask for step-by-step explanation |
| *Who's the point of contact for this?* | who is responsible |
| *Let's make sure we're aligned on this.* | check agreement |
| *We're on the same page.* | shared understanding |
| *What's the turnaround time?* | how long it will take |
| *It's on my radar.* | I'm aware and tracking it |
| *We're pushing the deadline back to Friday.* | delaying |
| *This is a priority — can we fast-track it?* | speeding up |
| *Let's get the ball rolling.* | start |
| *We'll need to run this by legal first.* | get approval |

| *That's above my pay grade.* | beyond my authority (informal, with a smile) |
| *Don't hesitate to reach out.* | invitation to contact you |
| *I'll keep you posted.* | promise of updates |
| *Let's not reinvent the wheel.* | reuse what exists |
| *I want to give credit where it's due.* | acknowledging a colleague |
| *Let's take stock of where we are.* | review progress |

| *That's a fair point.* | acknowledging |
